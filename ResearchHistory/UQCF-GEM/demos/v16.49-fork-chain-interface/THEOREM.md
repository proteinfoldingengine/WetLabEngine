# Binary-parent composition of compact chain repairs

Status: proposed proof accepted by preliminary independent analytical review; implementation and full closure validation pending.

## Objects, feasibility and canonical endpoint
A rooted tree consists of a binary root R and two internal-chain child subtrees A,B. Each chain may have arbitrary finite positive internal length and degrees>=2. Supports are nonempty and nested, R has ordered nonempty palette P fixed, and a move changes one nonroot label incidence. Fix an attained profile, with root hitting r in {1,2} and chain profiles qA,qB. Let mA,mB be their positive minimal requirements from the reviewed v16.48 compact-palette induction. That induction supplies normalizations with each chain root's support fixed, total chain L1 excursion<=1, and canonical descendants using exactly the first m labels of its ordered palette.

If r=1, the two child-root supports overlap and |P|>=max(mA,mB). The common canonical endpoint places A on the first mA labels and B on the first mB labels, with each chain filled by its own compact canonical pattern. If r=2, the two child supports are disjoint and |P|>=mA+mB. The canonical endpoint gives A the first mA labels and B the next mB labels, each canonically filled. Necessity follows from each child lower bound plus disjointness for r2. These constructions establish sufficiency and use exactly the first M labels among R's descendants, M=max(mA,mB) or mA+mB respectively. This is an interface composition lemma for chain children; no unrestricted branching induction is asserted here.

## Overlap case: retain a shared anchor
Expand A's root support to P and then B's to P, adding one label incidence at a time. The initial shared label is never deleted, so root hitting remains1. Child hitting numbers depend on their descendant supports, which do not change during root expansion. Normalize A on fixed palette P using v16.48, leaving B fixed, then normalize B on fixed P leaving A fixed. Throughout these phases R sees its two child supports P, so its hitting is exactly1. Only the currently active chain can deviate, by total L1 at most1.

After A is canonical, its descendants use exactly the first mA labels of P. Delete all other labels from A's root support; no descendant uses them, so these are admitted primitive moves and every child profile coordinate stays exact. Do the corresponding contraction for B after its normalization. Both contracted supports retain P's first label because mA,mB>=1. R remains exact. One may contract A before normalizing B: B's fixed root support P still contains the retained common anchor. The endpoint depends only on P and target profile, not initial state. This also handles singleton palettes.

## Disjoint case: normalize first, then exchange complete roles
Write the disjoint initial child-root palettes as SA,SB. Normalize A on fixed ordered SA while B is fixed, and normalize B on fixed SB while A is fixed. The parent remains exactly2. At each endpoint contract the normalized root to its first mA or mB labels, deleting only labels absent from all descendants. Disjointness is preserved, and the whole profile is exact at the end of each operation.

Each surviving label is a role carrying its complete canonical incidence pattern in exactly one child subtree. To replace role x by label y absent from that subtree, add y at every vertex carrying x in parent-before-child order, then delete x there in child-before-parent order. Parent support P contains y. Each support remains nonempty and nested. At every internal vertex of that child, during additions y's child-incidence set is contained in x's; during deletions remaining x-incidences are contained in y's. A hitting set using the redundant smaller incidence label can replace it by the containing label. Thus EVERY inner hitting number is preserved at every primitive move, independently of chain depth.

To exchange role x in A and role y in B, first replace x by y throughout A and then y by x throughout B. Initially y is absent from A; after the first replacement x is absent from A and from B, so both operations meet the absence condition in their destination subtrees. Inner profiles remain exact. The only possible new overlap is at R's two child supports, whose hitting number is always1 or2. It is2 again at the completed exchange. A same-child role swap is three cross-child exchanges through any fixed opposite-child role, (i,p),(j,p),(i,p). This restores the pivot and swaps i,j; both child palettes are nonempty, so a pivot always exists. Every constituent exchange finishes with the whole profile exact. Replacing a role by a globally unused label likewise preserves inner profiles and disjointness exactly.

Order the roles as A's canonical label roles then B's. Assign successive labels of P: if the desired label is unused, replace; otherwise exchange with its current role using the operations above. Earlier completed roles are restored after a temporary pivot use. This terminates at the common canonical endpoint. During transport all child profiles are exact and only R can carry a unit deficit. No two repairs have to remain active simultaneously.

## Consequence and limitations
Sequential chain repair and subsequent role transport have disjoint deviation phases, so the total L1 deviation over R and ALL internal vertices is<=1. Reverse one normalization and concatenate with another to connect any two states in a fixed attained profile. Distinct exact components exclude zero cost for each independent integer-valued scalar objective, yielding B1=Binf=Bs=1; within one exact component all are0. Separately defined LEX is(1,1,1) between distinct components.

The proof covers one binary parent with two chain children of arbitrary finite lengths and degrees>=2, including the frozen11-vertex fork where both children have two internal vertices. Eleven vertices is minimal only under that two-deep-children condition, not for internally branching trees in general. Arbitrary internal branching, higher-arity interfaces and the universal retained-tree conjecture remain unproved here. Finite exhaustive and directed audits test implementation, not general quantifiers. No physical interpretation is claimed.
