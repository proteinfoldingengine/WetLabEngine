What is constructive here?

For a specified next binary instrument K_b=P_bU, its input effects F_b=K_b†K_b define E(ρ)=Σ F_bρF_b. This is a CPTP conditional expectation and K_bF_c=δ_bcK_b, so every subnormalized branch output is identical after inserting E. Probabilities, normalized conditional states, and correlations with an external reference are therefore preserved. The next event type and its actual outcome are still supplied; this identity does not select either.

The 80 complete histories are recomputed with the matching event-specific preprocessing immediately before each declared event. No additional actual event or physical clock is assigned to that mathematical factorization.

Why open alternatives change the answer

Preserving an entire event instrument means preserving the still-unmeasured quantum state too, not just the outcome's probability. Each sharp event requires its full within-sector observable algebra to survive. At the seven two-ready contexts, the two such algebras generate the full current-sector algebra. A common CPTP preprocessing preserving both instruments must therefore be identity on that sector.

The two events commute, so this is not a claim of incompatible measurements. Executing both events as a fixed batch has a weaker endpoint requirement: pre-pinching both sectors preserves the final batch output. It does not preserve the intermediate output of either event as an open alternative.

A concrete probability-only false positive

The two witness inputs have the same certain B1 outcome but opposite relative phases across A1 sectors. A1-specific pinching deletes that phase. B1's probability is unchanged, yet its remaining state moves by trace distance 0.5; the two originally distinguishable B1 outputs become identical. Looking only at probabilities would miss the damage.

The finite proof uses standard quantum-instrument and multiplicative-domain mathematics. It applies to all input states on the declared current sector and the unchanged branch-output contract. It is not a new general quantum law or a ban on other encodings, restricted inputs, approximate outputs, or physically selected subsequent events.
