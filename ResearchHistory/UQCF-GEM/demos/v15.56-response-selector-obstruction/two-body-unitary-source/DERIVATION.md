# Exact support and polar-rank prediction
For Pauli P, H=P/2 and hidden h, commuting labels give Y=0. Anticommuting labels give Y=-iPh with HS norm one. A weight-two P anticommutes with a weight-three h iff exactly one of its two supported labels differs. The matched site can be chosen two ways, the differing label two ways, and the untouched label three ways: 12. Multiplication cancels the matched site, leaving two independent six-dimensional pair spaces on the other two edges. No retained response reaches the source-support edge or the disjoint singleton.

Each six-dimensional pair space is a matrix space with one row or column absent. Its projection onto polar skew is surjective for every orthogonal O: an annihilating nonzero skew K would require OK (or KO^T) to have support in a single excluded row or column, impossible since a nonzero three-dimensional skew matrix has rank two. Thus each responding edge has skew rank three, and the disjoint hidden-label blocks give rank six. Aggregating all sources spans all weight-two Pauli directions: leakage rank 27, Q/E rank nine. Weight-one commutators preserve full support and vanish under every pair restriction.

For C=OP, Q=O^T dC-dC^T O and PW+WP=Q. P positive makes the skew Sylvester map invertible, preserving rank. Coordinates (q01-q10,q02-q20,q12-q21)/sqrt(2) preserve skew HS norm.

U_s=cos(s/2)I-i sin(s/2)P is exactly unitary. The centered hidden-tangent channel derivative is sinc(s)Y; its analytic truncation error is approximately s^2/6. Positivity and mixture affinity follow from the channel form; numerical tests check implementation and the polar domain. Baseline source activity is a measured validity condition, not an algebraic theorem. Standard quantum correlation redistribution is the mechanism under test.
