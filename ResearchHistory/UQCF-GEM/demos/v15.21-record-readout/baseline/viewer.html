What is the actual channel?

At the parity-erasing boundary, Φ(ρ)=ρ/2 + I/32 − Tr(Γρ)Γ/32, where Γ=Y⊗Y⊗Y⊗Y and ρ is normalized. The channel is a convex mixture of unitary Pauli operations: identity with weight 1/2, and each of the 128 Pauli operations anticommuting with Γ with weight 1/256. Those weights define a channel; no actual outcome is sampled in this model.

Every traceless predictor observable has half its original expectation after this map. Twice the measured ensemble mean recovers the target value. For a general observable A in the predictor space, subtract its known identity offset before dividing by λ. This is not quantum-state recovery, and a doubled observable need not remain a valid probability effect.

Why the readout cost is not optional

The exact direct-readout theorem is broader than this displayed family: a CPTP channel that fixes every predictor observable must fix all four-qubit Pauli operators, including the supposedly removable parity. More generally, erasing parity on the same carrier forces at least one visible Pauli observable to have worst-input expectation distortion at least 1/2. The displayed channel attains that minimax bound. This does not mean every actual record probability changes by 50%.

Gain-two readout raises the single-shot variance bound from 1 to 4 for a zero-mean Pauli signal. At other means the variance ratio differs. The interface does not hide that sampling variance or claim a free quantum compression.

Does the existing motion still work?

All six frozen motions commute with Γ. The new channel therefore commutes with them too. The same motion operators act on the positive noisy states, and the signed ensemble readout reproduces the selected target predictions. No replacement motion or physical clock has been introduced.

What has not been derived?

The channel is an explicit diagnostic construction, not an adopted physical RAS selector or first-collapse law. It is not idempotent: applying it again attenuates the signal again. It stays on a 16-dimensional, four-qubit carrier, so the result is not a smaller quantum world. A common CPTP encoder/decoder preserving the whole predictor exactly would have to recover the entire original state.
