Why no single-copy physical instrument can reproduce even the first event

The two original states F/8 and (I−F)/8 require first-event probabilities 1 and 0. Their encoded states have trace distance 1/2 and both are full rank. No measurement on one encoded copy can separate them perfectly. The tight worst-case binary probability error is 1/4.

Every effect that would reproduce the exact first-event probability has the form B = 2F − I/2 + cΓ. Its eigenvalues are 1/2 ± √(1+c²). The negative eigenvalue cannot be removed by choosing c. Signed ensemble readout is therefore not a POVM effect or a physical branch-selection rule.

What still works constructively

All sixteen complete-history probabilities are recovered from ensemble values by the stated signed formula. A formal signed update also gives the correct encoded branch outputs on the reachable encoded image. It is not positive on arbitrary density matrices and is not accepted as a quantum instrument.

Multiple independently prepared encoded copies improve discrimination of the fixed two-state witness, but no finite number permits perfect discrimination of two full-rank states. Extra copies are an explicit resource, not cloning or a claim about physical actualities.

Scope and provenance

All four-qubit dynamics, record prerequisites, instruments and the positive predictor are reused unchanged. The simulation enumerates candidate records; it does not sample or make any outcome actual. These comparisons use standard measurement and state-discrimination mathematics, not a new collapse law. No entropy functional or clock selects an event. Pillar 3 remains open.

Finite-sample signed estimates may fall outside [0,1]. They are not silently clipped, treated as outcome weights, or sent into a physical controller. Exact ensemble identities are not zero-error finite-shot guarantees.
