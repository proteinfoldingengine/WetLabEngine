# Protection can transfer through one shared root

Analytical checkpoint: independently ACCEPTED. Implementation: not started. Universal higher-floor connectivity: OPEN. No new numbered version is assigned, and v16.54's bounded certification is unchanged.

The precise advance is a reusable handover theorem: two actual protective root subfamilies at exact-q endpoints may share one labelled root slot and still provide a primitive repair path with transversal in {q-1,q}. Disjoint protection is sufficient but no longer necessary for this construction. The proof works with arbitrary positive floors and uses only the existing palette and slots.

The protection is not a conserved scalar. It passes from an unchanged old guard, through a proved union-bridge certificate, to a completed destination guard. Exact endpoint redundancy is restored at completion. This permits repeated use along an eligible exact-endpoint chain without assuming redundancy survives every intermediate edit.

A two-shared-slot example explains the remaining difficulty: different small hitting sets can lose their old and new protection at different slots. The proposed union bridge then falls below the permitted lower bound even though another ordering repairs the same endpoints safely. Thus the failed construction identifies a scheduling and compatibility problem; it does not show that one-unit repair fails.
