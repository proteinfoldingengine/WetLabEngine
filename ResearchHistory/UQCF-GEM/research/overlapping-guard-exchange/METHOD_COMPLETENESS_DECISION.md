# Decision: close the bounded-buffer completeness conjecture

The previous next-obligation statement asked whether every exact-q endpoint pair admits some buffer and some whole-root edit order meeting every adaptive safe-pool inequality. UNBOUNDED_REPAIR_PARTICIPATION.md answers that question negatively by a symbolic family, not by numerical search.

The failure is stronger than a bad order or an unfortunate guard choice. For every m>=6 the same exact endpoints require at least m-3 roots to be away from both of their labelled endpoint supports at one state of EVERY lower-guard path. The one-buffer method permits at most two such roots at once and is therefore excluded for every choice. Any fixed b-buffer variant with only one additional active root is likewise excluded when m>b+4.

Yet these endpoints have a fully explicit one-unit repair through global label transpositions, each decomposed into single-incidence moves. The conclusion is a precise obstruction to universal completeness of bounded-participation repair architectures, not to the one-unit law.

Retain the accepted handover, sequential, fixed-buffer and adaptive-buffer theorems as valid conditional interfaces. Their conditions were never claimed universal. Mark only the later UNIVERSAL EXISTENCE CONJECTURE for the adaptive one-buffer method as disproved, and record that no fixed buffer count repairs that completeness claim within the stated architecture.

Future work should allow multiple roots to remain temporarily modified and seek a protection certificate that can span those distributed changes. The accepted label-role packets, contraction guards, star exchanges and maximum-layer theorem are relevant existing mechanisms. A new general theorem must prove eligibility, renewal and termination for such distributed participation rather than assume that bounded excursion localizes the repair to a bounded set of roots.

This does not authorize a numerical campaign or implementation changes. The certified v16.54 bounded implementation and its evidence remain unchanged. Universal higher-floor connectivity remains OPEN. No v16.55 is assigned and no independent scalar barrier optimum is inferred from participation counts.
