For an admitted state, a nonroot vertex v and a label x:

- Addition of x at v is a syntactically valid admitted primitive exactly when x is absent from S_v and present in S_parent(v). Enlarging S_v preserves all child inclusions and nonemptiness; only the parent inclusion can newly fail.

Shrinking S_v preserves its inclusion in the parent; nonemptiness and its child inclusions are the remaining conditions. At a leaf the last condition is vacuous.

## Small explicit source and record

Use existing labels P={a,b,c,x}, with an arbitrary fixed total order. The fixed topology has global root o with three ordered children (u,B,C); u has two ordered leaf children (r,w). There are six vertices, of arity 3, 2 or 0, inside the inherited tree category. In this candidate u is called the source support and r the proposed record support; those roles and addresses are supplied mathematical choices, not derived physical identities.

No record difference has been placed in r at initialization. The label x and its global-root incidence already exist in both states; no label, vertex or palette extension is performed.

Apply the same named request, addition of x at r. The syntax at r is identical in both sources.

## Retention and back-constraint

Suppose the admitted case-1 toggle has occurred. Every later admitted state with x still in S_r must retain x in S_u, because S_r remains contained in S_u. Thus while this record incidence is fixed, deleting x at its parent is forbidden by the inherited grammar.

This is a constraint on jointly admitted changes, not a derived stabilizing force or persistent-memory law. The record can be erased: delete x at the leaf r, leaving {a}. That primitive is admitted, leaves every h target exact, and returns the fixture to its case-1 initial record. Thereafter x can be deleted at u, since neither r nor w contains it and a remains. This second primitive is also admitted and reaches the case-0 fixture, again with all targets exact.

Consequently restoring this parent's source support to {a} cannot leave x stored in this descendant record. At any endpoint with S_u={a}, nesting already forces x absent from r. A historical record elsewhere would require a further justified transfer; it is not supplied by this two-level fixture. This does not exclude other record coordinates, other trees or other interactions. It also does not require all useful records to survive such a restoration; it states this candidate's retention scope.
