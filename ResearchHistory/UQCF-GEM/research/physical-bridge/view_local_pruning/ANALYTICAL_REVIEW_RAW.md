## Theorem 1: independent reconstruction and adversarial checks

The necessity proof is valid. Fix any i, any such Y, and any nonroot global leaf l belonging to Y. Assign T minus {l} to every other index. Removing a global leaf preserves root membership and prefix closure. Since another index exists, those views contain every vertex other than l, and the chosen Y contains l, so the completed family is a valid cover. Every other contracted output still omits l. Therefore F_i(Y) must retain l. Crucially, independent processing means changing the other views cannot change F_i(Y). Prefix closure then forces the entire ancestor chain of l. Root membership forces the remaining member of the stated hull.

This establishes necessity for every possible single-view input, not merely the inputs occurring in the illustrative fixture. The sufficiency proof is also valid. Every global leaf is contained in at least one input view; that view retains the leaf and its ancestors. Every vertex of a finite rooted tree has a descendant global leaf. Thus every vertex remains in the union. Finite T is an essential stated assumption; an infinite leafless branch would require a different argument and is not covered.

Degenerate cases are handled correctly. If T consists only of its root, the allowed input and output views are both {o}; no nonroot-leaf witness is needed.
