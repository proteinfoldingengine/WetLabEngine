## Statement

For each level-j block F_j(w) with owner w, the unit history is the nested chain F_0(w) ⊆ ... ⊆ F_j(w). Its shell span is CFR §3 eq. (3).
**Theorem 152.1.** Core_j = ⊕_w span{e_{F_l(w)} : l ∈ L_j(w)}, where L_j(w) = {c_j(w), ..., j} is an up-set of levels (a truncated unit history),
c_j(w) = max over g ∈ G of the cutoff c_g, and c_g is defined as follows. Let gC be the image chain landing in F_j(w), and k the lowest level at which gC agrees with C(w).
Set c_g = k−1 if F_k(w) = F_{k−1}(w) ⊔ gF_{k−1}, and c_g = k otherwise (c_g = 0 if gC = C(w)).
Equivalently, conjecture H holds: Core_j = alg(B_j, {e_{F_l(t)} : the whole G-orbit of e_{F_l(t)} lies in Ã_j}).
**Proof.**
(⊇) The algebra generated by a G-orbit lying in Ã_j is G-invariant and contained in Ã_j, hence in Core_j. So is B_j.
(⊆) On F_j(w), 𝒮_j is w's shell partition, and g𝒮_j is the shell partition of a second nested chain gC of π-blocks with the same top. Two chains of π-blocks that agree at level k agree above it,
because the π_{l+1} block containing a π_l block is unique. Let k be the lowest level of agreement. Shells above k coincide.
On F_k: if F_k \ (F_{k−1} ∪ gF_{k−1}) ≠ ∅, the two level-k shells overlap and each contains the other chain's whole lower part, so the join puts all of F_k in one block. Otherwise the join is {F_{k−1}, gF_{k−1}}.
Either way the join with 𝒮_j|F_j(w) is the partition generated by {e_{F_l(w)} : l ∈ L_g}, with L_g = {c_g, ..., j}.
For one fixed chain, intersecting the algebras span{e_{F_l} : l ∈ L} intersects the level sets L. Hence Core_j|F_j(w) = span{e_{F_l(w)} : l ≥ max_g c_g}.
Each retained e_{F_l(w)} lies in the G-invariant Core_j ⊆ Ã_j, so its orbit lies in Ã_j. ∎
