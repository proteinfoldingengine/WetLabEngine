## Exact fixed-effect criterion

The signed outcome-probability difference ranges over exactly
$$
\left\{\operatorname{Tr}[(F\otimes I_B)W\Delta\rho W^\dagger]\right\}_W
=\frac15[a_- - b_+,\ a_+ - b_-].
$$

Indeed the value is $(\langle e_u|A\otimes I_B|e_u\rangle-
\langle e_0|B\otimes I_B|e_0\rangle)/5$. Each term lies in its spectral interval, including entangled complement states. For the converse choose arbitrary unit vectors $a,b\in K_A$ and orthogonal flags $0,1\in K_B$. Set $e_u=a\otimes1$ and $e_0=b\otimes0$. This preserves all of RD-0, including the retained coherences, while allowing the two block expectations to vary independently. A Hermitian operator realizes every value between its extreme eigenvalues on unit vectors, by superposing extremal eigenvectors. Thus the entire displayed interval is attained with a two-dimensional hidden factor.

Consequently one fixed effect distinguishes the pair in every admitted realization exactly when
$$a_->b_+\quad\text{or}\quad b_->a_+.$$
Equivalently its two calibration intervals are strictly disjoint. The guaranteed absolute response is
$$\frac15\max(0,a_- - b_+,b_- - a_+).$$
Touching intervals admit a blind realization. If they overlap, select the same expectation from both intervals and use the orthogonal hidden flags above. These are necessary and sufficient conditions in the stated full realization class. They do not identify the weakest physical axiom across other classes.

This permits imperfections and within-sector variation. Exact equality to $\pi_p$ or constant response inside each retained sector is unnecessary for this criterion. Physically establishing the bounds for all admitted hidden states could itself require additional input. Calibration of two selected states alone cannot establish them.

## Why fixing the entire complement dimension can change the answer

The dimension-two exception is already proved in CORRECTION_T249 section 1. For the whole complement $K_A=\mathbb C^2$ and no $K_B$, orthogonality gives
$$5s_F(W)=\operatorname{Tr}A-\langle e_0|(A+B)|e_0\rangle.$$
This prior identity is used here as a control. [Existing correction](../receiver_delivery_boundary/CORRECTION_T249.md#1-signed-statistic-and-absolute-contrast-repairs-correction_t241-3-rd-7ii)

Take $A=\mathrm{diag}(7/10,3/10)$ and $B=\mathrm{diag}(1/5,3/5)$. Then $A+B=(9/10)I$ and every such minimal-complement realization gives $s_F=1/50$. Let
$$F=\pi_p\otimes A+(I-\pi_p)\otimes B.$$
The same effect, extended by the identity on a hidden qubit, can be blind. Set
$$h=\sqrt{3/8}|0\rangle+\sqrt{5/8}|1\rangle,\quad
e_0=h\otimes|0\rangle,\quad e_u=h\otimes|1\rangle.$$
Both block expectations equal $9/20$. The full channel remains exactly $C$. This is an enlarged realization class, explicitly compared with the smaller one. The available effect and source preparations stay fixed under the identity embedding. Its signed interval in the larger class is $[-3/50,1/10]$.

For a positive control take $A=\mathrm{diag}(7/10,9/10)$ and $B=\mathrm{diag}(1/10,3/10)$ in the same construction of $F$. Every hidden realization gives a response in $[2/25,4/25]$. Both effects are individually blind on the canonical pre-event pair for any common complement blank, because their $u$ and $v$ blocks agree. No full-interface initial-blindness assertion follows.
