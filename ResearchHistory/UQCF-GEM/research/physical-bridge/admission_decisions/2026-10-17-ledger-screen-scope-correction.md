## 1. Global preservation does not prohibit local acquisition

The information identity in section 3 compares the final full ledger with the JOINT input consisting of the initial ledger and incoming entries. It does not compare the receiver's initial ledger alone with its final ledger.

A diagnostic counterexample is a fair source bit S, an initially empty receiver ledger L0, and an incoming entry E=S. Append produces L1=[S]. The receiver goes from zero source information to one bit, while the joint-input information is one bit both before and after append. The two statements are compatible.

This is a logical counterexample to the no-acquisition inference, NOT a proposed native copying mechanism: E=S, arrival, append execution and receiver access are supplied in this example. None is adopted as physics. A mechanism can transfer an existing distinction without creating new information globally. Thus injectivity, alphabet naturality or data processing alone do not exclude it from the milestone.

The exact full-ledger identity also requires a recoverable split into initial ledger and entries, for example a fixed known initial length and event count, or explicit delimiters. Variable unmarked decompositions must not silently be treated as bijections.

## 2. The hash/certifier equivalence is too strong

Section 5 identifies a receiver output (c,r_T), not the entire recoverable ledger. With shared fixed side parameters and a source-independent readout, equal entry laws imply equal output laws. Equivalently, distinct output laws require distinct entry laws. The converse needs an additional distinction-preservation condition on that readout.

A noninjective readout can map two different entries to the same output. A finite hash and a certifier bit are not automatically injective on the allowed input domain; simply writing “up to collisions” does not prove the stated if-and-only-if for the actual preparations. This note does not assert or exhibit a collision in the five archived V997 histories. It withdraws the unsupported general equivalence.

Likewise, information equality for a deterministic readout need not require global injectivity: it depends on whether that readout preserves the source-relevant information under the specified distribution.

## 3. Refusal is not automatically an altered accessible record

Refusing an append may leave the retained ledger unchanged. A refusal flag, visible comparison with an accepted branch, or another accessible consequence needs its own derived retention/access argument. Noninjectivity of a candidate map is neither a proof of a record write nor a necessary condition for receiver acquisition.

The quantum paragraph also must not turn “noncovariant” into “supplied” by definition. A future symmetry-breaking reference or interaction remains eligible if it is derived from inherited structure. Any central-blindness argument must specify the outcome-label action and invariance assumptions of the instrument; the off-diagonal block formula alone is not a replacement for that full contract. No quantum theorem is recertified here.
