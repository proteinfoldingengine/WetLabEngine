## 5. Every proper receiver-pruning output separates

After (4), keep a and b fixed, keep the root and carrier fixed, and permit only native r-deletions at nonroot vertices. All such deletions use the inherited bare grammar; there is no profile budget, target guard or extra deletion-site restriction. An identity or prematurely stopped history remains possible.

**Theorem 1.** The reachable receiver views are exactly the prefix-closed Z satisfying

    {o,w} union A_s  subseteq Z subseteq T.                    (5)

Consequently every proper output Z != T has a nonempty missing set

    empty != M=T minus Z subseteq B_s.                        (6)

For the same source-independent role r, the two sets of proper output views are disjoint.

Proof. Every vertex of A_s and w has r as its sole remaining label by (4). Nonemptiness forbids removing r there. Root membership is fixed and native nesting makes Z prefix closed. This proves (5) for every reachable state, and (6) for any proper state.

Conversely, let Z be prefix closed and satisfy (5). Delete r at every vertex of T minus Z, in deepest-first order. These vertices all lie in B_s and retain b. Nonemptiness therefore holds. Prefix closure of Z and deepest-first deletion ensure that no retained r-child lies below an r-incidence being deleted; the parent relation is preserved. Every deletion is a legal native atomic move. This realizes Z and proves exact reachability. Since U and V are disjoint, no nonempty missing set can be contained in both B_0=V and B_1=U. QED.

**Corollary 1 (first primitive).** Immediately after (4), the exact set of admitted r-deletions is

    {-r(l) : l is a global leaf in B_s}.                       (7)

Every cone has at least one such leaf. All internal vertices still have r-children, so deleting r there would violate nesting. Leaves in A_s and w are solely covered by r, so deleting r there would violate nonemptiness. Leaves in B_s retain b and admit deletion. This proves (7), including existence of at least one proper option in each source case.

Thus no selected location or completed relaxation is needed: ANY actual first r-pruning event in this declared phase produces a source-specific own view. The proof establishes what a proper event must do, not that an event occurs or has positive probability.

**Corollary 2 (persistent partial record).** Along subsequent r-pruning with the external views fixed, M grows by inclusion and remains in B_s. Once nonempty it remains nonempty, so the same source answer stays valid at every later prefix. No end-of-pruning acknowledgment is needed for this post-source-phase decoder. This is retention under the declared pruning grammar, not retention under arbitrary native edits or carrier changes.

The identity view Z=T is common to both cases and decodes as UNKNOWN. Zero response must not be assigned a source value.

## 8. Controls and limits on the protocol

**Before source withdrawal, the same deletion can occur in both cases.** In the six-vertex fixture, -r(v) is legal initially in both because a covers v. It would misleadingly produce the source-0 record even in source 1. Thus the post-source phase is load-bearing. The theorem gives no all-phase decoder, source-completion flag, timing signal or way for r to verify that the source phase has finished.

**The first source phase writes no own-view record.** Y_r remains T across every a-deletion. Its changed native response possibilities are a dependence available to the joint relation, not an already observed bit. A proper r-response is needed to turn it into a differing own view.

**Post-response additions can erase the record.** In either case, reverse a single admitted r-deletion by the corresponding legal r-addition; the receiver view returns to T. Thus persistence is specific to receiver pruning. The first-step source classification is stronger than the persistence claim in one respect: from Y_r=T, a proper first receiver-only atomic edit cannot be an addition because no r-incidence is absent. Its direction is forced to be a deletion by saturation of the INITIAL r-view. This does not prevent later additions, and does not select the actor r or force any event.

**Other labels acting can give no receiver response.** After (4), b may legally be deleted at a leaf of B_s while r remains there. That is a proper native event with Y_r unchanged. Consequently "every proper event" would be false without the explicitly receiver-owned response class.

**Source stability matters.** Restoring a or changing b alters exclusive coverage and can invalidate the decoder. Fixing the external views during the r-response is an inherited receiver-experiment contract, not a new conservation law.

**No hidden legality oracle.** Formula (7) is proved from the joint native state; the receiver need not be given it as input. There is no implementation that queries hidden supports, resolves a request, emits a rejection flag or records a chosen command. Any such implementation would supply the still-open action/access connection.
