## Question and candidate being tested

The completed record-conversion checkpoint supplies a source-erasing five-edit path, but its positive historical record is sound only under a supplied ordered exclusive protocol. Can that history restriction instead follow from adding finitely many ordinary nested-support control vertices or labels, while keeping the inherited native primitive relation and total excursion budget one?

This tests a specific proposed controller architecture, not all physical control. The proposal is to encode the controller in the same ordinary support state and obtain protection from admissibility alone. There is no supplied scheduler, forbidden-edge list, immutable nonroot anchor, irreversible update, absorbing halt rule or compulsory first-hit stopping rule. If one of those is used, its justification becomes an additional premise outside this candidate.

The answer below is a corollary of the inherited v16.53 normalization theorem. The normalization mathematics is not new and is not rerun here. The consequence for this proposed controller is the object being analyzed. It does not rediscover the all-NOOP example or depend on zero progress.

## Precisely declared class

Fix a finite ordered rooted tree T whose internal arities belong to {2,3,4}, a finite nonempty ordered palette P and one target profile q on all internal vertices. The global root support equals P. Every support is nonempty; child support is contained in parent support. One primitive adds or deletes one incidence at one nonroot vertex while preserving those conditions.

Write h_v(S) for the inherited minimum hitting number of the immediate child supports and

    D_q(S)=sum over internal v of |h_v(S)-q_v|.

Let E_q be the exact states D_q=0 and G_1 the graph of admitted states D_q<=1 with every inherited legal primitive whose endpoints belong to that set. Only feasible profiles with nonempty E_q are relevant. This does not assume that every arbitrary integer target profile is feasible.

All proposed control registers, auxiliary vertices and labels must already be part of this fixed finite carrier before execution. They are ordinary supports with the same primitive rules. They may increase its size, but must retain arities 2,3,4. Both source preparations S_0,S_1 are exact states for this SAME T,P,q. They may have identical initial control/readout supports, as in the conversion example; the argument does not need that stronger restriction.

A history is any finite path of G_1, including a zero-length path. A readout R is any function of the complete terminal support state, or of a retained projection of that state. A declaration of success/halting encoded in support coordinates is also part of that state. No first-hit stopping restriction is automatically inferred from such a coordinate. The claim concerns universal historical soundness over this declared path class, not probabilities or which path physically happens.

## Controller-boundary corollary

**Claim.** Reach_G1(S_0)=Reach_G1(S_1). Consequently no terminal-state readout can be both universally sound for original source bit one and nonvacuously positive for a history from S_1 under this candidate architecture.

Proof. If E is reached from S_1 by a finite G_1 path, prepend the displayed S_0-to-S_1 path. Every state still satisfies D_q<=1, including the exact splice at S_1. Therefore E is reached from S_0. Exchange the roles of the two exact preparations to obtain the reverse inclusion. This applies even if E itself is only band-admitted rather than exact.

Suppose R(E)=1 for at least one endpoint reached from S_1. The same E is reachable from S_0, so the assertion that every positive endpoint came from original bit one is false. The endpoint is identical in ALL support coordinates, including every proposed control register, source coordinate and retained projection. A coarser readout cannot distinguish the two histories either. Universal soundness therefore forces the positive-output set to miss this entire common reachable component. QED.

The result quantifies over each finite controller augmentation satisfying the stated class. Increasing the number of ordinary control labels or allowed-arity vertices cannot evade it: apply the same inherited normalization lemma to the enlarged fixed tree and palette, provided both prepared states remain exact for a common profile. It gives an existence path, not a physically selected path or a practical efficient attack. No bound on length, probability or duration is asserted.

## Limits and the next obligation

Excluded from this corollary: zero-excursion-only histories; extra frozen nonroot supports; additional support floors/cover guards not in the inherited category; palettes/trees/profiles that differ between the two preparations; arity>=5; unbounded auxiliary carriers; first-hit or externally stopped histories; specified schedules, restricted transition kernels or irreversible/pruning-only laws. Exclusion means this argument does not settle those cases, not that any of them supplies an earned physical mechanism.

The concrete re-entry requirement is a justified restriction on actual updates or on physically preserved control structure, plus retained-output access, whose origin is independent of the desired answer. A state variable called phase, a designated terminal flag, or an ordinary auxiliary support does not by itself restrict the unchanged primitive graph. Any proposed extra mechanism must identify which of the prepended transitions it excludes and why, without using an observer-side hidden-state read or assuming every legal request commits.
