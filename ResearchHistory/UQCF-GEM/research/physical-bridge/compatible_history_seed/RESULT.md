## 2. A profile restriction for all sector-vertex covers

**Lemma.** For any covering family of these sector-vertex views and any internal x,

\[
h_x=\begin{cases}
1,&\text{if }x\le p_i\text{ for some }i,\\
d_x,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases} \tag{2}
\]

Proof. If x<=p_i, every child of x intersects p_i, so that single view covers all children. If no such p_i exists, a p_i that intersects x must, by laminarity, be strictly below x. It is contained in exactly one child of x, so its view meets exactly that one child. Other records miss x altogether. Covering all d_x children then needs at least d_x indices. The assumed cover supplies an index for each child, and those indices are distinct, giving equality. QED.

This is a restriction on minimal-central-sector vertices of this common tree. It is not a restriction on arbitrary prefix-closed views or on every possible central record convention.

It also exposes an event-size distinction. On the three-leaf star T={I,p_1,p_2,p_3}, the labelled record family (I,p_1,p_2,p_3) has root h=1. Refining only the first record to p_1 preserves the cover and changes h to 3. This family is a valid sector cover, but its mutually exclusive p_j are not asserted to be jointly actual records of one history. The supplied record refinement is not one atomic incidence deletion: deleting p_2 and then p_3 from the first view gives intermediate h=2, and that intermediate view is not V(p) for any vertex of this star. The earlier native factorization remains valid on its larger view domain. It does not make a whole record refinement a unit-cost event or enforce a unit band.

## 3. One compatible history forces a universal retained view

Now impose the candidate's additional common-history restriction. The mathematical condition needed is

\[
\prod_{i\in P}p_i\ne0. \tag{3}
\]

The product is unambiguous because all records commute. Records from one inherited compatible refinement history satisfy (3): they are nested nonzero projections. Conversely, within this laminar vertex family, (3) makes every pair intersect and hence every pair comparable. Thus there is a largest record p_max, and the join of the family is p_max.

Combining (1) and (3) forces p_max=I. Consequently some indexed view is V(I)=T and, by (2),

\[
h_x=1\quad\text{at every internal }x. \tag{4}
\]

This is a consequence of the stated representation and compatibility conditions. A nonzero common product is not itself a law of actual occurrence, a joint probability prescription, or an assertion that a native receiver possesses all records. The earlier bridge allowed labelled sector covers without (3); that broader contract is not silently narrowed here.

For the seed in section 1, q_x=1. Along any history preserving the fixed tree, the sector-vertex representation, common compatibility and the cover,

\[
D_q=\sum_{x\text{ internal}}|h_x-q_x|=0. \tag{5}
\]

With refinement-only indexed records, once an index ceases to be I it cannot return to I. On any finite prefix, an index that is I at its endpoint was I throughout that prefix. An atomic factorization retaining the target views can keep that universal view untouched, so there is also a factorization with h=1 throughout. No hidden intermediate positive deficit is needed to connect these endpoints.

The proposed D_q<=1 guard is therefore vacuous on this restricted history class. Equation (5) is not a new physical conservation law and does not derive why a unit constraint should govern any event. To obtain a nonzero deficit from this route, at least one premise must change: the common-history condition, the sector-vertex representation, the fixed carrier/cover, or the record/update architecture. Such a change needs its own native justification; it cannot be introduced merely to recover the desired signal.

## 4. Controls on the scope

**Individual record changes survive.** Reuse the two-sector carrier control from the earlier bridge: T={I,p,s}, p+s=I, and indexed records (I,I) change to (p,I). This is compatible with one history and preserves T. The first view loses s; at the root C_I({first index}) changes from 1 to 0, while h_I stays 1 because the second view remains universal. Thus (4) is blindness of the hitting profile, not constancy of the whole Boolean cover field, all memberships, a quantum state or every possible output. A native record interaction targeting such a bit remains eligible. Here the actual refinement and receiver access are still supplied or unresolved.

**Dropping the last I changes the carrier.** Continuing to (p,p) loses s from the union, so the fixed-T cover premise fails. Retaining only the new union is a possible different mathematical description; it cannot silently reuse the fixed-tree q, child slots and guard theorem.

**Positive marginal weights do not give common compatibility.** With rho=I/2 on the two-sector example, both p and s have positive weight, but ps=0. Their separate RCR admissibility does not make them jointly true sector records of the one compatible history. The family (p,s) covers T and has h_I=2; it belongs to the broader labelled-sector class, not the common-history subcase.

**Nonminimal records are outside the vertex restriction.** In a three-dimensional diagonal algebra let e_1,e_2,e_3 be its atoms. The nonminimal sums a=e_1+e_2 and b=e_2+e_3 have ab=e_2!=0 and a join b=I, although neither is I. Extending the compatibility formula to these non-vertex records on T={I,e_1,e_2,e_3} gives views {I,e_1,e_2} and {I,e_2,e_3}, hence h_I=2 rather than 1 or 3. With rho=e_2 both projectors even have weight one. This directly rejects extending either lemma to arbitrary overlapping central sums. It supplies no native process assigning or updating such indexed memories; that wider representation remains a separate eligible route.

Finally, the previous `chain_view_screen` has a different restriction: the views themselves are ancestor chains, giving h_x=d_x. Here the record projectors are nested while V(p) can branch, and the fixed cover yields h_x=1. Neither statement substitutes for the other.
