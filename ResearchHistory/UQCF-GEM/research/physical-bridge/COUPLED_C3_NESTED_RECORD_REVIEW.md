## Analytical checks

### Local addition and deletion

For an admitted state and a fixed nonroot vertex, addition of an absent label preserves nonemptiness and every child inclusion. Its only possible new nesting failure is exclusion from the immediate parent. Thus the stated addition condition is necessary and sufficient.

Deletion requires presence of the label and another label remaining at the vertex. Shrinking the support preserves its inclusion in its parent. Every immediate child must omit the deleted label, and this condition is sufficient: the initial state is admitted, so no deeper descendant can contain that label when its immediate ancestor omits it. At a leaf the child condition is vacuous. The fixed-root restriction is respected.

These are local equivalences for the unrestricted grammar. They are not exact characterizations after adding C3 floors, bands, universally protected hidden completions, or a path-excursion contract. PROBE.md explicitly makes that distinction. The native source separates endpoint exactness and the path budget from primitive admission, so the note does not silently weaken a primitive condition.

### Conditional record, retention and erasure

Nesting makes a positive record a sound certificate of **current** membership of `x` at `u`. A zero record is not a certificate of absence at `u`. The projection's definability does not itself make it accessible to an observer, which the note states explicitly.

Every admitted later state retaining `x` at `r` must retain `x` at `u`. This is an endpoint constraint on the fixed tree, not a force, event-selection rule, or proof that the record persists. The two stated erasures are admitted: first delete `x` at leaf `r`, retaining `a`; then delete `x` at `u`, after both children omit `x`, again retaining `a`.
