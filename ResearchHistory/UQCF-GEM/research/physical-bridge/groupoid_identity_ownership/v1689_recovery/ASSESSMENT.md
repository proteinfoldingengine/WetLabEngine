## Literal event coverage and output typing

The V1689.5 and .10 provenance tables have 40 rows each; .15 has 80 across its two pipelines. Their event-type sets are exactly:

`source_injection`, `dependency_addition`, `repair_event`, `order_boundary_crossing`, `provenance_relabel`.

V1689.10/.15 action tables have the same respective 40/80-row event coverage. None of these emitted tables contains `branch_prune` or `source_removal`. The V1689.9 *protocol* table does list the combined categories `branch_birth/prune` and `source_injection/removal`. A requested event class and an emitted instance are different evidence. This coverage finding does not deny that pruning occurs or prove that no other package implements it.

The output records contain a node label, affected_subspace_id, numerical rho_e and, in .15, event_projector_serialized/A_edge_id. They do not include a receiver's complete initial state, its post-event state, an available observable, or an identification of those matrices with the P1 owner's output. The reports do not supply that identification elsewhere in the inspected text. Calling a serialized affected subspace “owned” would insert the relation being sought.

The .15 manifest reports a compatibility residual near 0.796 and cosine near -0.213; .16 interprets compatibility as not established. These are **historical reported values**, not newly recomputed results. Neither their failure nor the source's rhetoric proves that a particular variational principle is necessary for all native models.
