# UQCF-GEM v7.3 — Pruning to Conditional Expectation

## T1 — Canonical unit-ledger expectation

Let \(\mathcal G\) be a finite discrete recoverable groupoid and let
\(\mathbb C[\mathcal G]\) be its complex groupoid \(*\)-algebra. Define

\[
E_0\!\left(\sum_g c_g g\right)
=
\sum_{u\in\mathrm{Obj}(\mathcal G)} c_{1_u}1_u.
\]

Then \(E_0\) fixes the unit/object algebra, is idempotent and bimodular over that algebra, and

\[
E_0(a^\dagger a)_u
=
\sum_{g:s(g)=u}|c_g|^2\ge 0.
\]

Thus the recoverable history algebra canonically carries a conditional expectation onto the identity ledger.

## T2 — Retained subgroupoid restriction

If a pruning operation literally retains a wide, dagger-closed, composition-closed subgroupoid \(\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal G\), the coefficient restriction

\[
E_{\mathcal H}\left(\sum_g c_g g\right)=\sum_{h\in\mathcal H}c_h h
\]

is the natural conditional-expectation candidate. The supplied finite discrete audit verifies positivity and the bimodule identity on a nontrivial finite-group control.

This is a conditional result: the Retained Bridge's actual pruning must first be proved to have this structural form.

## T3 — Many-to-one merge no-go

For the classical algebra \(\mathbb C^2\), merging two fine states into one retained class does not determine an expectation. For every \(w\in[0,1]\),

\[
E_w(x,y)=\big(wx+(1-w)y\big)(1,1)
\]

is a unital positive idempotent projection onto constants.

Therefore the coarse merge relation alone cannot select a unique conditional expectation. A native state, weighting rule, or symmetry principle is needed.
