## 3. Preserved grade does not preserve the original address

Use the inherited prefix chain X={o,v,d}, with d a child of v, and Y={o,v}. Let a be a positive rational diagnostic amount and J_X=a delta_d. The actual ancestral retraction sends d to v, so the inherited finite-additivity rule gives

    P_XY J_X = a delta_v.

The total source amount is a before and after, while d is absent afterward. Positive rescaling leaves the same conclusion. Therefore conservation of the retained/projective source amount does not certify that its original source address remains in the current carrier.

An original certified record that source s was bound to d remains a statement about that original binding. If retained as history, it is not automatically a statement that d is still a current node. The current localized field instead has its source at r_XY(d). Computing that current address uses the actual retraction, which depends on the supplied retained set Y.

This control does NOT assert that the two complete histories, current provenance roots, current full address domains or full indexed fields are identical. The exact inherited pushforward alone is sufficient to show why grade conservation is not a live-membership certificate.

## 5. A previously proved terminal fact can be maintained

There is a useful elementary corollary of literal contraction. Suppose a nonroot v is correctly known to be a leaf of an actual earlier view Y0, and the current view Y satisfies Y subseteq Y0 by a justified pruning-only history. Then v, if still present, is still terminal in Y: a strict descendant in Y would already have belonged to Y0, contradicting the earlier leaf fact.

Consequently the mathematical named command

    D_v(Y)=Y minus {v}

preserves root membership and prefix closure for every such Y, including the case v is already absent, when it is a NOOP. A fresh complete read of the current view is unnecessary for that type/safety conclusion, provided the earlier terminal certificate and the contraction premise are genuinely justified. Current presence is needed to assert that a nontrivial deletion actually happened, not to establish prefix safety of this command including NOOP.

If, in addition, a strict descendant d of v is known to belong to the SAME CURRENT common carrier T, then this single-view command preserves the union when other views are unchanged. Since v was terminal in Y0, d is absent from Y0 and from Y. Another view must cover d and hence v. If v was present, deleting it leaves v in that other view; if absent, nothing changes. This is the preceding positive-extension argument with an inherited historical upper bound on the own view. It is a conditional corollary, not a new complete classification or a physical actuator.

Two controls are essential. If additions are permitted, Y0={o,v} may become {o,v,d}, and deleting only v violates prefix closure; an old terminal certificate is then insufficient. If d was retained only historically and is absent from the current carrier, the live-witness premise fails and deletion may lose the union. A separately justified fixed-union history preserves such carrier membership, but it must not be assumed merely because the desired certificate needs it.

This proof treats one addressed deletion with other views unchanged. Arbitrary simultaneous commands, C3 floors, protected hitting bands, record persistence and receiver access require their own conditions. It neither mandates the command nor makes it physically executable. Initial acquisition of Y0's terminal fact and an event's access to that certificate remain open.

The closed known-seed/complete-ledger initialization theorem may maintain appropriate facts when its seed and full event channel are supplied. Its report explicitly keeps their origin and completeness assumed. Reapplying its induction is not a new bootstrap or an earned acquisition mechanism. The present source screen does not rerun or recertify that checkpoint.
