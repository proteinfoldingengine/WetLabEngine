## What the predecessor and successor actually establish

The predecessor explicitly distinguishes common ancestry from transformation restriction maps. Its same-fork example permits either diagonal or independent group actions depending on the supplied maps. Once maps are specified, their fiber product is determined; invariant-state admissibility is still a separate assumption.

The successor makes a real advance within that historical conditional setting: the already specified edge density supplies a canonical relation between local symmetries. Its classically correlated two-qubit example has a nontrivial $\mathbb Z_2$ matching quotient. This corrects an overly broad claim that no matching object can be extracted from provenance data.

It does not complete the missing event step. The report's section 5 says that the resulting global group acts on the fiber of compatible states; this does not say that each state is fixed. Section 6's Haar average is an existence argument for a mixed compatible state, explicitly not a physical averaging operation. Section 7's GHZ family shows why imposing pointwise invariance on pure completions can instead discard an otherwise compatible fiber.

Those are the historical report's own arguments and controls, not new proofs or rerun computations in this pass. The conditional Pauli entanglement bound in the predecessor is not promoted into an owner-action law.
