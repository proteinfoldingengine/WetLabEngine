## 0. Result first

1. **I* splits in two, and the first half is not a premise (Proposition 1).** Because an inherited grade is an address-indexed function, its configuration space is a Cartesian product over addresses. The observable algebra of that space is canonically the tensor product of one-address algebras, and its canonical reversible dilation is canonically the tensor product of one-address registers. So "an address is a subsystem whose state is its grade coordinate" (call it I*-a) is earned from the inherited data type. What remains (I*-b) is only "that subsystem counts as the receiver".
2. **The inherited event determines which subsystem receives the distinction (Theorem 2).** Take an initially uninformed receiver: two preparations that agree on every retained address. After a complete-fiber contraction r, the retained outcomes differ exactly on the owner set O = {a retained : the removed fiber sums of a differ}. For a single source difference at a removed atom u, O = {r(u)}: exactly one one-address subsystem becomes informed (every composite containing it is informed too, Theorem 2(b)), and it is the one the inherited fiber map names. No READS table, controller, or chosen readout is involved.
3. **The record follows the lineage and has exactly one holder (Theorem 3).** Along any finite history of complete-fiber contractions, a single source difference at u is held, at every step, by exactly one retained address, the lineage image L_k(u) = r_k o ... o r_1(u), and by no other. It is never copied and never lost while the holder is retained. This is the ancestry ledger read as a record trail.
4. **The gain is a redistribution, not a creation (Proposition 4, corrected in draft 2).** For random product preparations, the receiver goes from total-variation distinguishability 0 to TV(nu_a * p_u, nu_a * q_u), where nu_a is the law of the rest of the owner's fiber. This is at most TV(p_u, q_u), with equality when nu_a is a point mass, and it is strictly positive whenever p_u != q_u (injectivity, Corollary 4.1). For the deterministic source pair it is at least max nu_a (Proposition 4.2, sharp). The retained-plus-removed joint distinguishability never increases.
5. **The v15.13 tensor split is the dilation of the P1 address product (Proposition 5).** In the canonical reversible form, every native move acts on exactly the two registers {u, r(u)} and as the identity on all others. So the receiver locality that v15.13 supplied by hand is, under the dilation, the inherited address product.
6. **What remains (section 7).** I*-b is the only identification step left, and given Theorems 2 and 3 it can be stated as an operational definition rather than a hidden choice: the receiver of an event is the retained subsystem whose state carries the event's distinction. Occurrence stays a declared input; preparability G of the two preparations stays the premise of preparability_branch_split.

## 1. Setting

A prefix-closed carrier is a finite rooted forest X_s (every retained node's parent is retained). A complete-fiber contraction keeps an ancestor-closed subset X_t containing the roots, and sends each removed atom u to its nearest retained ancestor r(u); r(t) = t on X_t (address stability). A grade is x: X_s -> Z_{>=0} (or R_{>=0}); its configuration space is Omega_s = prod over z in X_s of Omega_z with Omega_z = Z_{>=0}. When a finite range is needed, Omega_z = {0, ..., N_z} with N_z at least the largest reachable owner total (a capacity for the dilation only; the commutative statements need no truncation).

A **preparation pair** is two grades x, x' on X_s. The receiver side is **uninformed** if x and x' agree on every retained address of the contraction about to happen: x|_{X_t} = x'|_{X_t}. In the random version, preparations are product distributions p, q on Omega_s with equal marginals on every retained address.

## 2. Proposition 1 (I*-a: address subsystems are canonical)

**Proposition 1.** For finite ranges, let B_z be the algebra of functions on Omega_s that depend only on x_z. Then:
(a) the B_z commute, are independent (a product of minimal projections, one from each B_z, is a nonzero minimal projection of C(Omega_s)), and together generate C(Omega_s); the map f_1 (x) ... (x) f_n -> f_1 ... f_n is a canonical isomorphism from the tensor product of the C(Omega_z) to C(Omega_s);
(b) l^2(Omega_s) = tensor product over z of l^2(Omega_z) canonically, by |x> -> tensor product of |x_z>;
(c) each B_z is central in C(Omega_s) (commutative), so by record_access Theorem 2.1 every B_z-record is freely readable and persists under every later retained operation that fixes B_z.

*Proof.* (a) Minimal projections of C(Omega_s) are point indicators 1_{x}, and 1_{x} = product over z of 1_{x_z}(x_z); the 1_{x_z} span B_z and products of spanning sets span. (b) Same map on basis vectors. (c) Commutativity. QED.

So in the commutative stack the subsystem structure that I* asks for is a consequence of the inherited data type (grade = address-indexed function), not an additional premise. This is the classical, trivial case of the observation that subsystems are induced by the algebra of operationally relevant observables (Zanardi 2001; Zanardi, Lidar and Lloyd 2004); Theorem 2 adds that here the inherited events themselves are 2-local in exactly this factorization.

## 3. Theorem 2 (the event names its receiver)

**Theorem 2.** Let r: X_s -> X_t be a complete-fiber contraction and x, x' a preparation pair, uninformed on X_t. Write Delta_a = sum over removed u with r(u) = a of (x_u - x'_u). Then:
(a) mu_r(x)_a - mu_r(x')_a = Delta_a for every retained a, so the retained outcomes differ exactly on O = {a in X_t : Delta_a != 0};
(b) for any set W of retained addresses, the W-marginals of mu_r(x) and mu_r(x') differ iff W meets O; in particular the informed subsystems are exactly the members of O, each informed through its own coordinate only;
(c) if x and x' differ at a single removed atom u, then O = {r(u)};
(d) if x and x' differ only at retained addresses, the pair is not uninformed; if they differ only at removed atoms whose differences cancel within each fiber, O is empty and the event erases the distinction (inherited loss arrow, ledger Proposition 11.6).

*Proof.* (a) mu_r(x)_a = x_a + sum over removed u in r^{-1}(a) of x_u, and x_a = x'_a. (b) Coordinatewise from (a). (c) Delta_{r(u)} = x_u - x'_u != 0 and Delta_a = 0 otherwise. (d) Direct. QED.

What is new here is the reading, not the algebra: the inherited fiber relation r fixes not only *how* the outcome changes (record_access Theorem 5.1) but *which* subsystem receives the distinction. In GPT's terms (record_consumer_trace section 3), the removed-address-to-owner relation r(u) = a is the source-backed correspondence between the native event and the receiver; together with Proposition 1 it supplies items 1 and 2 of that list (the receiver's own state structure, and an existing record within it tied to the event) without a declared READS key.

## 4. Theorem 3 (the record follows the lineage)

Let r_1, ..., r_K be successive complete-fiber contractions X_0 -> X_1 -> ... -> X_K, and L_k = r_k o ... o r_1 the lineage (ancestry-ledger) map, with L_k(t) = t for t retained through step k.

**Theorem 3.** Let x, x' on X_0 differ at exactly one address u. Then for every k, mu_{L_k}(x) and mu_{L_k}(x') differ at exactly one retained address, L_k(u), by exactly x_u - x'_u.

*Proof.* Pushforwards compose: mu_{r_k} o ... o mu_{r_1} = mu_{L_k}, since fibers of a composite are unions of fibers. The difference vector is (x_u - x'_u) e_u, and mu_{L_k} sends e_u to e_{L_k(u)}. QED.

So a single source distinction has a unique holder at every step, the holder is the lineage image of the source, the distinction is never duplicated (single-owner record, unlike the redundant fragments of quantum Darwinism; Zurek 2009), and it is never weakened in the deterministic case. Address stability r(t) = t is what makes "the same holder across steps" well defined. Under multiple differences the holder set at step k is {a : sum over z with L_k(z) = a of (x_z - x'_z) != 0}, and merging can cancel distinctions; this is the inherited loss arrow, now located at the step where two differing lineages first share an owner.

## 5. Proposition 4 (redistribution, random preparations; corrected in draft 2)

Let p, q be product distributions on Omega_s that agree on every address except a single removed atom u (marginals p_u, q_u), and let a = r(u). Let N_a = sum of x_z over z in r^{-1}(a) with z != u (the prior owner grade and every other atom absorbed into a), with law nu_a. By the product hypothesis nu_a is the same under p and q and independent of x_u.

**Proposition 4.** (a) Before the event, every retained marginal is the same under p and q: the receiver side is blank, TV = 0.
(b) After the event, the owner's laws are nu_a * p_u and nu_a * q_u, and TV(nu_a * p_u, nu_a * q_u) <= TV(p_u, q_u), with equality if nu_a is a point mass (sufficient, not necessary).
(c) The retained joint TV after the event equals the owner's TV in (b), and is at most the joint TV of all addresses before the event, which is TV(p_u, q_u). No distinguishability is created; the receiver's gain is at most what the dropped source held.

*Proof.* (a) by construction. (b) Owner = N_a + x_u with independent summands, so its law is the convolution; convolution with nu_a is a channel (data processing); a point mass is a shift, a bijection. (c) Disjoint fibers of a product input give independent output coordinates; every non-owner output has the same law under p and q, so the joint TV equals the owner's TV; the pushforward is a channel on the full joint, so data processing gives the bound. QED.

[D2] Draft 1 wrote p_a (the owner's own prior) in place of nu_a; that is correct only when no other removed atom is absorbed into a. Counterexample to draft 1's equality clause: see the revision note.

**Corollary 4.1 (injectivity; GPT, D2).** For grades in Z_{>=0} with ordinary (non-wrapping) addition, p_u != q_u implies nu_a * p_u != nu_a * q_u. *Proof.* Let d = p_u - q_u, m the least index with d(m) != 0, n the least index with nu_a(n) > 0. The coefficient of nu_a * d at m + n is d(m) nu_a(n) != 0, since every other term with i + j = m + n has i < m or j < n. QED. There is no uniform lower bound: delta_0 against delta_1 with nu_a uniform on {0, ..., M} gives TV = 1/(M+1).

**Proposition 4.2 (sharp lower bound for the deterministic source pair; new in draft 2).** Let p_u = delta_s and q_u = delta_t with k = |t - s| >= 1 (the Witness A form: the source distinction is definite). Then for every nuisance law nu_a on Z_{>=0} with finite support,
max_n nu_a(n) <= TV(nu_a * delta_s, nu_a * delta_t) <= 1,
and the lower bound is attained by nu_a uniform on {0, ..., M} with k = 1.
*Proof.* TV = (1/2) sum_j |nu(j) - nu(j - k)| (shift invariance of TV). Fix n* with nu(n*) = max nu and restrict the sum to the residue class j = n* (mod k). Along that class the sequence nu(n* + i k), i in Z, is 0 for |i| large and takes the value max nu at i = 0, so its total variation is at least 2 max nu. Hence TV >= max nu. For uniform nu on {0..M} and k = 1 the sum is 2/(M+1). QED.

Reading. Whatever else the owner's fiber absorbs, a definite source distinction leaves the receiver with at least the weight of the most likely nuisance value; the receiver's discrimination degrades only by spreading of the nuisance law, never to zero. This is the ledger point of addendum (b) in exact form: the receiver gains information without global creation, by a move from the removed source into the owner, and the gain is bounded below by a property of the inherited fiber alone.

## 8. Novelty, stated plainly

- Proposition 1: the canonical tensor factorization of functions on a product space is elementary; observable-induced subsystems are known (Zanardi, PRL 87, 077901, 2001; Zanardi, Lidar, Lloyd, PRL 92, 060402, 2004). New for UQCF: it discharges I*-a, which record_access had listed as missing.
- Theorems 2 and 3: elementary linear algebra of pushforwards. New for UQCF: the inherited fiber and lineage maps identify the receiver of a distinction uniquely and track it as a single-owner record trail. Compared with quantum Darwinism, the record here is single-owner (a move, not a copy), so objectivity across many receivers is not addressed and is not claimed.
- Proposition 4: data processing; standard. New only in placing the ledger's "gain without creation" in exact form for random preparations. Corollary 4.1 is the elementary injectivity of convolution on the nonnegative integers (leading-term argument). Proposition 4.2 is an elementary total-variation bound for a shifted law; I know no specific citation and claim no mathematical novelty, only its use here as a fiber-only lower bound on the receiver's gain.
- Proposition 5: Stinespring-style dilation of a classical move; elementary. New for UQCF: the v15.13 receiver split becomes the inherited address product under that dilation.

## 11. G: the native form of the pair, and what it needs (new in draft 3)

Theorem 2 compares two grades on one carrier that differ at a removed atom. This section asks when such a pair is natively preparable, using only the inherited append-only structure (preparability_branch_split section 1: E-pin, E-addr, E-ret, E-anc) and keeping every legitimacy statement as a typed premise.

**Proposition 11.1 (where a native difference can sit).** Let two histories both be append-only extensions of one pinned prefix tree P with grades x_P. Then they agree on every entry of P (E-addr: append-only extension changes no existing address or grade). So a native preparation pair can differ only at appended entries. In particular the same-carrier pair of Theorem 2 cannot be realized by changing the grade of an existing atom of P; its native form puts the difference at an entry appended after P.
*Proof.* E-addr, as stated in preparability_branch_split Lemma 2.1. QED.

**Lemma 11.2 (zero padding).** Let T = P plus one leaf e under l in P, and let U = P plus leaves e under l and e' under l' (l' in P). Write the history as contractions written in addresses of P, as in preparability_branch_split's common operation: at step k a set X_k of P is retained (ancestor-closed, containing the roots), and an appended leaf is retained exactly when its parent is (it goes with its parent's subtree). Extend a grade on T to U by x_{e'} = 0. Then at every step the retained outcome on U, restricted to the retained addresses of T, equals the retained outcome on T, and the extra retained address e' (if retained) has value 0.
*Proof.* T is ancestor-closed in U, so the nearest retained ancestor of a node of T is the same in T and in U; e' contributes 0 to whichever owner receives it. QED.
Scope: the padded leaf is a bookkeeping device for comparing two different trees on one carrier. It does not assert that a zero-grade entry exists natively, which is the zero-grade scope that preparability_branch_split leaves open.

**Theorem 11.3 (one-location occurrence pair).** Compare the prefix history P (nothing appended) with T = P plus one leaf e of grade w > 0 under l. Pad both to T (x_e = w against x_e = 0). Along every history of contractions written on P-addresses, with lineage maps L_k on T:
(a) at every step the two retained outcomes differ at exactly one address, the holder L_k(e), by exactly w (Theorem 3 applied to the single difference w at e);
(b) the holder starts at e itself (L_k(e) = e while l is retained), and when l's subtree is first retracted it moves to the nearest retained ancestor of l; from then on it follows the lineage of l;
(c) a receiver a in P is informed at step k exactly when L_k(e) = a, that is, from the arrival step s_a(l) of preparability_branch_split until a itself is retracted, after which the holder is a's lineage image. Before arrival a is blank (Lemma 2.1 there);
(d) the distinction is never erased and never copied: a single difference cannot cancel at a merge (Theorem 3).
*Proof.* (a) and (d) are Theorem 3 on T with the single difference at e, together with Lemma 11.2 for the padding of P to T. (b) The appended leaf is retained exactly when its parent is, and a removed node's owner is its nearest retained ancestor. (c) Restates (a) and (b) with s_a(l) = min{k : e in F_k(a)}. QED.

**What this asks of G.** Theorem 11.3 needs exactly two legitimate histories: the pinned prefix P itself (legitimate by N1, the inherited definition) and one append at one location. Call the premise
- **N_1(l):** appending an entry of grade w > 0 under l in P is a legitimate carrier history.
This is strictly weaker than preparability_branch_split's location-free N2 and than GPT's two-location premise (both appends legitimate). It is the carrier form of N3 at one specified location. GPT's source audit (TWO_LOCATION_EXTENSION section 1) shows that the V997 certificate-extension lemma earns syntactic continuity for a supplied event and does not supply the event-to-tree action (parent location, grade). So N_1(l) is still a premise, now in its smallest form: one supplied event, interpreted as one append at one location. Nothing about where or whether the event occurs is explained; the occurrence is the declared empirical input, and the theorem says only that if both histories are legitimate, the receiver named by the lineage records which one happened.

**Theorem 11.4 (two-location pair).** Let l != l' in P, T = P plus e under l, T' = P plus e' under l', both of grade w > 0, padded to U (Lemma 11.2). Along every history of contractions written on P-addresses, with lineage maps L_k on U:
(a) the retained difference at step k is w(delta_{L_k(e)} - delta_{L_k(e')}); so either exactly two retained addresses differ, L_k(e) by +w and L_k(e') by -w, or none, and none happens exactly when L_k(e) = L_k(e');
(b) once L_k(e) = L_k(e'), the distinction is erased at every later step (the lineage maps compose: L_{k+1} = r_{k+1} o L_k);
(c) the total retained grade is the same in both histories at every step: the gain at one holder is matched by the loss at the other, so this is pure redistribution;
(d) a receiver a in P is informed at step k exactly when exactly one of e, e' is in its fiber, which is preparability_branch_split Theorem 2.2(2) restated as a present-state fact about named holders.
*Proof.* (a) Linearity of the pushforward and mu_{L_k} e_z = e_{L_k(z)}, as in Theorem 3. (b) Equal images stay equal under composition. (c) Pushforwards preserve the total. (d) e is in F_k(a) iff L_k(e) = a; if both are, L_k(e) = L_k(e') = a and (a) gives no difference. QED.
Needs: both appends legitimate (GPT's two-location premise). Gains over Theorem 11.3: equal totals, so the source distinction is not visible in the global grade sum, and the receivers are a named pair with opposite records. Cost: a second legitimacy premise and a merge step that erases the distinction (the inherited loss arrow, located at the first common lineage image of l and l').

**Corollary 11.5 (random nuisance).** If the grades on P are drawn from a product law that is the same in both histories, then at step k each informed holder h in Theorem 11.3 or 11.4 sees nu_h * delta_w against nu_h (nu_h = law of everything else in h's fiber), so by Proposition 4.2 its TV is at least max nu_h, and the receiver is never left with nothing while it holds the record.
*Proof.* Proposition 4.2 with the source pair delta_w, delta_0 (k = w), applied to each holder separately; independence of the fiber sum from the appended grade is the product hypothesis. QED.

### 12.3 The holder is a present-state fact and climbs monotonically

**Theorem 12.3 (factorization independence).** h_k depends only on the retained view X_k', not on the sequence of contractions that produced it.
*Proof.* Retractions compose, r_{YW} = r_{ZW} o r_{YZ} (v16.25 P3; FCV P1), so the lineage image L_k(e) equals the one-step retraction r_{T X_k'}(e). QED.

**Theorem 12.4 (the holder climbs).** Along any history X_0' ⊇ X_1' ⊇ ... of nested retained views of T:
(a) h_{k+1} is h_k or a strict ancestor of h_k;
(b) h_{k+1} = h_k exactly when h_k is in X_{k+1}'; otherwise h_{k+1} is the nearest ancestor of h_k retained in X_{k+1}', so its depth strictly decreases;
(c) the holder never returns to a descendant of a former holder.
*Proof.* (a) and (b): h_{k+1} = r_{X_k' X_{k+1}'}(h_k) by Theorem 12.3, and a retraction sends a node to itself if retained and to its nearest retained ancestor otherwise. (c) Depth is nonincreasing by (a). QED.

**Corollary 12.5 (what holding the record certifies about the past).** A retained address a holds the occurrence record at step k (Proposition 12.2: the two histories differ at a) if and only if a is e or an ancestor of e and every node on the path from e up to a, excluding a, is absent from X_k'. Because retained views are nested, each of those nodes was pruned at some step at or before k and stays pruned.
*Proof.* h_k = a means exactly that a is the nearest retained ancestor of e in X_k'. QED.

Reading, and its limits. This is the ledger point applied to the receiver's own history. The record's value (the shift w, or the TV bound of Corollary 11.5 under nuisance) says *that* the append occurred. Its location says something about the receiver's own past retained structure, namely which part of the source's ancestry has already been pruned. The location is a function of the present retained view alone (Theorem 12.3), so reading it needs no log of the order. What it does carry is a monotone index: the holder's depth never increases (Theorem 12.4), which is the ordered recoverability update in present-state form. Pruning between incomparable branches leaves no trace in it, so the record does not fix a total order, which matches GPT's order-independent present-record contrast. For the access premise A, this gives a precise partial answer. A receiver that holds the record holds, in its own coordinate contrast, a certificate about past pruning. Whether it can read that contrast without the comparison history is still I*-b and the access premise. The contrast is a comparison between two histories, not a single-history observable.

## 13. Single-history access: the holder reads its own coordinate (new in draft 5)

Setting. This is the one-location occurrence pair of Proposition 12.2 under N_1(l): the prefix history has grade x_P on P, and the appended history has x_T = I_{PT} x_P + w e_e with w > 0. Both are compared on the lifted view X_k' through the inherited section I. Nuisance (as in Corollary 11.5): the grades x_v, v in P, are independent, with laws p_v, and the laws are the same in both histories. The contractions are written on P-addresses. Write y^0 and y^1 for the retained states at step k in the two histories, h = h_k for the holder, and nu_a for the law of the fiber sum s_a = sum over v in F_k(a) of x_v (with nu_e = delta_0 when e is in X_k').

**Lemma 13.1 (one differing factor).** The law of y^0 is the product over a in X_k' of nu_a. The law of y^1 is the same product with the factor at h replaced by nu_h * delta_w (the shift by w).
*Proof.* The fibers F_k(a) partition P (complete-fiber contraction, address stability), so the fiber sums are functions of disjoint blocks of independent grades and are independent. Proposition 12.2 gives y^1 = y^0 + w e_h. QED.

**Theorem 13.2 (the holder's coordinate is sufficient, and nothing else carries the record).** With the notation above:
(a) the likelihood ratio dL(y^1)/dL(y^0) at a retained configuration y is (nu_h * delta_w)(y_h) / nu_h(y_h), so it depends on y only through y_h, and y_h is a sufficient statistic for the occurrence pair (Fisher–Neyman factorization);
(b) TV(L(y^0), L(y^1)) = TV(nu_h, nu_h * delta_w), so the whole retained state is exactly as informative as the holder's own coordinate. With equal priors, the best single-shot test reads y_h alone and succeeds with probability (1 + TV)/2, and that TV is at least max nu_h (Proposition 4.2);
(c) every retained subsystem S that omits h has equal marginals in the two histories, so it has zero power, while every S that contains h has the full TV of (b). So {h} is the unique minimal retained subsystem with any power, and also the unique minimal sufficient one;
(d) if nu_h is a point mass (a definite prefix in h's fiber, in particular h = e), the TV is 1, and a single reading of y_h decides the occurrence with certainty.
*Proof.* (a) follows from Lemma 13.1, since the other factors cancel. (b) TV(Q ⊗ A, Q ⊗ B) = TV(A, B) for any probability law Q. (c) Marginalizing a product over factors other than h gives equal laws; if h is in S, the remaining factors cancel as in (b). (d) Point masses at c and c + w with w > 0 have disjoint supports. QED.

**Proposition 13.3 (the quantum read is the same read).** In the canonical reversible form (Proposition 5, classical inputs), both retained states are diagonal in the address-product basis. So their trace distance equals the TV of Theorem 13.2(b), and a Helstrom-optimal measurement is the projection onto {y : (nu_h * delta_w)(y_h) > nu_h(y_h)}. That projection acts only on the holder's register, in its grade basis, so no entangled or noncentral measurement on the retained register does better.
*Proof.* Commuting density matrices have trace distance equal to the TV of their joint eigenvalue laws, and the Helstrom projector is the positive part of their difference, which is diagonal. Lemma 13.1 makes that set a cylinder over y_h. QED.

**Proposition 13.4 (the read disturbs nothing).** The nonselective read of y_h, meaning the pinching onto the holder's grade basis (= the conditional expectation onto the holder's central coordinate algebra), leaves both retained states unchanged. So reading at step k and then contracting gives the same law as contracting without reading, and after the read the record is still held by h_{k+1} (Theorem 12.4), with the bound of Corollary 11.5.
*Proof.* Both states are diagonal (Proposition 13.3), so the pinching fixes them. This is record_access Theorem 3.1 (pinch = the register's own Lüders read) applied at h. Composition with later pushforwards is then trivial. QED.

**13.5 What the read tells the receiver about its past.** The holder's identity h_k is a function of the present retained view alone (Theorem 12.3), and the view is part of the present carrier. So in one history, the receiver at h can read two things with no added channel: the value y_h, which says whether the append occurred (Theorem 13.2), and its own position, which certifies that the path from e up to h has been pruned (Corollary 12.5). This is "a retained receiver reads its own present record, and the present record carries its past", in exactly the sense these results give.

**13.6 Control: correlated nuisance breaks sufficiency.** Let w = 1, and let the nuisance in h's fiber equal the grade at another retained address b, uniform on {0, 1}. The prefix history gives (y_h, y_b) in {(0,0), (1,1)}, and the appended history gives {(1,0), (2,1)}, each with probability 1/2. The holder's marginal TV is 1/2, b's marginal TV is 0, and the joint TV is 1. Here the holder is still necessary (b alone has no power) but not sufficient: b works as a reference that cancels the nuisance. So the product hypothesis of Lemma 13.1 is load-bearing. Without it, the minimal sufficient subsystem is the holder together with whatever is correlated with its fiber. This is the ledger point in a second form: correlation already present in the retained structure can be redistributed into the record's readability, without creating information.

**What this does for the access premise A.** The question was why a retained receiver can read its own record with no added readout channel. Three parts of the answer are earned. First, the record is a coordinate of the receiver's own central algebra (record_access Theorem 2.1). Second, under product nuisance that coordinate is sufficient, so nothing outside the receiver could help (Theorem 13.2, Proposition 13.3). Third, the read is non-disturbing, so it is compatible with every later native step (Proposition 13.4). Three things are still not earned. (i) The test uses the declared hypothesis pair (nu_h, w): the receiver must "know what it would see" under each history. That is standard in hypothesis testing and is what "initially uninformed relative to the pair" means, but it is a premise. (ii) Identifying the receiver's reading with evaluating a function of its own coordinate is still I*-b. It is now pinned to the unique minimal sufficient subsystem rather than chosen, but it is still a definition of what a receiver is. (iii) N_1(l), and the occurrence of each contraction, as before.

## 14. Multi-step access persistence along the holder climb (new in draft 6)

Setting. Continue the one-location occurrence pair of Proposition 12.2 under N_1(l) and product nuisance (Corollary 11.5 / Lemma 13.1). At step k the holder is h_k, the retained laws differ only in the h_k factor (nu_{h_k} against nu_{h_k} * delta_w), and {h_k} is the unique minimal sufficient retained subsystem (Theorem 13.2). Let X_k' ⊇ X_{k+1}' be a further nested retained view, with h_{k+1} = r_{X_k' X_{k+1}'}(h_k). By Theorem 12.4 the holder either stays (h_{k+1} = h_k, when h_k is retained) or climbs strictly to a proper ancestor (when h_k is pruned). This section asks what happens to accessibility under that native contraction: does the new holder still read the same occurrence contrast with no added channel, how does the readable TV change, and what of the past record survives.

**Theorem 14.1 (persistence of minimal sufficiency under native climb).** Under product nuisance, at step k+1:
(a) the retained laws differ only in the h_{k+1} factor (Lemma 13.1 applied at step k+1), so {h_{k+1}} is again the unique minimal sufficient retained subsystem for the same occurrence pair (Theorem 13.2 at step k+1);
(b) in particular, after a strict climb that prunes h_k, the ancestor h_{k+1} inherits the role of unique minimal sufficient subsystem: every retained subsystem that omits h_{k+1} has zero power, and every one that contains it has the full TV of the new holder;
(c) no added readout channel appears: the sufficient statistic is again a single central address coordinate of the present retained view (Proposition 1(c), record_access Theorem 2.1).
*Proof.* Fibers of a complete-fiber contraction still partition the prefix addresses (address stability), so product grades remain independent across the new fibers. Proposition 12.2 gives the retained difference w e_{h_{k+1}} at step k+1, independently of the path taken (Theorem 12.3). Lemma 13.1 and Theorem 13.2 therefore apply verbatim at the new step. QED.

Reading. Access under premise A is not a one-shot property of the arrival step. As long as product nuisance holds and the occurrence contrast has not been cancelled by a merge of opposite-sign lineages (Theorem 11.4(b)), every later native contraction that moves the holder keeps a unique minimal sufficient subsystem, namely the current lineage image. The climb redistributes the contrast; it does not create a second copy and does not require a new instrument.

**Lemma 14.0 (fiber growth under climb).** Write S_k for the fiber sum at h_k in the prefix history (law nu_{h_k}) and S_{k+1} for the fiber sum at h_{k+1}. Then S_{k+1} = S_k + R, where R is the sum of grades over every address that lies in F_{k+1}(h_{k+1}) but not in F_k(h_k) (the newly absorbed rest, including any sibling branches under the open path from h_k to h_{k+1}). Under product nuisance, R is independent of S_k and has the same law rho in both histories. In particular, when the holder stays (h_{k+1} = h_k) the rest R is the sum over addresses newly pruned into h_k; when the climb is strict, R also absorbs the grades that sat on the open path from h_k up to (but not including) h_{k+1}.
*Proof.* F_{k+1}(h_{k+1}) is the union of F_k(a) over retained-at-k addresses a with r(a) = h_{k+1}, together with any appended leaf still under that image; one of those a is h_k when h_k maps to h_{k+1}. Product independence gives the claim on R. QED.

**Proposition 14.2 (TV along the climb: nonincreasing). [D7: draft 6's title said "preserve or dilute" as an exclusive pair.]** Let TV_k = TV(nu_{h_k}, nu_{h_k} * delta_w) and TV_{k+1} = TV(nu_{h_{k+1}}, nu_{h_{k+1}} * delta_w). Then:
(a) TV_{k+1} <= TV_k, with equality whenever rho is a point mass (the newly absorbed rest is definite);
(b) more sharply, TV_{k+1} = TV(nu_{h_k} * rho, nu_{h_k} * rho * delta_w), so the climb acts as convolution by rho on the holder's law (data processing / DPI);
(c) TV_{k+1} >= max(nu_{h_k} * rho), by Proposition 4.2; in particular a definite source distinction never vanishes while it has a holder, but can be diluted arbitrarily by a diffuse absorbed rest (uniform rho on {0, ..., M} with w = 1 gives TV = 1/(M+1));
[D7] (a') a random rho can cause strict loss but need not: GPT's counterexample above keeps TV 1 with rho fair on {0,1}. The exact condition is Theorem 15.1.
(d) the same monotone bound holds when the holder stays but absorbs new random siblings into its fiber: TV is nonincreasing along any nested retained history under product nuisance, not only on strict climbs.
*Proof.* (b) is Lemma 14.0: nu_{h_{k+1}} = nu_{h_k} * rho. (a) Convolution by a fixed law is a classical channel, so data processing gives TV_{k+1} <= TV_k; a point-mass rho is a shift, a bijection, hence equality. (c) Proposition 4.2 applied at step k+1; the uniform example is Corollary 4.1's lower-bound illustration. (d) Same argument with R the newly absorbed siblings. QED.

Honesty about the loss. When TV drops, distinguishability has not been created elsewhere in the retained state (Theorem 14.1(a): only one factor differs). It has been erased into the absorbed nuisance, exactly the ledger's loss arrow (recoverable_pruning_ledger Proposition 11.6) and the Landauer-style cost of merging a record into a larger fiber. ## 15. The exact loss law along the climb, and keeping the fact versus recovering the past (new in draft 7)

Setting. As in section 14: one-location occurrence pair, N_1(l), product nuisance, grades valued in the inherited additive grade group G (ordinary integers for P1; the statements hold verbatim for any abelian group, for example a modular wrap Z_n). At step k the holder's two laws are P_0 = nu and P_1 = nu * delta_w. Write f = P_0 - P_1 = f^+ - f^- (Jordan decomposition, so TV_k = ||f^+||_1 = ||f^-||_1), A^+ = supp f^+, A^- = supp f^-, and for a law rho on G write D(rho) = supp rho - supp rho, its difference set (symmetric, contains 0). Along a later contraction the newly absorbed rest has law rho (Lemma 14.0), so the new holder's laws are P_0 * rho and P_1 * rho.

**Theorem 15.1 (exact loss law).**
(a) TV_{k+1} = TV_k - sum over y in G of min((f^+ * rho)(y), (f^- * rho)(y)). The lost distinguishability is exactly the mass at which the two hypotheses' smeared supports overlap.
(b) TV_{k+1} = TV_k if and only if (A^+ - A^-) ∩ D(rho) = ∅, that is, no difference between a point favoured by "no append" and a point favoured by "append" is a difference of two possible rest values.
*Proof.* (f * rho) = f^+ * rho - f^- * rho with both terms nonnegative, each of mass TV_k. For nonnegative a, b, |a - b| = a + b - 2 min(a, b), so ||f * rho||_1 = 2 TV_k - 2 sum min, giving (a). The sum vanishes iff the supports A^+ + supp rho and A^- + supp rho are disjoint (convolution of nonnegative functions has support equal to the sumset, with no cancellation), iff no x^+ + r = x^- + r' with x^± ∈ A^±, r, r' ∈ supp rho, iff (A^+ - A^-) ∩ D(rho) = ∅. QED.

This replaces draft 6's "exactly when rho is a point mass". A point mass has D = {0}, and 0 ∉ A^+ - A^- because A^+ and A^- are disjoint, so equality follows: the old sufficient condition is the special case.

**Corollary 15.2 (definite prefix; several steps).**
(a) If the prefix law at the holder is definite, nu = delta_s, then TV_k = 1 and TV_{k+1} = TV(rho, rho * delta_w) = 1 - sum_y min(rho(y), rho(y - w)). The occurrence stays perfectly readable at the new holder if and only if w ∉ D(rho).
(b) For integer grades this holds whenever |w| > max supp rho - min supp rho: an append larger than the spread of everything the climb absorbs is never blurred. GPT's example is w = 2 against spread 1. Draft 6's dilution example is w = 1 against a fair bit, so 1 ∈ D and TV = 1/2.
(c) Over a run of later contractions with independent rests rho_1, ..., rho_m (product nuisance), the total rest is rho_1 * ... * rho_m. Its difference set is the sumset D(rho_1) + ... + D(rho_m), and the contrast survives the whole run without loss iff (A^+ - A^-) ∩ (D(rho_1) + ... + D(rho_m)) = ∅. Since every D(rho_i) contains 0, the sumset only grows, which is the exact form of the monotone loss arrow of Proposition 14.2: once a step overlaps the two hypotheses, no later step can separate them again.
*Proof.* (a) is Theorem 15.1 with A^+ = {s}, A^- = {s + w} and D symmetric; the TV formula is a shift of (a) of the theorem. (b) For |w| larger than the diameter of supp rho, w ∉ D(rho). (c) supp(rho_1 * rho_2) = supp rho_1 + supp rho_2, so D(rho_1 * rho_2) = D(rho_1) + D(rho_2); then apply Theorem 15.1 to the total rest (Lemma 14.0 composed along the nested history). QED.

**Proposition 15.3 (when the past grade can be recovered).** Let S = supp P_0 ∪ supp P_1 be the admitted values of the old holder grade over both histories, and T = supp rho. On the admitted past configurations (s, r) ∈ S × T:
(a) the old holder grade s and the absorbed rest r are both functions of the present holder grade y = s + r if and only if (S - S) ∩ D(rho) = {0} (the sum map is injective on S × T);
(b) otherwise there is an explicit collision: s != s' in S and r, r' in T with s - s' = r' - r, so the admitted pasts (s, r) and (s', r') give the same present grade s + r = s' + r' [D8: draft 7 wrote (s, r') and (s', r), whose sums differ by 2(r' - r)] with different past grades. Each has positive weight under the product law in the history that admits it. This is the correct witness for Proposition 14.3(b);
(c) the same statement conditional on a known history H uses S_H = supp P_H in place of S.
*Proof.* (a) s + r = s' + r' with (s, r) != (s', r') forces s != s' (otherwise r = r') and s - s' = r' - r ∈ D(rho) \ {0}; the converse is the same equation read backwards. (b) is the failing case of (a). (c) Restrict the admitted set. QED.

**Corollary 15.4 (keeping the fact is strictly weaker than replaying the past).**
(a) If the past is recoverable (15.3(a)), the contrast is preserved: the present grade determines s, so the step is invertible on the admitted support and TV_{k+1} = TV_k.
(b) The converse fails. Take nu uniform on {0, 1}, w = 10 and rho uniform on {0, 1}. Then A^+ - A^- ⊆ {-11, -10, -9} misses D(rho) = {-1, 0, 1}, so TV stays 1, while S = {0, 1, 10, 11} has 1 ∈ (S - S) ∩ D(rho), so the old holder grade cannot be recovered from the present one.
(c) In words: after a climb the receiver can still know *that* the append occurred, with certainty, while the fine-grained *what it was at the old address* has been fused into the absorbed nuisance. Combined with Corollary 12.5, what survives the native update is the fact of the event and a coarse certificate of which ancestors were pruned, not a replay of the pruned grades.
*Proof.* (a) Under history H the present grade is the image of P_H ⊗ rho under the sum map, which by 15.3(a) is injective on S × T. TV is invariant under pushforward by a map that is injective on the union of the supports, so TV_{k+1} = TV(P_0 ⊗ rho, P_1 ⊗ rho) = TV(P_0, P_1) = TV_k. (b) Direct computation. QED.

Reading for access premise A and for time as ordered recoverability. Section 14 showed the occurrence contrast moves with the lineage image and never grows. Section 15 says exactly how much of it survives each native step, using only the inherited P1 sum and the laws already declared. There are three regimes, decided by the difference sets of what the climb absorbs:
1. **Past recoverable** ((S - S) ∩ D = {0}). Nothing is lost; the update is a relabelling on the admitted support.
2. **Fact kept, past fused** ((A^+ - A^-) ∩ D = ∅ but (S - S) ∩ D != {0}). The receiver reads the occurrence with the same power, but the fine past grade is no longer recoverable. This is the regime that matches "time as ordered recoverability update": the update removes recoverability of fine past detail while keeping the record that the event happened.
3. **Fact blurred** ((A^+ - A^-) ∩ D != ∅). Distinguishability is lost by exactly the overlap mass of Theorem 15.1(a). It is not moved elsewhere in the retained state (Theorem 14.1(a)), so it is erased, which is the ledger's loss arrow (recoverable_pruning_ledger Proposition 11.6).
[D8] Measured from a fixed base step k, with S, A^+ and A^- taken at k and D the difference set of the cumulative rest absorbed since k, the regime sequence along a history is monotone (Corollary 15.2(c)): 1 can turn into 2 or 3, and 2 into 3, but never the other way. Measured step by step against each step's own rest it is not monotone (a fair-bit rest with w = 1 blurs; a later definite rest is a per-step relabelling), so the arrow belongs to the cumulative comparison with a fixed past, not to individual steps.

Earned and assumed. Earned: Theorem 15.1, Corollaries 15.2 and 15.4 and Proposition 15.3, from Lemma 14.0, Theorem 14.1 and the inherited additive P1 transport alone, with no added channel, controller or readout. Assumed, unchanged from section 14: product nuisance, N_1(l), the declared hypothesis pair (nu, w), I*-b as the identification of reading with a function of the holder's coordinate, and occurrence of each contraction (Aaron's empirical input). Correlated nuisance (control 14.5) is outside this section: there the rest is not independent of the old holder's fiber, and Theorem 15.1's convolution form does not apply.

Novelty, stated plainly. Mathematically this is not new. Theorem 15.1 is the L1 norm of a convolved signed measure. Proposition 15.3 is the injectivity of a sumset (S + T is a direct sum iff (S - S) ∩ (T - T) = {0}, standard in additive combinatorics). Together they are the zero-error confusability conditions for an additive-noise channel (Shannon 1956, "The zero error capacity of a noisy channel": inputs x, x' are confusable iff x - x' ∈ D(rho) \ {0}), applied once to the two hypothesis classes and once to individual past values. What is new for UQCF is only the application. It gives an exact, assumption-light classification of what an inherited holder climb does to a single occurrence record, it corrects draft 6's overclaim, and it separates "the receiver keeps the fact of the event" from "the receiver can replay the past", which is the distinction the ordered-recoverability reading of time needs.

## 16. Typing the event: the elementary pruning step forces P1 and its receiver (new in draft 9)

Where this sits. PROBE equation (11) asks for two things: (i) the actual event's typed action on the grade configuration, and (ii) the identification of the receiver's accessible state with its own coordinate algebra. The accepted P1 receiver-process review ([receiver_process_review/REVIEW_ACCEPTANCE.md](../../receiver_process_review/REVIEW_ACCEPTANCE.md), run 39136458718) accepted acquisition and internal consumption *given* that the event is a P1 contraction, and left both parts open. Throughout sections 1 to 15, and in FCV PROOFS P1, the P1 map is *defined*: r_XY(v) is the longest prefix of v in Y and (P_XY x)_y = sum over r_XY(v) = y of x_v. This section asks whether that type can be derived from the elementary native update, so that accepting "a pruning occurred" (Aaron's empirical input) already fixes what it does and who receives. Part (ii), ownership, is not addressed here and stays the I*-b premise.

**Setting.** X is a finite prefix-closed carrier with Genesis 0, parent map par, and grades in a field F (FCV P0: Q or R; integer grades are the declared instance). A *single pruning* removes exactly one address l from the carrier W and leaves a prefix-closed carrier W \ {l}.

**Lemma 16.0 (one pruning removes a leaf).** W \ {l} is prefix-closed if and only if l ≠ 0 and l is a leaf of W (no child of l lies in W).
*Proof.* If a child c of l is in W, then c ∈ W \ {l} but its prefix l is not. Conversely, if l is a leaf, every remaining address keeps all of its prefixes. QED.

**Elementary-step conditions.** A single-pruning action T_{W,l}: F^W → F^{W \ {l}} satisfies:
- (E1) linearity (FCV P0 linearity of the grade space);
- (E2) total-grade conservation (FCV P1: summing over fibers preserves total grade);
- (E3) unpruned grades stay put: T_{W,l}(x) restricted to configurations supported on W \ {l} is the identity (the FCV P2 section property P I = id at this step);
- (E4) ancestry: T_{W,l}(e_l) is supported on prefixes (ancestors) of l. The pruned distinction is credited only to retained addresses whose history is a prefix of its own, which is the compatible-history and ancestry-ledger reading of the carrier;
- (E5) relative rule: there are fixed numbers w_1, w_2, ... such that, for every carrier W and every leaf l at depth k, T_{W,l}(e_l) puts weight w_d on the ancestor of l at distance d (d = 1, ..., k). The rule refers to positions *relative to the pruned distinction*, not to absolute depth (which would count the number of earlier distinctions, a clock reading) or to Genesis as an absolute anchor. This condition is assumed, not earned; see Control 16.5.

**Theorem 16.1 (the elementary step is forced).** Under (E1)-(E5), T_{W,l}(x) = x|_{W \ {l}} + x_l e_{par(l)} for every carrier and every leaf. Equivalently, T_{W,l} = P_{W, W \ {l}}, the inherited P1 map for that single pruning.
*Proof.* By (E1) and (E3), T(x) = x|_{W \ {l}} + x_l T(e_l). By (E4) and (E5), T(e_l) = sum_{d=1}^{k} w_d e_{anc_d(l)}, where anc_d(l) is the ancestor of l at distance d and k = depth(l). By (E2), sum_{d=1}^{k} w_d = 1. Leaves exist at every depth k ≥ 1 (the chain 0 → 1 → ... → k), so sum_{d ≤ k} w_d = 1 for all k ≥ 1. Taking k = 1 gives w_1 = 1, and subtracting consecutive equations gives w_d = 0 for d ≥ 2. Hence T(e_l) = e_{par(l)}, and par(l) is the longest prefix of l in W \ {l}. QED.
No positivity is needed: signed formal sources (FCV P2) are covered.

**Theorem 16.2 (every native contraction is a sequence of forced steps, in any order).**
(a) For prefix-closed Y ⊆ X with 0 ∈ Y and Y ≠ X, every maximal element of X \ Y (in the prefix order) is a leaf of X, and removing it leaves a prefix-closed carrier containing Y. So X reaches Y by |X \ Y| single prunings, and every such sequence removes only leaves (Lemma 16.0).
(b) For every such sequence l_1, ..., l_m, the composite of the forced steps of Theorem 16.1 equals P_XY. In particular the result does not depend on the order of the prunings.
*Proof.* (a) If m is maximal in X \ Y and c is a child of m in X, then c ∉ Y (otherwise its prefix m would be in Y), so c ∈ X \ Y and is above m, which contradicts maximality. So m is a leaf of X; Lemma 16.0 gives prefix-closure, and Y is untouched. (b) Each step is P_{W, W \ {l}} by Theorem 16.1, and FCV P1 proves r_YZ r_XY = r_XZ for nested prefix-closed carriers, hence P_YZ P_XY = P_XZ. Compose along the sequence. QED.
Reading: a unit of grade at v moves up v's chain one parent at a time, only when its current address is pruned, and stops at the first address that is never pruned, which is the longest prefix of v in Y. The inherited longest-prefix definition is therefore the *output* of the elementary update rule, not an extra input. Order-theoretically, r_XY is the right adjoint of the inclusion Y ⊆ X (for y ∈ Y, y ≤ v iff y ≤ r_XY(v)): the most recent retained record compatible with v.

**Proposition 16.3 (forced receiver, whole-algebra blindness, native addition).** Let x and x' = x + w e_l (w ≠ 0) be a one-location pair at a leaf l (the occurrence pair of section 11 at its appended entry), and let the event be the single pruning of l.
(a) Before the event, the two configurations agree at every address other than l. In particular, the receiver-to-be p = par(l) has the same value of its own coordinate, so the *entire* own-coordinate algebra B_p^X = functions of x_p is blind to the pair (identical states for every observable in it, not a selected effect).
(b) After the event, T(x') - T(x) = w e_p. Exactly one address's own coordinate changes, and it is p. Every other address's own algebra is blind before and after.
(c) On p's own coordinate the event acts as q_p ↦ q_p + q_l: native addition of the absorbed grade, the commutative form of a controlled-add, obtained from the forced step with no supplied gate, controller or READS table.
(d) Nothing is created globally: the difference w e_l becomes w e_p, with the same total and the same magnitude. The distinction is redistributed from l to p (the ledger point of STANDING_DIRECTIVE addendum (b)).
(e) For a later contraction to any Y not containing p, Theorem 16.2 and Theorem 3 carry the difference to r_XY(l). The holder climb of sections 12 to 15 is therefore a sequence of forced elementary steps; every result there that used P1 now rests on (E1)-(E5) instead of on a supplied P1 type.
*Proof.* (a) Definition of the pair. (b) Linearity of T and Theorem 16.1. (c) Theorem 16.1 restricted to the coordinate p. (d) Conservation (E2). (e) Theorems 16.2 and 3. QED.

**Corollary 16.4 (what this does to PROBE equation (11)).** If the accepted empirical event is a single native pruning, and (E1)-(E5) hold, then its typed action on grade configurations is P_{X, X \ {l}}, its receiver is par(l), and the receiver's pre-event own-coordinate algebra is blind while its post-event own coordinate separates the pair. That is the event half of (11), for the elementary step and hence (Theorem 16.2) for every native contraction. The other half is untouched: that the receiver's accessible state *is* its own-coordinate algebra B_p (I*-b; ownership), together with the premises of sections 11 to 15 (N_1(l), product nuisance for the sufficiency results, the declared hypothesis pair). This section does not derive ownership and does not claim native access.

**Control 16.5 (the relative-rule condition is load-bearing).** Two rules satisfy (E1)-(E4) and give order-independent composites, but violate (E5) and give different receivers:
(a) *Genesis dump*: T(e_l) = e_0 for every leaf. The receiver of every distinction is Genesis, so no local receiver exists, and Theorem 3's single-owner trail collapses to the root. The rule refers to Genesis as an absolute anchor.
(b) *Grandparent when available*: T(e_l) = e_{par(par(l))} if depth(l) ≥ 2, else e_0. It skips a retained parent, so the receiver is not the nearest retained record. Its weights depend on absolute depth (w_1 = 1 at depth 1, w_2 = 1 at depth ≥ 2), which (E5) excludes.
Both being order-independent (scratch check below) shows that confluence alone does not select P1. Of the conditions used, (E5), stated relative to the pruned distinction, is the one that does.

**Earned and assumed.** Earned from inherited sources: Lemma 16.0 (prefix-closure), (E1) (FCV P0), (E2) (FCV P1), (E3) (FCV P2 section), and the order independence in Theorem 16.2 (FCV P1 composition). Interpretive but grounded: (E4), the ancestry-ledger reading that a pruned distinction is credited only to a compatible earlier record. Assumed: (E5), the relative rule. It is motivated by the no-fundamental-clock and no-inserted-anchor rules, but it is a homogeneity premise, not a derivation. Still assumed from earlier sections: occurrence of the pruning (Aaron's empirical input), I*-b ownership, N_1(l), and the product-nuisance and hypothesis-pair premises for sections 13 to 15. Native bridge: NOT_DERIVED.

**Novelty, stated plainly.** Mathematically this is elementary. Theorem 16.1 is a triangular linear system. Theorem 16.2 is the standard fact that contracting tree edges one at a time composes to the nearest-retained-ancestor map, with the order independence already in FCV P1. The adjunction remark is standard order theory. Within UQCF, though, the P1 type has been a definition in FCV and a supplied premise in every receiver argument, and PROBE (11) and the accepted receiver-process review list the event's typed action as unearned. This section replaces that supplied type with five conditions on a single pruning, four of them inherited, and identifies the one remaining homogeneity premise exactly. It does not give a physical measurement model (no decoherence, einselection or quantum Darwinism content is claimed). The receiver is fixed by the update rule, not by a pointer basis or an environment.

**Setting.** A carrier W (finite rooted tree, Genesis 0), a grade x on W, and nonroot cone totals c_v = C_v(x) for v ≠ 0. The *Genesis-balanced part* of x is x° = x − (Σ x) δ_0. **Lemma 23.1 (local reads are incident-flux reads).** For nonroot p, the local reads at p are exactly f = −β a_{k} c_p + Σ_{u ∈ ch(p)} γ_u a_{k+1} c_u with k = depth(p) and free scalars β, γ_u. *Proof.* A linear functional of the values φ_q, q ∈ N[p], that vanishes on constants is a combination of the differences φ_q − φ_p, and these are, up to sign, the edge values (dφ) on the edges incident to p. The edge (par(p), p) has depth k and carries a_k c_p. A child edge has depth k + 1 and carries a_{k+1} c_u. The map from W_W to the incident edge values is onto (d_W is an isomorphism), so every combination is attained. At Genesis there is no parent edge. QED.
