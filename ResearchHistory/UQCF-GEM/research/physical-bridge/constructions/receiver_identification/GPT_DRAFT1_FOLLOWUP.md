## Proposition 4: include the entire owner fiber

Keep the draft's hypotheses: p and q are product distributions, agree at every address except removed u, and undergo the same contraction r. Write a=r(u), and define

    N_a = sum of X_z over z in r^{-1}(a), z != u,
    nu_a = law(N_a).

N_a includes the prior owner and every other removed atom absorbed into it. Independence gives the same nuisance law nu_a under both preparations, independent of X_u.

The correct owner laws are nu_a*p_u and nu_a*q_u. Therefore

    TV(P_r p, P_r q)
      = TV(nu_a*p_u, nu_a*q_u)
      <= TV(p_u,q_u)
      = TV(p,q).

The retained-joint equality follows because disjoint fibers of a product input produce independent output coordinates, and every non-owner output has the same law in both preparations.

Equality is guaranteed when nu_a is a point mass. A point-mass prior at a alone is insufficient. This is a sufficient condition, not an iff characterization.

### Exact counterexample to the current equality sentence

Take a rooted tree with root a and children u,v, and retain only a. Let a=0 in both preparations; let u=0 under p and u=1 under q; let v be independent and fair on {0,1} under both.

The owner laws after P1 are respectively

    p: mass 1/2 at 0 and 1;
    q: mass 1/2 at 1 and 2.

Output TV is 1/2, while source TV is 1 and the owner's prior is deterministic. This satisfies the draft's product and single-differing-address assumptions.

The frozen P4 verification domain considers triples (p_u,q_u,p_a), without a second random removed atom in that fiber. Passing those tests establishes their restricted cases; it cannot establish the broader equality sentence.
