Under the declared product nuisance and common nested contractions, the old holder's prefix fiber is contained in the new holder's fiber. Its new law is therefore `nu * rho`, where `rho` is the common, independent law of the newly included rest. The two occurrence laws at the new holder are `nu * rho` and `nu * rho * delta_w`.

## Exact native-tree counterexample

Use ordinary nonnegative integer grades, with no modular wrap. Let the prefix be the rooted path `a -> l -> m`. Root `a` has grade `R`, fair on `{0,1}`; `l` and `m` have grade zero. These prefix grades are independent.

Compare that prefix with the append of a leaf `e` below `m`, of grade `w = 2`, under the same supplied premise `N_1(m)`. Let `H = 0` and `H = 1` label the no-append and append histories; H is a hypothesis label, not an added carrier register.

1. Prune the subtree at `m`, retaining `a,l`. Complete-fiber absorption leaves retained state `(R, 2H)`. The current holder is the common prefix address `l`.
2. Prune `l`, retaining `a`. This is a strict holder climb `l -> a`. The final root grade is `Y = R + 2H`.

The newly included rest is the new owner's prior R, which is nondegenerate. Nevertheless:
- Before the climb the holder laws are `delta_0` and `delta_2`, with TV = 1.
- After the climb the laws are uniform on `{0,1}` and `{2,3}`, still with TV = 1.
- Both the old holder grade and the rest are functions of the final grade: `y_l = 2 floor(Y/2)` and `R = Y mod 2`.

All four admitted past states have different outputs. The unrestricted sum map is many-to-one, but its restriction to this occurrence pair is injective. The retained receiver acquires the distinction without creating information, and random rest causes no loss here.

## Minimal correction

1. Keep Proposition 14.2(a)'s one-way statement. Replace the exclusive preserve/dilute language by: **“TV is nonincreasing. Definite newly included rest guarantees equality; random rest can cause strict loss but need not.”**
2. State Proposition 14.3(b) as nonrecoverability **in general**, rather than whenever rho is nondegenerate. A particular nonrecoverability claim needs distinct admissible past configurations with the same present retained state and different target past grades. Nondegeneracy alone does not establish that collision.
3. The displayed pairs in 14.3's proof do not have equal sums as written. Even after correcting the second coordinate, independence does not guarantee that the required shifted old grade is in its admitted support.
4. In 14.4, distinguish removal of an address as a retained factor from inability to reconstruct its old value. A removed address's value can remain a function of the present grade, as above.
