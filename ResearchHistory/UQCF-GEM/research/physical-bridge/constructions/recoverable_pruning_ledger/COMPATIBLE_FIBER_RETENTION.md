## 1. The constructive move

The internal-reference witness needs the first absorbed grade to remain comparable with the second aggregate. Instead of postulating an appended numerical history, retain the receiver's earlier fiber as a distinguishable part of its enlarged fiber. The same finitely additive grade then assigns an amount to the old part and to the new whole. Their ratio is the desired record content.

This gives an explicit candidate for connecting inherited record compatibility to the grade construction: **the old receiver support remains a central projection inside the enlarged receiver support**. Once that inclusion is justified, the least algebra containing it and the new support is forced; no grade-dependent writer or selected source-bit encoder is needed. The sources inspected here earn the fibers, grades, and the conditional central-preservation argument. They do not yet identify these fiber projections with this receiver's actual accessible records. That identification is the remaining bridge, not an established conclusion of this note.

This is one continuation of the existing witness. It does not introduce a noncommutative carrier, a new outcome law, or an alternative physical dynamics.

## 2. Objects inherited from the pruning construction

Fix the same finite pruning sequence in both source preparations,

    X_0 ⊇ X_1 ⊇ ... ⊇ X_j,

with the same Genesis identifier and a receiver address t retained throughout. Define F_l(t) = r_{0l}^{-1}(t). P1 gives F_0(t) = {t} and nested fibers F_0(t) ⊆ ... ⊆ F_j(t). A retained address is fixed by each relevant retraction. P0 and P1 give, for a finitely additive grade μ,

    (P_{0l} μ)(t) = μ(F_l(t)).                         (1)

For the ratio witness, take nonnegative real grades (positive rational examples suffice analytically). This is a declared graded instance, not a proof that every such source is physically preparable.

For a subset B of X_0 write e_B for its indicator in the auxiliary pointwise function algebra C^{X_0}. Thus e_B e_D = e_{B∩D}. This algebra is a notation for distinguishable subsets of the original native addresses; it is not an asserted quantum Hilbert space or physical observable algebra. A grade evaluates it by

    μ(f) = Σ_{x∈X_0} μ({x}) f(x).

In particular μ(e_B)=μ(B). Identifying that evaluation with receiver access is a separate physical obligation. If μ were instead interpreted as a quantum probability law, its evaluation would generally be an expectation, not a single realized record. This construction does not make that substitution.

## 3. A minimal compatible retained part, constructed online

Begin with C_0(t) = span{e_{F_0(t)}}. At a later step, view a function supported on F_{l-1}(t) as a function on F_l(t) by zero extension. This is a canonical map for the actual subset inclusion; it preserves products and adjoints. It is nonunital when the fiber grows: the old unit e_{F_{l-1}(t)} becomes a proper projection under the new unit e_{F_l(t)}.

Here is the precise candidate compatibility premise to be justified natively:

> The surviving receiver retains its previous subset algebra under this zero-extension map, and its new coarse fiber is its current support.

This is stronger than the address identity r(t)=t. It asserts persistence of a retained subobject, not just persistence of a name. It is also not yet a consequence of the v15.11 algebra chain: that theorem needs an already justified physical algebra/center correspondence.

Conditional on this premise, the least compatible next algebra is uniquely

    C_l(t) = alg(C_{l-1}(t), e_{F_l(t)}).              (2)

Let A_0(t)={t} and A_l(t)=F_l(t)\F_{l-1}(t). Empty shells are omitted. Then

    C_l(t) = span{e_{A_0(t)}, ..., e_{A_l(t)}}.        (3)

Proof. The previous algebra contains its unit e_{F_{l-1}(t)}. Any algebra containing that algebra and the new unit contains their difference e_{A_l(t)}. The latter is orthogonal to every previous supported function. Therefore the displayed span is already an algebra containing the required inputs, and every admissible algebra contains it. Induction establishes (3). Repeated fibers add nothing. QED.

Equivalently, for a nonempty new shell, C_l(t) is the direct sum of the old algebra and the scalar algebra on that shell. The proof does not require preserving every distinction internal to the incoming shell, or inheriting the removed owners' own histories. Those would be additional retention requirements.

This is an online construction: only the previous retained parts and the newly determined fiber enter (2). It does not give an early receiver the future pruning sequence. Neither the source value nor the desired answer chooses the new projection. Relabeling native addresses while transporting the pruning sequence transports every set and indicator in (2)-(3).

The minimality statement is exact but limited: least **subset algebra** retaining the old algebra and admitting the current support. It does not claim minimum physical memory among every possible representation of one chosen bit. Nor does uniqueness of this least algebra force nature to select it; any physical realization of the premise may retain a larger algebra.

## 4. The first grade survives as a value on a present retained part

Restrict the existing grade to C_l(t): ν_l = μ|_{C_l(t)}. Its values on the orthogonal shell indicators are

    ν_l(e_{A_k(t)}) = μ(A_k(t)).

For every earlier m≤l, e_{F_m(t)} = Σ_{k≤m} e_{A_k(t)} belongs to C_l(t), and

    ν_l(e_{F_m(t)}) = μ(F_m(t)) = (P_{0m} μ)(t).      (4)

Thus the earlier aggregate is recoverable from the present retained graded structure. Equation (4) uses preservation of a part and finite additivity; it does not require a separate numerical append operation. In this representation the history is redundant notation for current values on retained nested parts.

At the level of unnormalized grade restrictions, ν_l restricted to C_m(t) equals ν_m. For projective access, use the one common grade class [μ] before taking local restrictions. If μ(F_l)>0, the normalized present functional is ν_l/μ(F_l). On the old corner, renormalizing by its mass recovers the earlier normalized functional when μ(F_m)>0. The old corner's mass relative to the enlarged whole is new relational content; it is not determined by the earlier normalized functional alone.

This matters for the internal-reference witness: merely retaining a sequence of independently normalized one-dimensional records is insufficient. The candidate retains the old support projection in the same graded structure as the new unit, which is precisely what lets one common rescaling cancel.

## 5. Apply it to the same one-receiver witness

Use X_0={o,a,u,v}, with u and v children of a. The receiver is a; it does not read the root grade c. In order (o,a,u,v), let

    x_0=(c,0,g,h),    x_1=(c,0,h,g),    g,h>0, g≠h.

Remove u and then v, retaining o and a. Both the pruning sequence and every subset algebra are identical between preparations. The source difference enters only through the existing grades.

| Stage | Receiver support | Least retained parts | Grades, source 0 | Grades, source 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial | {a} | {a} | (0) | (0) |
| After u | {a,u} | {a}, {u} | (0,g) | (0,h) |
| After v | {a,u,v} | {a}, {u}, {v} | (0,g,h) | (0,h,g) |

The first two receiver grade classes are identical between sources. At the final stage the old unit e_{F_1}=e_{\{a,u\}} is a retained projection below e_{F_2}=e_{\{a,u,v\}}. The same present-state query gives

    q = ν_2(e_{F_1}) / ν_2(e_{F_2}),
    q_0 = g/(g+h),    q_1 = h/(g+h).                  (5)

For g=1,h=2 this is 1/3 versus 2/3. One common positive rescaling cancels. No external root reference is used. With exact access to this present graded structure, the receiver therefore separates the sources. This remains conditional on that access; the formula is not itself a detector or sampling law.

For this particular acquisition, even the full shell algebra in the table is unnecessary. The smaller algebra

    D_2(a) = span{e_{F_1}, e_{F_2\F_1}}

already has grade vector (g,h) versus (h,g). It is the least algebra containing the previous coarse receiver unit e_{F_1} and the current unit e_{F_2}. It need not distinguish a from u or reconstruct all earlier grades. Thus the immediate native target can be narrowed to retention of the previous receiver support, rather than retention of every past reading. Preserving this smaller algebra and the second support in later enlargements suffices for the persistence statement below; bare retention of only the most recent support at each later step would not suffice.

For later compatible enlargement, preserve both e_{F_1} and e_{F_2}. Their ratio remains (5), even if the current total grows. The denominator is the retained second support, not the new whole. Consequently the acquired distinction persists without keeping an explicit list of numerical readings.

An endpoint-only merge retains just span{e_{F_2}} and its grade g+h. It is a valid coarse pushforward but does not satisfy the candidate old-part retention premise. This comparison locates the work done by compatibility, rather than claiming that bare aggregation already supplies memory.

## 6. Connection to the inherited record primitive

v15.11 proves that for an already given physical retained-algebra chain B⊆A, the condition Z(A)⊆B preserves the old center in Z(B). Its compatible actual records then refine by projection inclusion. This earns a preservation argument **after** the old central records and the relevant algebra chain have been identified.

Equations (2)-(5) give a concrete object to which such a preservation argument could apply: the old receiver-support projection inside the enlarged receiver. The type correspondence still to establish is

    native earlier receiver fiber F_m(a)
        ↔ retained central subobject e_{F_m(a)}
        ↔ an available part of the receiver's present graded state.

The left-hand subset is derived. The auxiliary indicator and its algebraic consequences are derived. The physical middle/right identification is not supplied by naming the indicator a record.

There are two scope distinctions to preserve in that identification:

1. These local algebras have growing support and nonunital corner inclusions. They are not literally the same fixed-unit decreasing physical algebras in v15.11. The source theorem cannot be applied just by matching the word “center.” A native embedding into the appropriate receiver-retention structure is needed.
2. Retaining old parts of surviving receivers differs from retaining every old global central part, including all parts owned by removed receivers. The latter would preserve more information. No global center-preservation law is inferred from this local candidate.

The certified source-binding tuple supplies Genesis, provenance, event identity and full address, but no grade-history contents. The provenance hash certifies an ordered history only when its underlying ledger is present; the hash alone does not expose this fiber projection or its grade. Neither source justifies the missing correspondence by itself.
