## What was learned

An actual visible deletion of d from the initial fourth root leaves core support {e}. The known floor then certifies at least one hidden spectator without reading its identity or total root size. A second visible deletion can certify at least two. Restoring the original core projection does not erase the retained maximum deficit while spectators stay invariant.

Thus an ordered observation record can retain information which its final core snapshot does not contain. D is an information lower bound, not energy or a conservation law. D>=1 certifies positive hidden capacity; D=0 remains ambiguous. No such history certifies emptiness. The all-rejection history prevents guaranteed finite two-sided decision. Restoration is reachable through a committed addition, not guaranteed to occur.

This differs from the closed spectator-only safe-probe theorem: the core probe may reveal an actually committed edit whose floor feasibility depends on hidden capacity. An attempted edit alone is never credited. No nondestructive legality oracle, mandatory success, fairness or new acknowledgement variable is introduced.
