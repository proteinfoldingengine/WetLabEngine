3. Refined research priorities

Priority A — Derive necessary consequences

Determine which mathematical consequences follow from the inherited native structure, without assuming the desired observable outcome.

Focus on:

* Information preservation and distinguishability.
* Pruning, retained grade, and provenance.
* Record acquisition and transfer.
* Receiver algebra and observational equivalence.
* Event-induced changes in correlations and outcome laws.
* Conditions under which a previously inaccessible difference becomes accessible.

Distinguish universal consequences from conditional consequences and counterexamples.

A theorem showing that a proposed consequence is not forced can be scientifically valuable when it identifies the exact missing premise.

Priority B — Translate consequences into observable predictions

For each promising theorem, explicitly determine:

1. What informational difference exists before the event?
2. What actual native event or admissible transformation applies?
3. What difference necessarily survives afterward?
4. Which already justified receiver observable distinguishes the outputs?
5. What would an experiment or observation detect?
6. What result would falsify the proposed connection?

Seek a common observable consequence across all justified admissible mechanisms, even when their microscopic implementations differ.

Do not introduce a copying gate, reader, detector, measurement basis, or accessibility rule merely to make the consequence visible.

An outcome probability law can qualify without predicting a unique outcome. Distinguish ensemble statistics from individual retained records.
