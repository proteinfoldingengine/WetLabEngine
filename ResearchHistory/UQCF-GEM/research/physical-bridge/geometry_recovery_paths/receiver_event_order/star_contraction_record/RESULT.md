# A source withdrawal changes another chart's own record during the inherited star contraction

**Result.** With the same initial 121-chart atlas, source chart, receiver prior and supplied chart order, two different withdrawals at chart 0 can force different extensive records at one and the same third-party chart B when the same v16.43 normalizer is invoked. The difference can be compared immediately after B first loses an existing leaf U in both histories. No distance evaluator, new update rule or global clock is used by the contraction. Every chart starts with 200 leaves. B already held the leaf whose retention differs. The distinction concerns whether it keeps an existing record. Geometry admission is unnecessary for the historical normalizer.

## Original operation and its premises

The v16.43 star producer takes a chart-indexed incidence state, m leaves, k charts and the exact hitting number q. A hitting set is a set of charts that collectively hold every leaf. The code enumerates q-subsets in chart order and takes the first such set H that covers all leaves. In leaf order, it keeps each leaf at the earliest member of H holding it, deleting it from every other holder one chart at a time. The next phases recolor to the common canonical assignment when m>q. Its producer invokes normalize in a finite campaign and stores the returned trace as indexed graph states. The published campaign includes (5,3), not (968,121). We use the same function's mathematical specification at the larger size. The exact q and numeric B at that size were not computed. No caller starting this normalization after an orbit withdrawal has been found here.

With 968 leaves, chart footprints of 200 and maximum work 1472, every two distinct charts share at least (400−1472/4)/2=16 indexed leaves. Five charts can collectively hold at most 200+4(200−16)=936, so q≥6. Translation invariance supplies a minimum cover containing chart 0. Lexicographic priority therefore ensures A=0 is the first member of H. Let B be its second member. The same reasoning applies to arbitrary chart order by translating its first chart to 0 and comparing every possible B. Which physical chart occupies this role depends on the supplied ordering.

If one leaf originally has two members of H among its holders, removing it at just one of those members preserves H. Removing a membership can only discard feasible hitting sets. Therefore H stays minimum and lexicographically first. The membership change can affect the contraction owner of only that leaf. At the first all-singleton state, B has one additional leaf in one history exactly when the changed contraction owner switches to B in that arm and does not in the other. On this star the v16.23 P1 mass at B's local Pin is 1+2(968−number of leaves B holds). An extra held leaf thus gives two fewer units at the Pin. Only B's local grade and count are needed to distinguish these records once the comparison event is identified.

## Same actor, different source leaf

Let S be a leaf shared by A and B. We also need a leaf T held by A and another member of H, outside B. The stronger guess that every third chart D supplies such a T fails for 56 of 14,280 ordered pairs (B,D) with A fixed. For a given B, call D exceptional if every leaf shared by A and D is also in B. Enumerating the pinned orbit shows that A, B and all exceptional D together omit at least 160 indexed leaves, for every B. Thus H contains some nonexceptional D, and T exists. Both possible source cuts keep H and B's entire initial and immediate post-cut holdings, with all fine grades identical. In the S arm, S goes from preferred owner A to B; in the T arm, T changes to some selected holder outside B. Here “owner” designates the single survivor after contraction; no leaf is gained during contraction. Every other leaf keeps the same preferred owner. The resulting local count difference survives through the all-singleton state, reached in both arms after exactly 24,200−1−968=23,231 deletions from the cut. This conclusion about contraction output is independent of the order of the leaves.

For a receiver-local comparison, choose S before another leaf U shared by A and B, possible under every leaf order since at least 16 are shared. In both arms A still holds U and B makes a real deletion of U. After B first loses U, it still holds S in the S arm and has deleted S in the T arm. B never held T, so its remaining incidences otherwise agree. This is an address in the original receiver prior and a common transition in its own trace. A local detector choosing U or an intrinsic priority of indexed charts has not been derived. The count of edits need not agree at this marker.

A second control holds the indexed leaf fixed, and compares loss at A against loss at a third original holder C distinct from A,B (each leaf has 25 holders). The same H survives; only the A cut moves the contraction survivor from A to B. All these observations condition on initiating the stated cut and running the normalizer.

## Indistinguishable first-cut geometry and the order control

The orbit includes two indexed copies of each of 484 support masks. For each possible B, there are at least eight twin pairs shared with A having a third shared leaf U strictly between the twin addresses in the inherited orbit enumeration. The receipt records a witness triple of one-based leaf addresses for each B. Delete the earlier twin at A in one arm, the later twin there in the other. The source chart, B's entire prior and both full pair-work tables (before and immediately after the cut) match between arms, since swapping equally weighted twin columns preserves all Hamming distances. The post-cut atlases are related by that twin permutation if leaf order is forgotten. The first-cut minimum-neighbor graph is irregular in both arms: if an old edge at A drops below 704, all minimum edges then touch A; otherwise A loses all four old edges and is isolated. The other edges cannot cross the 704-to-1088 gap. Thus the old square-geometry admission gate cannot account for the subsequent activity.

In the contraction the earlier twin is kept at B only when A dropped it. By B's first loss of U the later twin has not yet been processed. The local count differs by one and P1 Pin by two. At the all-singleton state B holds exactly one of the two twins in each history. Their numerical totals now agree while their addressed records differ. Later canonical recoloring sends both full inputs to exactly the same terminal indexed state. Swapping the semantic twin identities in the input order reverses the intermediate contrast. Placing twins adjacently removes the intermediate shared-leaf landmark between them. This exposes which part of the result depends on the supplied order. The distinct-support result above remains valid at the contraction boundary under every permutation of the leaves.

## Same physical receiver, priority removed from its earlier role

Hold fixed chart 0, B, the two original source alternatives, all incidences and all leaf identities. Promote B to the front of the chart order without changing the relative order of other charts. The same H is still lexicographically first in each arm. It contains B; competitors omitting B now rank after it, and any two covers containing B retain their previous relative order because their remaining entries have the same order as before. The numerical optimum is independent of the enumeration. Now every leaf initially at B must remain at B throughout contraction, since B is its own first available selected holder. B makes exactly zero deletions in either arm. In particular the comparison event at which it lost U under the previous priority never occurs. The code replays this change with the physical source, receiver and source leaves held fixed in both the distinct-support and twin small fixtures.

This argument works on the full orbit and for every original matched witness described above. Changing priority can eliminate the entire receiver-local difference even when the same selected set survives. Thus a first-principles explanation must also account for how the system determines priority, or demonstrate a recipient distinction that survives all the physically interchangeable choices.
