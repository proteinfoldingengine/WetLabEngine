## Direct acquisition reaches the original geometry interface
As in the original study, A's withdrawal of T is legal and leaves the retained union unchanged. It removes the four minimum edges from A to its old neighbors L={1,4,5,20}, and adds four edges to B's old neighbors H={7,11,13,17}. At this cut B's own extensive-grade record is unchanged. The original removal-only routine chooses B withdrawing U. The new alternative is B retaining T, the **same address** just withdrawn by A; the source leaf has other still-active holders, so the signed operation is legal and its inverse is verified by the original v16.26 verifier. This is direct acquisition, not a rule converting a geometry score to a record.

For every third chart i the two alternatives produce **identical work rows**. Indeed the change at B against i from acquiring T is q(1−2h_T(i)), q=4. The change at B from withdrawing U is −q(1−2h_U(i)); complementarity gives equality. Their only full pair-work difference is A–B: 1998 upon acquisition versus 1990 on the old withdrawal route. The entire final work matrix for direct acquisition is exactly the initial matrix with the chart labels A and B exchanged. On both routes the A–B edge is strictly above the global minimum 1984. A and B have no common minimum neighbor, so that pair enters none of the six opposite-pair comparisons at any chart. Thus every minimum edge, every opposite-pair choice, the derived directions, cycles and baseline coincide. ## Completeness of the one-step choice
The next editor affects only its own work row in addition to the earlier A row. Some initial minimum edge avoids both indices, since every vertex of the initial minimum graph has degree four. The final minimum is therefore at most 1984. If it were lower, every final minimum edge would touch one of the two edited indices; any outside chart could then have degree at most two. Hence any admissible endpoint has minimum 1984.

After A's first edit, the four L charts have degree three, the four H charts have degree five, and all others have degree four. Any second editor outside H must already be a minimum neighbor of every H chart to lower each degree by one. Their common-neighbor set is exactly {A,B}. An editor in H cannot repair the other H vertices because they are mutually nonadjacent at the minimum. For either A or B, the second edit must decrease its work by 4 to all L (each at 1988) and increase work to all H (each at 1984). The only addresses with subtree grade 2 in this fixture are T, U, and branch 3. T is absent from A and B, has all L among its holders and no H, and its parent is retained, so adding it at either A (undo) or B (acquire) works. U is absent A and held by B, has all H and no L, so its only successful edit is the known B withdrawal. Branch 3 also favors H, but only A of the possible editors retains it, and A still holds its child, prohibiting withdrawal. No zero grade or tied lower minimum creates another candidate. All three candidates pass the actual full carrier check; null and the B acquisition without the source withdrawal both fail degree four.

Thus *geometry restoration does not force acquisition*, even though it uniquely determines the acquiring action if the non-source recovery is an addition. The fixed same-union relation and each view's total P1 conservation allow both. Restoring the incidence count or preserving all of B's prior holdings would distinguish the routes, but neither is an inherited event law here.
