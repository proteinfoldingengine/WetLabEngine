# The same chart-12 cut records which of two edits came second

On the archived 25-chart tree, two histories start and end at exactly the same indexed state. Chart 12 makes the same successful deletion in both. It has the same complete P1 data and the same 24 incident pair-work readings at every step. The existing v16.26 operation nevertheless returns a different cover increment on that deletion, and the existing v16.27 event map retains it under the chart-12 event key.

| Order of the two decisive edits | Minimum charts covering the Pin's children at the chart-12 edit | Archived value at event (12,163) |
|---|---|---:|
| Chart 12 drops 163 before chart 0 drops 1354 | 3 → 3 | 0 |
| Chart 0 drops 1354 before chart 12 drops 163 | 3 → 4 | 1 |

The unit increment belongs to whichever of the two edits occurs second. All 120 orders of the five legal edits obey this rule. The finite count does not impose probabilities or a clock. This is an author-verified result on the native deletion and event-attribution routines. The recorded key is in the path data held by the caller. Receiver-owned storage and an autonomous call remain open.

## Which actual writes still preserve the original work

There is a second, one-sided result **before** the probe. After history A, no nonempty deletion by chart 12 alone can restore all work values, even when any number of its leaves can be removed. After history B, the only such sets are {718,1301} and {718,1302}. Either order is legal. The two replayed pairs each raise chart 12's nominal P1 root to 244 and restore the original admitted carrier exactly. After B, absence of 718 from the receiver is permanent. After A its last copy prevents this addressed outcome on every ordinary deletion continuation.

To check all lengths, sum chart 12's work changes against charts 0, 7, 11, 13 and 17. Every available single-site term is strictly positive in A. In B the only nonpositive term is −3 from 718. Every positive term eligible to cancel it has unit grade, contributes at least 1 to that five-partner sum and has at least 23 holders. Summing over all 24 partners instead, 718 contributes −22 while each such candidate contributes at least 20. Thus exactly one of them must accompany 718. Checking the individual signs leaves only 1301 and 1302. Because every nonroot site in chart 12 is a true root-child tip, the signs add for every subset. A successful unit cut at chart 12 without the work condition can happen in both arms.

## Controls and reproduction

For a single positive-grade source cut, the work jump on each incident source pair has magnitude equal to the squared transferred grade and sign equal to the partner's membership sign. Two such cuts with identical full endpoint work therefore have identical grades and holder sets. All 1,305 legal single source cuts in this preparation are original root-child tips. Their 618 work classes contain 1,702 unordered matched pairs. Each such pair is related by a leaf swap preserving the prepared state. A name-independent probe, or one fixed at a third unchanged site, cannot separate such swapped histories in this comparison. The two-source-edit construction bypasses that symmetry with the original grades.
