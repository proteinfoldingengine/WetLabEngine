# A third chart can identify a remote withdrawal from its own next change

Two charts can legally withdraw the same tip from the same initial star. A third chart takes neither action. Its retained row, its P1 grades and its legal single-tip commands are identical immediately after either event. Under the original four-child normalization it can nevertheless distinguish the events when its own tip count first leaves three. In 288 of the 720 studied event pairs, this remains true after every allowed interval of ordinary pruning, without an announcement that normalization has started. In the other 432, a legal common delay can produce identical *complete* third-chart histories. The later invocation of the original writer is supplied in both arms.

## The comparison

Use the original flat four-tip R campaign with three labeled charts. The Pin is retained by every chart. A chart's degree here counts its retained tips. Write h for the fewest charts that cover the four tips together. The new population includes every legal v16.26 source withdrawal A at s that raises h from 2 to 3. Before that step s has precisely two holders, A and P. After it, P has its first private tip, while A and the third chart C already had private tips. This follows because all three charts are necessary exactly when each has a private tip, and only s changes holders. It also follows that each non-source chart initially holds one or two tips.

We tested three preparations from the same starting atlas. In the **raising release** A drops s. In the **competing release** P drops s. In the **unchanged control** A retains s until normalization begins. The two active releases are ordinary legal inputs. They require no intervention-specific restriction on subsequent ordinary edits. The unchanged control excludes that precise A-at-s withdrawal during its preparation. After either active release s is singly held, and cannot be withdrawn under further ordinary pruning.

The competing release has cover 2, since A and C still cover everything and have private tips. After the two active releases every ordinary deletion history has a corresponding history in the other arm with exactly the same chart and tip commands. The only differing incidence is which of A and P holds s, and neither can legally withdraw its remaining copy. All other sites evolve under identical rules. In particular C's record along those matched ordinary histories is identical. The global source certificate can contain different cover information.

## The first local exit from three

In the cover-2 input branch the unmodified v16.53 four-child routine first fills every missing incidence. C grows to three tips and its next own edit gives it all four. In the cover-3 branch the same code operates on complementary memberships. The first pass keeps the earliest missing tip for each chart and fills all its other missing tips. This takes C to three. The cover must stay within one of the target 3, so no chart can hold all four. The next C edit must therefore take it down to two. That edit occurs before the end because the original cover-3 compact endpoint assigns at most two tips to each chart.

For an immediate input of degree b, the first exit is C's (4−b)th own edit. The same calculation works for either non-source chart in the raising-versus-unchanged comparison. No global edit count or chart/tip priority is needed to use the first exit. For an illustrative delayed case, take A={1,3,4}, P={4} and C={2,3}. The two charts can each release tip 4, and the overlap at tip 3 can be thinned independently before normalization. At the first exit, C reads 3→2 for A's release and 3→4 for P's.

This boundary is exact for immediate launch when the priority is unknown. For the first 3−b local changes, both branches can show any ordered selection of 3−b distinct originally missing tips. In the low branch put the omitted tip last in the child order. In the high branch put it first so that it is temporarily kept as the sole missing incidence. Each earlier addressed prefix, with the same fixed fine grades, thus occurs under both hypotheses. The two hypotheses in this lower-bound argument can have different undisclosed priorities. If the physical child priority is supplied, their first added tip already differs under the same priority.

## Which distinctions survive an arbitrary ordinary delay

**Criterion.** Let P be the newly indispensable holder in the raising release. If P initially has only s apart from the Pin, then every later entry after ordinary withdrawals still reads cover 3 in the raising arm and cover 2 in each control. In the raising arm every chart keeps at least one private tip. After the competing release P is empty. In the unchanged control P either holds only s, also present on A, or has become empty. In both controls A and C keep their private tips and their union covers everything. The control retaining A at s is needed only for the unchanged-arm assertion.

During the ordinary interval all chart degrees can only decrease. C starts at degree at most two, so its first exit from degree three cannot occur during that interval. Whenever the original caller is eventually invoked, the same local transition identifies the actor even for different delays or different priorities in the two arms. Neither receiver-visible launch nor global termination is needed. Progress into the normalization procedure and access to C's own successive edits remain premises.

**Converse.** If P also holds tip t, let every other holder of t withdraw from t while P keeps it. Apply the same sequence in both active arms. Both entries now have cover 3 and C has exactly the same local row and history. C already owns a private tip u outside s and t. With u before s in child order, A and P each have a common earlier missing tip in the two entries. The first-missing-tip choices of the complementary pass agree. Only edits at A or P differ while the two copies of s are reconciled. C's ordered edits agree during this pass, the surviving complementary assignments agree afterward, and the remaining path has the same C record and endpoint. One common admissible priority is enough to defeat a uniform attribution claim. The exact program replay gave equal entire C histories on **all 144** common child/chart priorities for this constructed delay in every failing case.

In the unchanged control the same t-clearing delay also yields cover 3. The two entries differ only on A at s. Prioritizing t before s makes A's first missing-tip choice identical. Here even the joint ordered sequences of all non-source edits coincide. The replay found equality on all 144 priorities. These witnesses compare successful local edits and retain their physical tip addresses. They also give identical addressed P1 records for every fixed assignment of ambient grades.

## What information the receiver retains

The native replay included all 1,548 cover-2 and 132 cover-3 three-chart R inputs, with their full paths checked by the independent original verifier. For fixed target, chart position and starting mask, every resulting complete single-chart history is identical, after removing only consecutive repetitions. There are 45 such classes at target 2 and 30 at target 3; the full table is archived. For the overlapping initial masks the first exit separates the two targets. Thus the receiver can identify the source when the alternatives enforce different covers. Within either target its own trace carries no further distinction about remote starting incidences when its prior, physical ordering and grades are held fixed. This exact finite classification is for one chart, not a claim about joint event timing.

We also tested the earlier hidden-grade intervention. Two tips absent from the receiver initially have identical holders, are distinct from s, and carry grades 1 and 3 in opposite assignments. All other fine grades are 1. The receiver prior and all pair works match between grade alternatives within each event arm. Without selecting on the outcome, there are 216 immediate raising-versus-unchanged receiver/hidden-pair comparisons. In 72 the receiver is the independent third chart C in the competing-release experiment. At degree three it necessarily holds at least one hidden tip, so its addressed P1 distinguishes the grade assignments. Its next degree (2 or 4) identifies the event. The sets of two successive addressed outputs are disjoint across all four event/grade combinations, even when different priorities are compared. A single record at the decision is weaker. In every comparison, 28 of the 144 raising-arm priority combinations have erased the hidden distinction at that point. Retaining or observing both local records is an additional access assumption.

For example A={1,2,3}, P={3}, C={4}. In default order C's raising-arm masks through its decision are 17,21,29,25. Its competing-arm masks are 17,19,23,31. The Pin occupies bit zero. With hidden tips 1 and 2, mask 29 includes tip 2 and mask 25 includes neither. This is a concrete case where a single late grade reading loses information present one local edit earlier.
