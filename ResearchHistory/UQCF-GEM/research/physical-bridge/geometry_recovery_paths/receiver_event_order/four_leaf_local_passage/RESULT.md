# A guaranteed local distinction after a source withdrawal

In the historical v16.53 four-leaf R routine, two legal withdrawals at the same chart can leave every other chart's prior identical and still force **every other chart** to experience a locally distinguishable edit. At its first deletion during normalization, a chart has **three leaves left** if the first withdrawal left a two-chart cover, and **two leaves left** if it takes three charts to cover the leaves. Both arms guarantee a first gain and a first deletion. The chart needs its own holdings and the occurrence of its own edit. It can distinguish the arms with unknown and even independently chosen chart and leaf orders, without polling other charts or knowing the global step.

This applies to a flat rooted star with four leaves, all jointly covered, under the stated routine. The source event changes which branch of the existing code runs. The earlier five-chart v16.43 study held cover size fixed and observed a first gain only on some orders. Here we have changed both the routine and the condition on the source events. The statements concern separate input families.

## How the operation makes the distinction

Write q for the fewest charts whose union contains all four leaves. In producer.produce, the R branch obtains the four supports from its case, encodes the rows, and calls coordinate(start,layout(tree)[0]) to compute q. It calls normalize on that state and computed profile. Although the lower-level normalize interface accepts a target profile, the R caller calculates it from the incidences. The four children are leaves, so recursive child calls make no edits. The program stores the whole path. The R campaign iterates cases; it does not choose which withdrawal to initiate or itself run upon a withdrawal. We apply the original v16.26 atomic withdrawal, then evaluate the post-cut state via the original R computations. Two resulting paths were also found verbatim in the old v16.53 certificate.

* For q=2, normalize_four expands all four supports to every chart before deleting anything at any chart. If a receiver starts with d leaves, its first 4−d edits are gains, ending with four leaves. The endpoint has cover size two, so no chart can end with all four. Its first deletion necessarily reduces its holdings from four to three.
* For q=3, normalize_four calls cover.reconfigure on the four complements. A chart appears in one complement bin per missing leaf. Thinning keeps its first missing leaf in bin order and removes its remaining memberships from those bins, each removal adding a leaf at the receiver. After exactly 3−d such gains the chart holds three leaves. The complementary-cover primitive keeps every chart in at least one bin, hence no chart can hold all four. Any globally covered state that ends at q=3 has at most two leaves per chart: if a chart held three, any holder of its missing leaf would complete a cover of two. Consequently the next local edit must occur and must be a loss from three to two. This argument uses the code's thinning and coverage constraint and a return to q=3; the identity of the canonical endpoint adds nothing to the conclusion. The stock path terminates: after thinning, each relocation loop places a misplaced label in its goal bin; a displaced label at a full goal was itself misplaced, and cannot dislodge an already-correct owner. Remaining additions expand directly to the target complement.

In a paired q=2/3 comparison every unchanged receiver necessarily has d≤2. At local edit number 4−d, the q=2 receiver gains and holds four; the q=3 receiver loses and holds two. This is the earliest local edit ordinal that separates the two arms for every hidden order using even addressed local records. Before it, both have made only 3−d gains. Any ordered list of 3−d distinct initially missing leaves is possible in either arm: put those first in the expansion arm and put the one remaining missing leaf first, followed by the list, in the complement arm. The physical leaf identities, receiver prior and source cuts stay fixed. This lower bound on an ordinal allows the orders to vary between arms. With the actual priority supplied one can distinguish earlier by address. The first-loss test avoids using either an ordinal or a supplied leaf priority and is guaranteed in both arms.

The geometric grade prep is a common Pin of grade one with four leaves of grade two. The original v16.23 projection puts 9−2d units at a d-leaf chart's local Pin. Immediately after the first loss the Pin is therefore 3 versus 5. The edited leaf has grade two in both cases. The information resides in what else the receiver holds.

## A six-chart example with no locally visible work difference at the cut

| Chart | Initial leaves |
| --- | --- |
| A | 1, 2, 3 |
| X and Y (one row each) | 1 |
| Z | 2 |
| C | 2, 4 |
| B | 3, 4 |

A is the only three-leaf chart. B is the only chart holding both leaves with two initial holders; every other leaf initially has three holders. These roles survive arbitrary chart and leaf renaming. A may withdraw leaf 2 or leaf 1. Both leave every leaf with at least two holders and give the same entire local state at B. B held neither source leaf. Even its entire incident pair-work row computed from the inherited squared cut weights agrees across the two post-cut states: (8,12,12,12,8,0) in the displayed chart order. The original work row was (12,12,12,12,8,0). The full network's post-cut work differs here. In fact, for two distinct equally graded leaves initially held by A, equality of the entire work matrix after either withdrawal would force every other chart to hold both leaves or neither: its work change against A is +4 for a shared leaf and −4 for an unshared one. The original columns would then be identical, and the two cuts would have equal q by exchanging leaf names.

After loss of leaf 2 at A, A and C still cover the leaves. After loss of leaf 1, only X and Y hold it, and neither holds anything else. Covering leaves 2, 3 and 4 takes two additional charts. The native atomic calls report a zero versus unit cover increment and false versus true trigger. Here the historical normalization program computes the resulting q; the v16.26 Boolean itself is never passed to the normalizer.

B starts with two leaves. In every ordering its first edit gains a leaf. Its second edit is another gain after A withdrew leaf 2 or a loss after A withdrew leaf 1. The first-loss records are distinguishable even when the very same initial receiver leaf is lost. For example, with chart order as tabulated and leaf order (3,1,2,4), B first loses leaf 3 in both arms. Its holdings immediately afterwards are {1,2,4} versus {2,4}. Across all 6! × 4! = 17,280 order pairs its first deleted address agrees in 7,680. Across the five remote charts, all 86,400 paired receiver comparisons have the guaranteed first loss; 26,160 match the address. These are enumeration counts, not probabilities. In the independent-order comparison every receiver's scalar outcomes stay disjoint.

## Exact boundary for this program

For two distinct, individually union-preserving withdrawals at a single source chart in the same prepared star, q is the complete criterion for a guaranteed distinction at hidden priority on remote charts:

* If either post-cut q is one, a full chart other than the cut chart must exist and survives both cuts. If either is four, all charts after that cut hold at most one leaf (a chart holding two would allow a cover of at most three). The source previously held exactly two, and the other cut also leaves it with exactly one. Both arms then still have q=4. Hence unequal q can only mean the pair 2 and 3.
* For common q=1 or q=2, expansion is identical at every unchanged chart. Both global states coincide when full, and all subsequent moves are identical at a common priority.
* For common q=3, choose any leaf already absent at the source and put it first. Such a leaf exists, since a post-cut q=3 state could not contain a chart holding three leaves. The source now keeps the same first hole in the complementary thinning in both arms. Every unaffected chart performs the same local edits. The full one-hole states coincide, so the subsequent routines coincide.
* For common q=4 put the source last among charts. Each withdrawn leaf has a different holder from the source by legality. The earliest holder of every leaf therefore agrees between arms. The disjoint case of the stock normalizer drops surplus holders on each leaf before transporting roles; unchanged charts have identical local deletion words and the global states join once leaves are singly held.

Thus for equal q there is a single common priority at which **all remote addressed local histories** coincide, with idle repetitions removed. For unequal q, **each** remote chart can tell the source arms apart at a guaranteed local transition even if their priorities differ. Different q exposes a property of the full incidence structure through actual receiver edits. It does not generally identify a unique withdrawal among a larger menu of possible source events. A standing comparison of two specified source alternatives was supplied.

## Evidence and limits

The historical R domain has 2,507 states: all admitted k=1,2,3 rows and 24 singleton-permutation cases at k=4. The original compressed certificate at e61d5b0e827f7b412824ad46021e2ad6cc6f7875 is pinned by Git blob 9dd2e4445bca36dfe59f10fc7b7debd1ee2bc81e. The independent set-cover readback checks all unordered pairs of legal source withdrawals at a chart. Of 8,046 pairs, 288 give q=2/3 and all 576 remote comparisons pass the signature. The other 7,758 pairs admit a common erasing order with exact equality of the archived remote words. Those controls comprise 2,280 cases at q=1, 5,406 at q=2 and 72 at q=3. A five-chart direct q=4 control fills the missing archival case. The identity-order original paths for the selected historical three-chart witness agree state for state with the archived records. Its full 144-order direct replay also passes (288 remote comparisons). Search masks, ordered traces and addresses in the JSON files are zero-based except the explicit source addresses noncritical/critical, which use one-based leaf numbers.

The six-chart example is a new call on the same original kernel, outside the historical R enumeration. Its bounded search used one distinguished source, nonempty remote rows of degree at most two, and at least two holders per leaf after either cut. Only the 4-, 5- and 6-chart search cells were run before selection. The primitive programs use only incidences to choose edits; the grade readback is calculated on the actual local states.

The guaranteed local records follow from an inherited operation once called. The computation still starts from a supplied four-leaf tree, prepared holdings and chosen source event. Neither automatic dispatch after the withdrawal, physical access to the records, nor an initial network generated by the Pin is established.
