## Why the roles follow

Let μ be the initial positive minimum. Only the two edited rows i,j can change. Both admitted degree-four graphs have an edge avoiding i,j, so their minima agree. Every old edge from either edited chart to an untouched chart changes its value by precisely the nonzero squared subtree grade of its own toggle; none can stay on the minimum. The untouched chart's degree can only be restored by exchanging its connection between i and j. In particular the two edited charts cannot share any untouched minimum neighbor.

If i and j initially neighbor one another, their mutual edge has to remain; each has three old external neighbors and must trade them for the other's three. For each of i's three other neighbors k the earlier pair work W(j,k) must equal μ plus the second edit's squared grade, since all three must decrease to μ under that single row change. The two disjoint maximizing pairs in the old neighborhood of i include one pairing j with an old k, so equality of all three such pair works forces three maxima instead of exactly two. That contradicts the original opposite-pair rule. This adapts the earlier two-withdrawal argument without assuming the second edit's sign.

Hence the charts are nonadjacent with disjoint neighbor sets. Every old neighbor transfers to the other actor. Consequently all four entries of W(i,N(j))−μ must have the same positive squared grade of the edit at i, and all four entries of W(j,N(i))−μ must give the grade square at j. This exact screen on the pinned 25-chart table has 150 unordered distant pairs. Exactly 28 have both rows constant. Twenty-seven require 8 in both rows and therefore cannot arise from an integer subtree grade in this preparation. Only {0,12} gives (4,4). That pair and the size of both edits are now consequences, not supplied. The result does not extend the 8 exclusion to arbitrary real-grade preparations.

Branch 3 has the wrong membership for a gain by B and cannot be removed at A because A holds its child. Match signs to the required work: an outgoing deletion has to be held by all own old neighbors and none of the other's, while an addition has the opposite required support. Therefore A may only withdraw T or add U; B may only withdraw U or add T. All four choices of the two edits, in either actor order, are union-legal and admitted at return by the original builder.
