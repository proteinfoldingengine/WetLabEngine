# A source cut can force a particular chart to empty while another cut protects a leaf there

Two unit cuts at the same source have the same counting-root gain, the same cover increase from one to two and the same positive v16.26 trigger. They change **which two charts cover the leaves**. On the common preparation below, maximal deletion that preserves this new cover number must empty chart 3 after the first cut and leave it nonempty after the second. The existing v16.43 star writer realizes both outcomes before its first addition. In its original chart order both arms actually delete at chart 3. On the second arm the writer then reads its surviving leaf from that same chart and copies it onward.

This is an implication about the finite native deletion rule under a stated continuation condition, and an actual execution of an inherited software operation. With unrestricted deletions the two arms can join before a receiver edit. The earlier v16.53 all-chart expansion is unnecessary for the local distinction and erases it at its own canonical endpoint.

## Why the destination follows from incidence

Let each leaf of a finite rooted star have a nonempty set of chart holders. Each chart retains the root. A legal native deletion removes one leaf membership while preserving the union. Write h for the minimum number of charts whose combined holdings include every leaf. The original v16.26 atom computes h on both sides of its edit. Call a deletion sequence maximal at fixed cover if it preserves its initial h=q at each step and ends when no further such deletion is possible.

**Terminal assignment theorem.** Its endpoints are exactly the assignments of each leaf to one of its initial holders for which precisely q charts get a leaf.

To prove necessity, deletion only shrinks holdings, so h cannot decrease. At any state with a duplicated leaf, choose a minimum covering set H. Keep one of its holders in H and remove another holder of the same leaf. The old H still covers and the union survives. Thus an additional deletion preserving h is possible until every leaf has one holder. At a singleton assignment every nonempty chart is necessary. There are consequently q nonempty charts. Conversely, fix any such assignment using q original holders in total. Delete every other incidence in any order. The q target charts cover throughout, and the initial lower bound on h persists. The final singleton state admits no further union-preserving deletion. Without fixing h, the same argument without the size restriction gives *all* single-holder assignments as endpoints.

For a given minimum covering set H, each of its members has a leaf that none of the other members holds. Otherwise that member could be dropped from H. Assign each leaf to any of its holders in H. These private witnesses ensure all of H is used. Hence chart membership in all, none or only some initial minimum covers determines, respectively, forced survival, forced emptiness or both possibilities at the end of maximal fixed-cover pruning. This proves the result for any finite star with leaves. It does not require a chart ordering, a preferred leaf assignment, a time coordinate or a scalar optimization over paths.
