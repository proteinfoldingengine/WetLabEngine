# Five-edit lower bound for recovery avoiding view 12

For this 25-view preparation, recovery after the source deletion while leaving view 12 untouched takes at least **five further deletions**. The archived 100-edit witness gives the upper bound. This is an analytical extension beyond the depth-two computation. It neither runs a deeper enumeration nor assumes that recovery must occur.

## Objects and premises

**SUPPLIED.** Use the [frozen preparation](https://github.com/proteinfoldingengine/WetLabEngine/blob/e61d5b0e827f7b412824ad46021e2ad6cc6f7875/ResearchHistory/UQCF-GEM/research/physical-bridge/forced_parent_deletion/check_geometry_repair.py) on the periodic 5-by-5 grid. Let $A=0$, $B=12$, $D=N(A)=\{1,4,5,20\}$ and $E=N(B)=\{7,11,13,17\}$. The source deletes $T$ from $A$. A further editor is a view making at least one deletion after this event. Assume throughout the lower-bound argument that $B$ makes none. Recovery means the original carrier admits the resulting snapshot. Exclusion at the four-regular minimum graph suffices.

**INHERITED.** The v16.26 atom removes one nonroot leaf from one view while preserving the union. Write $s_v$ for the integer ambient subtree subtotal, which stays fixed. The work is $W_{ij}=\sum_{v\in Y_i\triangle Y_j}s_v^2$. Against an untouched view $j$, a deletion of $v$ in $i$ contributes $+s_v^2$ if $j$ holds it and $-s_v^2$ otherwise.

**ACCEPTED-CONDITIONAL.** The [two-edit proof](https://github.com/proteinfoldingengine/WetLabEngine/blob/b7689e9a73c36d795b324b347dd3639d9dd83a0e/ResearchHistory/UQCF-GEM/research/physical-bridge/geometry_recovery_paths/DEPTH_TWO_PROOF.md) and the [source-only and delayed paths](https://github.com/proteinfoldingengine/WetLabEngine/blob/e61d5b0e827f7b412824ad46021e2ad6cc6f7875/ResearchHistory/UQCF-GEM/research/physical-bridge/forced_parent_deletion/GEOMETRY_DELAY.md) apply. With at most twelve views changed since the initial preparation, a four-regular endpoint has minimum $\mu=1984$. Indeed, at each of the two four-regular endpoints, some one of its 50 minimum edges avoids all changed views. The two inequalities force equality. At the post-source state $S_1$, all works are at least $\mu$, the labels in $D$ have degree three and those in $E$ have degree five. The $A,B$ work entries against any third view coincide. The positive excesses belong to $\{4,8,10,12,20,24\}$. A single further editor avoiding $B$ cannot recover at any depth. These statements cover the starting boundary and the one-editor case.

## What a view edited once can still touch at the minimum

Call a label untouched here if it makes no further deletion after the source. At $S_1$ no label of $E$ has work excess four against any label. Against $A,B$ its excess is zero. For other endpoints the fixture's only route to excess four is a straight two-step pair whose common center has $A$ or $B$ as a perpendicular neighbor. Such a center lies in $D$ or $E$. There are no grid edges between $D$ and $E$ since the grid distance from $A$ to $B$ is four, and $E$ is independent. Thus an endpoint in $E$ cannot participate in such a two-step pair.

A view edited once can tie an untouched label at $\mu$ only by decreasing its work from $\mu+s_v^2$. The only positive square in the excess list is four. There are exactly three subtotal-two features $T,U,R$ (the child of $R$ supplies one unit of its subtotal). $A$ cannot delete any of these after the source event. Its exclusive child protects $R$. Views in $E$ have no excess-four entries. Any remaining editor apart from $B$ can use only $T$ among these three. Its deletion decreases work only against labels lacking $T$ in $S_1$, namely $A,B$, and $E$. This leaves only $A$ and $B$ as possible untouched minimum neighbors, and the deleting view must lie in $D$ to have excess four against them: the equal $A,B$ rows have that excess precisely on $D$. Consequently every once-edited view apart from $B$ has at most two untouched minimum neighbors. If it has any, it belongs to $D$.

## Cases with a view edited once

With two further editors, an editor acting once can have at most three neighbors at the minimum: at most two untouched and the other editor. With three or four distinct editors each acting once, each needs at least one untouched neighbor, so all belong to $D$. Every old minimum edge at a label of $E$ survives since its five neighbors at $S_1$ are $A$, $B$, and three ordinary grid neighbors, all outside $D$. Recovery fails.

The other case with a single-edit view through four deletions has three further editors of multiplicities $(2,1,1)$. Each of the two single-edit views needs both $A$ and $B$ as untouched neighbors and belongs to $D$. The double editor therefore differs from $A,B$. Only this double editor can remedy any surplus at $E$. An editor in $E$ can affect its own surplus but no other old surplus edge, as $E$ is independent. Any other ordinary editor can neighbor at most two members of $E$ in the grid. Hence some label of $E$ keeps all five old minimum edges.

## Two editors making two deletions each

An ordinary view outside $A,B,E$ can delete substrate leaves and possibly $T$. Each substrate leaf is absent from at most two labels. At untouched labels in $D$, deleting $T$ contributes $+4$. Two such edits at the ordinary view can create new minimum edges to at most two untouched members of $D$. For unequal squares a negative net change requires absence of the larger feature; a dominant $T$ cannot decrease work against $D$, while a dominant substrate is absent at at most two labels. For equal squares, both features must be absent to achieve a decrease. The substrate has no subtotal-two leaves and one view cannot delete $T$ twice, so the equal pair consists of substrate leaves.

**If $A$ edits**, its two signed positive squares cannot sum to $-4$ against an untouched member of $D$. Thus it cannot supply a new edge to such a member. At least three members of $D$ are untouched and each needs a new edge. The other editor must therefore be in $E$ by the preceding bound. All four members of $D$ are then untouched. Two substrate deletions cannot create all four edges. With one substrate and one of $U,R$, some member of $D$ holds the substrate. At that member the change is $s^2-4$, which must be negative to create a new edge. This forces $s=1$, giving an odd change between two even work levels. The only remaining pair is $U,R$. Each of the three ordinary old grid neighbors of this editor (excluding $B$) lacks both features and stays untouched. Their old work drops from $\mu$ to $\mu-8$, contradicting the required minimum.

**If $A$ stays fixed**, each surplus in $E$ must itself edit or lose an old minimum edge to one of the two editors. Each editor can cover at most two surpluses, and an editor in $E$ covers at most one. Both editors must thus be ordinary grid neighbors of two distinct members of $E$. Opposite neighbors of $B$ have only $B$ in common; perpendicular neighbors share $B$ and one diagonal grid neighbor of $B$. Both editors are therefore diagonal neighbors of $B$, outside $D$ and $E$. All four labels in $D$ are untouched. Each needs a genuinely new minimum edge. By the two-deletion bound above, each editor must supply two such edges.

For an editor with unequal squares the larger feature must then be a substrate leaf absent at both of its two newly reached labels. Outside that cut the net change to every untouched label is positive. With equal squares the two substrate cuts must both contain the same two newly reached labels, hence both cuts are that exact pair. Again work increases against every other untouched label. In either case the editor has at most two untouched minimum neighbors, plus at most the other editor. It cannot have degree four.

The cases exhaust the partitions of one through four deletions. The resulting shortest-depth interval avoiding $B$ is **[5,100]**. Five is a lower bound; an attaining five-deletion path remains an open question.

## Parity of retained grade at an admitted geometry

There is a separate necessary condition for every common-tree integer-grade preparation with an odd number of views admitted by this carrier. In a view $i$ define $M_i=\sum_{v\in Y_i\setminus\{0\}}s_v$. This is the P1 retained-grade depth moment: each ambient unit contributes once per retained nonroot ancestor on its path. As $s_v^2\equiv s_v\pmod 2$, symmetric-difference work satisfies $W_{ij}\equiv M_i+M_j\pmod 2$. An odd minimum would put every minimum edge between the two parity classes. A positive regular bipartite graph has equally many vertices on each side by summing degrees, so its total order would be even. Hence the minimum is even. The original v15.43 carrier checks all minimum edges, degree four and connectedness. An even minimum forces parity agreement along every edge and hence across all views.

Between two such admitted endpoints each view loses the same parity of total subtree subtotal through its deletions. If a source event has odd subtotal and the source makes no further odd-subtotal deletion, every other view must lose an odd total before recovery. If any other view stays fixed instead, the source must undergo an additional odd-subtotal deletion. These are conditional constraints on retained grade. The Q536 source has subtotal two, so it does not activate the odd-source implication. On the published 100-step all-unit witness, 19 editors act four times and four editors act six times, consistent with the two fixed views. This arithmetic reads the existing witness.

The update law that would require geometric recovery, select a continuation and make the retained consequence physically accessible remains open. Pruning count measures ordered combinatorial edits.
