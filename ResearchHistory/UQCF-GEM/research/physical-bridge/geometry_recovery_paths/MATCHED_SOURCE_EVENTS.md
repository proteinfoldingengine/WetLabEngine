# Two actual source events leave different records in one initially blank chart

On the same four-leaf atlas, deleting source membership at leaf 3 or at leaf 4 produces two completed, independently checkable receiver histories under the existing v16.53 R normalization. Both source edits move one unit of counting grade to the same source root. The initially blank chart acquires **two leaves after the first event and three after the second**. Both writes begin at the first normalization edit. The endpoint distinction survives forgetting leaf addresses. The software reads the post-cut incidence pattern and computes a global cover number. Its ordered palette determines which chart gets the distinguishing count.

This answers the comparison requested in [the agent thread](https://github.com/proteinfoldingengine/WetLabEngine/pull/111#issuecomment-7133614140). The 25-chart geometry fixture cannot give two completed chart-12 writes by varying just the first chart-0 deletion. The positive result uses a separate, previously implemented four-chart caller. The two source alternatives within each comparison always use one identical starting atlas.

## What the historical geometry allows

**INHERITED.** On the archived 25-chart atlas a pair's work is the sum of squared subtree grades over differing memberships. The minimum work is 1,984 and its graph has degree four. A source deletion at chart 0 edits only its incident work distances. If another chart edits next, only its own work distances can change. There is always an old minimum edge away from both editors. If the final minimum drops, all edges at that lower value touch at least one of the two editors. Every other vertex then has degree at most two. The degree-four test therefore requires final minimum 1,984.

For any legal source other than leaf 1, its holders omit at most two charts. Distances to holders increase strictly. Thus at most two neighbors of chart 0 can tie 1,984 before the second edit. An edit at a different chart can change just one of chart 0's distances. Even allowing its best possible change, chart 0 cannot acquire four neighbors. On the archived integer grades the sharper observed tie count is zero for each of the 1,304 ordinary source choices. The original work gaps from chart 0 are 0, 4, 8, 14 and 20. The only positive square in that set is 4 and ordinary source leaves have grade other than two. The exceptional depth-two parent cannot be deleted at chart 0 while it retains its child; its private child is also ineligible.

Both enumerations recover the same 1,305 legal source identities. On the original leaf-1 input the historical caller verifies the sole next geometry write (12,2) among 32,575 raw legal commands. Supplying ordinary leaf 662 at the same source-argument boundary, leaving the fixture fixed, executes the verified source event and returns zero geometry candidates; its singleton guard stops before a receiver write. Other ordinary sources are excluded by the proof. No second full run is inferred from a failing guard. This result concerns a next edit at a different chart and makes no classification of second edits at chart 0.

## What reaches the four-chart writer

**INHERITED.** The v16.26 atomic primitive deletes one retained incidence if the chart keeps its root, the site is locally terminal, and the union survives. Its independent verifier checks the complete before and after. The v16.53 R branch takes a supplied specimen of child memberships, encodes chart views, computes the minimum number of charts covering all children, computes a canonical endpoint and executes its normalizer. No receiver address or numerical target is passed with the specimen. An independent v16.53 path verifier checks every transition is a legal single incidence, the profile excursion is at most one and the final endpoint is exact.

**SUPPLIED.** The primitive occurrence, the common preparation, calls to the controller, the lossless conversion from the primitive's returned membership lists to R specimen rows, the four ordered labels, and comparison of chart 0 are experimental inputs. The R branch is historical software. These four-label inputs extend its archived sample iterator, which covered all supports only through three labels and used singleton permutations at four. Neither witness input is an archived campaign row. The experiment changes only the specimen iterator inside the historical producer. Call instrumentation forwards the original arguments. A separate stale-target control is deliberately interventionist.

## Exact criterion and minimality within this star model

Let c be the specified source, let the receiver start blank, and allow two other charts a and d. A legal source deletion can only preserve the minimum cover size h or raise it by one. An old minimum cover that ceases to work can be augmented by another holder of the deleted leaf. If h=1 and the source is the only full chart, every eligible deletion breaks the singleton cover. If another chart is full, every deletion preserves its cover. If h=3, the three potentially nonblank charts still cover the whole tree after every legal deletion. Thus distinct target sizes from two cuts at c require initial h=2.

A strict cut must destroy every old two-chart cover. Any cover omitting c survives. If both {a,c} and {c,d} covered before, destroying both would require cutting c from a leaf held by neither a nor d, violating union preservation. Hence there is exactly one old minimum pair, say {a,c}. Failure of {a,d} requires a child private to c, and failure of {c,d} requires a child private to a. The strict deletable child must be held by c and d but exclude a; the flat deletable child must be held by a and c and may also be held by d. The four roles require four distinct children. Conversely those four children suffice. With at most two potentially nonblank charts the singleton-cover argument handles h=1, and at h=2 every allowed cut preserves coverage by the two. Four charts including the blank receiver and four children are minimal in this specified three-other-chart comparison. This proof makes no assertion about other trees, more active labels on smaller carriers, or other update laws.

At exactly four children there are two choices of partner, two choices for the flat child's outside membership and 4! permutations. Each resulting preparation has exactly one unordered pair that separates. For width-one children, canonical endpoints assign one label per child in lexicographic order with exactly q distinct labels. At q=2 the assignments are (0,0,0,1), and at q=3 they are (0,0,1,2). The first chart therefore has three or two leaves respectively.

## Rejecting controls and the next physical question

- Replacing the third child's {2,3} by {1,2} gives two legal cuts with the same source grade transfer and different deleted addresses. Both return the same two-chart target and identical receiver endpoint. The global incidence relation, beyond the deletion label, matters.
- Keeping the original strict arm but forcing the R target to the stale value 2 triggers the inherited exact-parent guard before its first edit. This failed control supplies no receiver record.
- Swapping chart positions 0 and 3 throughout the input preserves both cover numbers and the two sorted endpoint shapes. The originally blank chart now sits in position 3 and ends with zero leaves in both runs. The different completed leaf counts follow position 0. The distinguishable *global shape* survives this relabeling; a special physical claim for the originally blank receiver fails this test.
- The checker rejects missing and duplicate preparations, identity substitutions, a wrong profile, a false chart-0 source identity, an uncovered state, deleting a private leaf, a two-incidence jump, and false attribution of a new specimen to the archived campaign.
