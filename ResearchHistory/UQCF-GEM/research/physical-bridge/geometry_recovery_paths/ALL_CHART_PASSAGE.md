# Two source cuts separate the histories of every chart

From the same four-child star, one source cut makes the existing v16.53 writer put all four leaves in every chart simultaneously. A different, equal-grade cut forbids even a single full chart throughout the execution. The distinction follows the initially blank chart under every relabeling. In the first arm its full mask is a live input to a subsequent local deletion. The complete mask can disappear before the writer returns. This establishes an order-independent property of actual intermediate states and edits in this finite program.

## Preparation and observed objects

**INHERITED.** The accepted [matched-event comparison](MATCHED_SOURCE_EVENTS.md) gives four child supports in leaf order, {1}, {2}, {2,3}, {1,2}. All charts hold the root. Chart 0 is uniquely blank. Deleting leaf 3 or 4 at chart 2 preserves the union and sends one unit of counting grade to the same source root. The starting memberships, fine grades and pair works agree exactly between arms. The complete immediate membership of every other chart is also identical between arms.

**SUPPLIED.** After the verified v16.26 source edit, the experiment converts its returned membership lists losslessly to the historical R specimen and invokes the v16.53 producer. The four-label inputs are valid extensions of its archived specimen iterator. Only that iterator is replaced. Neither receiver nor palette order is passed. The existing producer encodes the post-cut supports, computes the cover number from those masks and drives the default-order normalization to completion. This route uses global incidence. A post-cut P1 aggregate and a physical actuation rule are still absent at this interface.

Let h be the minimum number of charts whose union covers the four leaves and q be h at entry to the writer. A chart is full exactly when it holds all four leaves. The local question is whether that chart itself deletes a leaf while its pre-edit mask is full. This question refers to a primitive in the executed history and uses no numerical chart name, named leaf or synchronized clock.

## Why each chart gets the distinction

After cutting leaf 4, charts 1 and 2 together still cover every leaf, and neither alone does. Thus q=2. The archived four-child branch first adds every missing child membership across the palette, then deletes memberships outside its computed canonical target. All children are terminal, so the recursive calls perform no interior edits. The common full state is actually written. Every chart subsequently leaves that state by exactly one local deletion from a full mask because the q=2 endpoint has leaf counts (3,1,0,0) and the contraction portion only deletes. In the original labeling the state with all masks full occurs at path index 11, counting the post-cut input as index 0. The local departures occur on edits 12, 13, 14 and 21. The first and last of these belong to different charts. Ordered indices carry no time units.

After cutting leaf 3, leaves 1, 2 and 3 each have a different sole holder. Thus q=3. Every admitted state of the writer satisfies |h-q| ≤ 1 by the historical path check. One full chart would give h=1. Every chart therefore stays short of full on this entire arm, even for a different admissible choice of path. The two executed paths complete. Permuting the chart names or the leaves changes neither proof. Individual edit identities, order and terminal holdings may change.

More generally, in this four-leaf R domain with one initially blank chart, legal single cuts leave q in {1,2,3}. For q=1 or 2 the archived expand-and-contract branch puts every chart in the common full state. Each chart whose endpoint is proper then makes exactly one full-mask departure. For q=3 all full states are excluded by the guard. The q=1 endpoint leaves the first palette chart full, so it would be incorrect to claim four departures in every low-cover case.

## Pair work and the numerical P1 projection

Let A_i be the leaves of chart i and let w_v>0 be the squared grade of leaf v. The inherited star work is W_ij = sum of w_v over the symmetric difference of A_i and A_j. The common root contributes zero. Let n_v be the number of charts holding v. Counting unordered separating pairs gives

    sum over i<j of W_ij = sum over leaves v of w_v n_v(4−n_v).

Union preservation gives n_v at least one. Total work is zero exactly when every n_v is four. If there is no full chart there must be at least two partly held leaves. With only one, any holder of that leaf would be full. Each partly held leaf contributes at least three times its weight. Hence in the strict arm total work is always at least 6 min_v w_v. For unit weights the original path attains 6. In the other arm all six pair works vanish simultaneously. This is a degeneracy of the inter-chart work comparison, with coincident chart data. No spacetime metric evolution or physical gravitational field is inferred.

For the supplied unit grade, ancestral aggregation at a chart gives root value 1 plus its number of missing leaves. The checker evaluated the inherited P1 routine on both witness paths and checked the inherited work function on every state. The writer itself makes zero P1 aggregation calls. It reads and edits chart membership masks. Its global cover guard consumes the same live list that was just mutated. A value-equal copy fails the identity check. The path rows returned in the historical record are snapshots.

At every departure the original in-place clear consumes that chart's own complete mask. The four observed edits were separately reconstructed as v16.26 atoms and independently verified. The writer need not branch on the predicate “was full” to consume a full mask. Its guard computes the cover number and checks the permitted excursion after each edit. It stores the trajectory globally. We have not identified a persistent chart-local flag for the occurrence or a local device that reads the P1 value. With the original blank moved to palette slot 3, its endpoint is root-only in both arms despite the sharp difference between its intermediate states. Every chart participates in the low-cover event, so this result does not single out one physical recipient.

## What the geometry actually forces

From the flat arm's same post-cut state (1,19,13,9), add leaves 1, 2 and 3 to chart 0. Then remove leaf 1 at chart 1, leaves 2 and 3 at chart 2, and leaf 3 at chart 3. The end is the historical endpoint (15,17,1,1). All seven edits are primitive, keep h=2 exactly, preserve the union and avoid a full chart. The historical path verifier accepts this path. Seven equals the endpoint Hamming distance. The actual writer takes 23 edits and reaches zero pair work. Its expansion rule forces passage through that state on this input. The cover constraint and the endpoints alone permit the seven-edit bypass. In the strict arm the exclusion of fullness follows from the constraint alone.

## Scope of verification

An incidence-oriented producer called the existing atomic edit and verifier for each legal choice and the actual R writer with its path verifier for each distinct post-cut input. A whole-chart enumeration derived each legal source arm separately and predicted which charts could be full. A second checker reconstructed preparations, pairs, both arms of each relabeling and every requested path identity; it checked moves, endpoints, union, cover bounds, each chart's local departures and geometric zeros using column multiplicities. The finite census gave 2,401 preparations, 4,116 legal arms, 2,646 same-start pairs and 96 pairs separating on this local-history property. It includes empty menus and 2,550 nonseparating pairs. All 24 chart permutations crossed with all 24 leaf permutations of the named witness passed; 2,577 distinct complete writer paths suffice for the census and the relabelings together.

The counts describe this finite domain with the specified source and initial blank. The same-target duplicated-support example has two different cuts and the same local passage in both. Inserting a primitive excursion through full occupancy into the strict path fails its guard. The relabeled blank endpoint refutes terminal persistence. These tests do not assign occurrence probabilities. The seven-edit bypass and the equal-endpoint blank are discriminating controls for those two obligations.
