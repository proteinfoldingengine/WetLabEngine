## One-step classification without a fixed-minimum assumption

The geometry constructor requires four minimum-work neighbours per chart. Let μ=1984, the original minimum. Chart 0's four old neighbours are 1,4,5,20. Chart 12's are 7,11,13,17. Consider a second legal deletion at any editor i.

1. Only edges incident to A or i can change from the initial table. At least 42 old μ edges avoid both. A minimum above μ is impossible. If the new minimum were below μ, each outside chart could meet that level only on edges to A and i, giving degree at most two. Any degree-four endpoint therefore has minimum μ.
2. If i is A, all edges outside A stay fixed. Each other chart's original degree forces its adjacency to A to remain exactly as it was. All four old A neighbours hold T, so their edges gained s² in the first edit. One more A deletion could undo this on all four only if the removed leaf were absent from all four. Every legal remaining A leaf is absent from at most two. Thus A cannot repair the graph.
3. If i differs from A, only the A–i edge can change in A's post-source row. For holders of T that row exceeds μ. The four B neighbours had original A-edge value μ+4 and now have μ+4−s². The A–B value is μ+14−s², strictly larger than μ because s²<8. Consequently when s² differs from 4, A has zero μ ties after the source. Altering one further edge cannot bring its degree to four. This obstruction is independent of which grade the proposed receiver deletes.
4. At s²=4, positivity gives s=u=2. The archived all-editor search and the independent complete enumeration give exactly (12,2).
