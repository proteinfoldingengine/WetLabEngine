# One source deletion can immediately remove a specific receiver command

At a common preparation with exactly the original admitted 25-view geometry, one legal deletion at view 0 leaves view 12 as the sole holder of leaf 718. The original union-preserving atom then forbids view 12 to delete 718. An alternative one-unit deletion at view 0 leaves that very same receiver command legal. This distinction in the available actions exists immediately after the source event and before any receiver edit, recovery selection or post-source schedule.

The source's row of work changes identifies which chart lost a command. The code enforces the prohibition by retained membership and conservation of the union. It does not read a numerical work value to select or dispatch a command. Whether the actual receiver can observe this change in availability remains open.

## Which other chart loses a command?

Suppose a single legal event at $A$ deletes a **global tree leaf** $x$ of subtotal $s_x>0$. Put $\Delta_j=W_{Aj}^{\rm after}-W_{Aj}^{\rm before}$ for $j\ne A$. If $j$ holds $x$, the contribution to this distance changes from zero to $s_x^2$. If $j$ lacks $x$, it changes from $s_x^2$ to zero. Every other summand is fixed. Thus
\[
 H=\{j\ne A:\Delta_j=+s_x^2\}
\]
is exactly the set of remaining holders, and $H$ is nonempty by union preservation.

Every $j\in H$ was allowed to delete $x$ before the source event because $A$ also held it and $x$ is a leaf. After the event, that same deletion is allowed exactly when $H$ has another member. All nonsource chart memberships and their local leaf tests remain fixed. Coverage of every vertex other than $x$ is unchanged. Therefore the **complete set of newly forbidden commands outside the source** is
\[
 \begin{cases}
 \{(j,x)\} & H=\{j\},\\
 \varnothing & |H|\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]
There are no newly enabled nonsource deletions in these two cases. The hypothesis on a global leaf is sufficient and avoids assumptions about local children. One pair's magnitude or sign alone does not establish that its positive label is unique. The row comparison identifies the owner without giving that row to the owner as an instrument.

## Preserve the whole geometry while changing coverage

Use $A=0$, $B=12$, $q=718$ and $p=662$. These are distinct unit root leaves. Write $D=\{0,12\}$, $C=\{8,13\}$ and $O=V\setminus(C\cup D)$, which has 21 labels. Initially $q$ is held by $D\cup O$. Take the least unit root leaf $r$ initially missing precisely at $D$, raw leaf 46. Its holder set is $C\cup O$. The archived cut at $D$ has squared mass 33 and its greedy allocation includes unit leaves. Initially $p$ is missing at 7 and 12. The two source choices are the same at every preparation tested.

First, deleting $q$ at all 21 labels in $O$ leaves exactly $A$ and $B$ holding it. The direct work between these two unedited charts is still 1998. This preparation has mixed moment parity and fails the full carrier. To keep the original geometry, at each label in $O$ delete **both** $q$ and $r$. All 42 edits are legal since the respective survivor pairs always retain their leaf. The four charts in $C\cup D$ stay untouched.

For a support $S$, the contribution of a unit leaf to $W_{ij}$ is the cut indicator $1_{i\in S}\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}1_{j\in S}$. This is identical for $S$ and its complement. The two starting supports are the complements of $C$ and $D$. The two final supports are $D$ and $C$. Their equal-weight cut contributions therefore exchange places. Every other contribution stays fixed. **All 300 entries of the work table are identical to the original entries.** The unchanged full v15.43 constructor returns the identical admitted carrier, including the flat baseline.
