## Independent historical-terminal and union proof

Let Y0 and Y be actual root-containing prefix-closed views in the same address system, with Y subseteq Y0, and let nonroot v be a leaf of Y0. No strict descendant of v belongs to Y0, hence none belongs to Y. Consider Y'=Y minus {v}. Its root remains. For any x in Y' and any prefix p of x, prefix closure gives p in Y. If p were v, then x would be a strict descendant of v because x differs from v, contradicting the preceding fact. Thus every prefix of x lies in Y'. This proves prefix safety whether v is present or absent. In the absent case Y'=Y. A current presence query is therefore unnecessary for this total set command's safety; it remains necessary to assert that the command made a nontrivial deletion.

Now let the current indexed views cover T and suppose a strict descendant d of v belongs to this same current T. Since d is not in Y0, it is not in Y. Some other current view must contain d; its prefix closure places v in that view. Holding all other views fixed, deletion from Y can only remove v from the union, and that other view retains v. Hence the union remains T. The argument requires neither identification of that other view nor a fresh complete read of Y, once the historical certificate, contraction and current coverage premises are justified.

The limitations are essential and correctly stated. Allowing additions can turn the historical leaf v into an internal node, making isolated deletion invalid. A merely historical descendant does not establish current redundant coverage. Concurrent updates to other views invalidate this particular unchanged-other-view argument unless a separate collective guarantee applies. No hitting-band, floor, physical actuator, receiver access or record-persistence conclusion is implied by prefix and union safety alone.
