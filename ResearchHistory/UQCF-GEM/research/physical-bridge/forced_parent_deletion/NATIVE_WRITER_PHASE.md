# The same pruning certificate can precede a receiver write or silence

The inherited v16.52 and v16.53 producers actually edit the membership of indexed views. They carry controller information across edits. The question is whether their receiver response can be recovered from a v16.26 source event, the entire indexed before and after tree, and the original saved profile. These are the full original fields of an atom plus the normalizer's tree, ordered palette, and target. The v16.52 comparison also grants the receiver its sequence of own retained states up to the source event.

The examples use original campaign paths. A transition selected as the source is a genuine union-preserving single deletion and is independently checked with the unchanged v16.26 verifier. A possible future receiver write is taken from the actual suffix of that historical run. It is the first later change of that receiver's membership, regardless of how many other indices edit. Unit ambient grade and the original nearest-ancestor P1 aggregation give the record at the retained root. This mathematical read requires neither a clock nor a proposed physical measurement.

## Consequence and scope

The indicated full event and target cannot be a sufficient input to a deterministic rule for these actual receiver futures. The same-run case also supplies the identical original preparation. The two-run case supplies the identical receiver-local prefix, including all changes of its retained grade for the fixed ambient grade. The distinguishing information in the first case is the pending outer root lift. The second case follows two different saved role contexts and executable stages. A complete replay can use those contexts, which are present in the historical program's control state.

This is an exact obstruction to obtaining the historical response solely by identifying a native occurrence with the witnessed indexed tree atom. It leaves open a native rule that supplies an equivalent retained distinction, chooses a different response, or establishes access to the record. The software involves both additions and deletions. The source atom is verified in the original deletion grammar. The historical continuations use the broader one-incidence support rules of their respective normalizers. No physical reprise of the loop, invocation of the normalizer, or access to its transient record is derived.
