# One unchanged view constrains a different view's root amount

On the five-vertex deletion example, the existing source coordinate at the root of the A-indexed view has disjoint possible values after the two competing source events. For the original counting representation, the allowed amounts are **3 through 5 after A was removed**, and **0 through 2 after B was removed**. The result holds even if the data assigned to A can vary arbitrarily at each endpoint. We keep just one unaffected view's source marginal fixed and require that A and this view admit a common nonnegative source. The distinguished amount is the same coordinate in both cases.

## Exact ranges

Write the C marginal in order (o,p,w) as (b,u,v), with b,u,v nonnegative rationals. Put m=u+v and M=b+m. Thus m is the total amount in the p subtree seen by this witness and b is the amount outside it. Define the fixed reading a to be the root coordinate of A's own source vector.

Once A is removed at l, the only next deletion removes A at p. The retained A view is {o,d} and its intersection with C is the root alone. The old v16.23 gluing rule at the common root gives a joint root amount of a+b−M = a−m. Nonnegativity requires a≥m, and A's total M requires a≤M. Every rational a in this interval occurs. In the full vertex order (o,p,l,w,d), one joint source is

    (a−m, u, 0, v, M−a).

If B is removed at l, the next deletion removes B at p. A now contains {o,p,l,d}; its intersection with C is {o,p}. Projecting A and C to that intersection gives a+x_A(d)=b at o. Thus 0≤a≤b. Every such a is attained by the full nonnegative source

    (a, u, 0, v, b−a).

These constructions also show that imposing positive realizability on the whole tree and supplying the corresponding B marginal leaves the same ranges when B is otherwise free. The proof itself needs a joint nonnegative lift only for A and C. The upper bound in the B-source case follows directly from overlap agreement and nonnegative A data.

| First label removed | A view after the second deletion | Exact possible A-root amounts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | {o,d} | [m,M] |
| B | {o,p,l,d} | [0,b] |

The intervals mean rational points, including their endpoints. They are disjoint exactly when **m>b**, equivalently m>M/2. When disjoint, the smallest possible difference between the A-source and B-source readings is m−b. With positive total, division by M gives a normalized coordinate; the raw amount is extensive. If m≤b the shared value m is attainable in both branches, and at m=b their intervals touch. Total zero gives zero in both branches.

## Numerical application and controls

The original fiber push of (1,1,1,1,1) to C is (2,2,1), so M=5, m=3, b=2. All possible A-root amounts after removal of A lie in [3,5], and all after removal of B lie in [0,2]. **At least one unit of separation is forced**, even if the two endpoints use different admissible joint sources. The normalized minimum gap is 1/5. Supplying the unit grade on the entire tree gives 4 and 1, a single special pair inside those intervals. A's root amount on that common counting preparation is initially 1 and still 1 immediately after either first deletion. With arbitrary intermediate grades consistent with C, both pre-parent-edit ranges are [0,2]; particular intermediate values need not agree unless the preparation or retained data fix them. Nevertheless every admissible pre-parent reading is at most 2. On the A-source branch the second edit therefore forces a rise of at least one in this local coordinate even if its earlier value is free. The fixed property “more than half the total is at A’s root” was false for every allowed intermediate reading and becomes true precisely on the A branch.

1. **Equal weights.** Take C=(2,1,1). Here m=b=2. Full sources (0,1,0,1,2) after A and (2,1,0,1,0) after B both give the same A-root amount 2. The weaker class allows different full sources across the two endpoint histories. No coupling between them was assumed.
2. **Local positivity only.** In the A-source endpoint the local vectors A=(0,5), B=(2,2,1), C=(2,2,1), in increasing vertex order of each view, are individually nonnegative and compatible on all intersections. Their unique signed full-tree source is (−3,1,1,1,5). The original positive gluing routine rejects it. A-root equals zero, which also occurs in the B-source positive range for the same C. Thus the result fails if local matching is substituted for joint nonnegativity.
3. **Exact numerical scope.** Of the 126 nonnegative integer allocations of five units to this tree, the counting C marginal admits exactly nine. All nine can be written (s,t,2−t,1,2−s) with s,t in {0,1,2}. Two independently implemented archived projections agree on them, and the archived positive gluing operation recovers each full source from the complete family. Rational attainability in the theorem follows from the displayed constructions.
4. **Source of the second event.** The original v16.25 `refinements` returns exactly the unchanged post-source state and the forced-deletion endpoint for each branch. Its normal caller enumerates the choices. A different original routine really does select a path. For default order A,B,C and target profile (1,2), v16.50 `normalize` constructs its own endpoint.
