# A source-specific first receiver edit on the seven-site fork

A two-view preparation on the existing binary fork has a source-specific first write at C under the original v16.45 fork normalizer, freshly invoked with the measured post-event profile. Its sign survives both index orders and the choice of marked sibling on either branch. No extra branch-transfer controller is used. The outstanding premise is when normalization begins and how the receiver accesses its first write. The archived controller can pass through the very same post-event state already in flight and give the opposite sign.

## Common preparation and source operation

The root has left junction 1 with leaves 2,3 and right junction 4 with leaves 5,6. Source A retains all seven vertices. Receiver C retains both junctions and one marked leaf on each branch. These are two indices on the same topology as the preceding three-index experiment. Removing a view from that earlier preparation would not give this one. ## Proof for the next deletion

After the source event all four junction memberships still have at least one retained child. The source-side leaves and the unmarked opposite leaf have unique holders. The opposite marked leaf is the only duplicated leaf. Hence exactly two union-preserving deletions remain immediately possible. Deleting A's copy splits the opposite siblings between A and C, raising its local hitting number from one to two. Deleting C's copy leaves A covering both and preserves the entire profile. The original trigger and independent verifier certify both classifications. Thus a neutral next deletion necessarily happens at C, at the opposite mark. No exhaustion or completion is assumed. This argument applies to every sibling choice and either ordering of the two views.

The unrestricted control allows A to do the other source deletion. Both orders reach (55,91) with C unchanged throughout and every leaf singly held. No further bare deletion is possible there. Both legal next moves in each arm preserve the global maximum of two. A law preserving just that maximum also admits the silent path.

## Signed grade and actual writer

Put one unit of ambient extensive grade at each of the four leaves and zero elsewhere. Project to C with the original P1 operation. Define D to be the grade at C's right junction minus the grade at C's left junction. Both junctions already exist in C. Each starts with one unit from its missing sibling, so D is zero both before and immediately after the source event. Removing the right mark adds a unit at the right junction, giving D=+1. Removing the left mark gives D=−1. This is a difference between two coordinates within C's own view. The physical ability to compare them is open.

The checker then invokes the unmodified fork writer and measures the first edit of C's view. With prep (55,127) the first global edit is precisely the neutral deletion in each arm. Reversing the index order changes the edit after a left-source deletion: the first write adds the missing left sibling. That removes one unit from the left junction and gives the same D=+1. After a right-source deletion D=−1. The enumeration covers all four choices of marked siblings and both owner orders, with both source events, giving 16 distinct writer inputs. Re-expressing each case with the two directions interchanged gives 32 oriented cases. The first C write is at global edit one or two. The signed rule is covariant under a branch swap: swapping the named directions swaps both source side and sign. The necessary neutral deletion alone does not account for every actual writer trajectory.

## Historical phase and physical scope

The checker also inspects every occurrence of the tested inputs in the original two-view campaign paths. None of the 16 inputs was an original scheduled starting representative. None of their observed visits was entered directly from the matched preparation by the chosen source deletion. In the primary example, the archived paths from representatives 2 and 4 visit (55,123). Their next C mask is 119, giving D=−1. The path from representative 22 visits (55,95). Its next C mask is 63, giving D=+1. These signs reverse both fresh-start responses. Even within the freshly invoked runs of this example the terminal signed readings reverse the first-write signs. The result concerns the first receiver edit relative to a stated launch.

The live tree and global maximum alone do not establish which controller phase accompanies a physical event. An inherited invocation or equivalent boundary condition, together with receiver access, is still needed for the physical bridge. Neither a timestamp nor a gravity observable is an input to these finite checks.
