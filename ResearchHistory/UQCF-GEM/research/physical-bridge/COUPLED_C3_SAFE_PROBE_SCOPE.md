Question: Can an observer distinguish the INITIAL spectator occupancy of two hidden prepared states using an adaptive sequence of guaranteed-safe native spectator edits, when the only observed post-state information is core projections, core-only miss counts, tau and floor-validity?

Carrier: E(Z)=({a,b},{b,c},{a,c},{d,e} union Z), original floors2, tau3; spectator palette T finite and disjoint from core. Initial worlds Z0=empty and Z0={u}, with u in T. Controller sees the SAME core retained record in both and does not know which is actual. Controller may choose any spectator incidence toggle on r4 that is guaranteed syntactically valid in EVERY currently possible hidden world; every chosen command is guaranteed to commit exactly once and its sign, label and root are known. No failed probe, rejection response, full root size, or full-palette miss count is supplied. The observer may adapt to the observed transcript.

Required theorem:
1. Show core observations are invariant under every valid spectator-only toggle on r4: core supports and M_core unchanged, tau3 and floors2 unchanged.
2. Prove by induction that any adaptive policy selecting uniformly valid committed spectator toggles generates the SAME observed transcript in both initial worlds, as long as the policy continues. Therefore no such policy can identify the INITIAL occupancy bit b0=1[Z0 nonempty].
3. Note that current b_n may become equal across worlds after adding a common fresh spectator; this does NOT recover b0. Exact count difference q_n^{(B)}-q_n^{(A)}=1 persists for identical committed signs.
4. Show a distinguishing failed-command/legality response can break the obstruction: attempt -u when u is absent in one world and present in the other, but it is NOT uniformly valid and lies outside the safe-probe domain.
5. Explain that this is a conditional intervention-observability no-go, not a universal no-go for all native controls, and no native observer access or physical force follows.
