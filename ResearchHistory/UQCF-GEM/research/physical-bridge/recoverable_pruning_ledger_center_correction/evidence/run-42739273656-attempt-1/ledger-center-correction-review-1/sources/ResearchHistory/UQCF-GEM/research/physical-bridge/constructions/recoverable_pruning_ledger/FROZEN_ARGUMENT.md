## 13. Record closure (CORRECTED)

### Lemma 13.1 (straddle lemma) — unchanged; sound

For unital inclusion \(B\subseteq A\) of finite-dimensional *-algebras: a minimal central projection \(q\) of \(B\) **straddles** if \(qp\neq0\) for at least two minimal central projections \(p\) of \(A\).

1. \(Z(A)\cap B=Z(A)\cap Z(B)\).
2. \(Z(A)\subseteq B\) iff no minimal central of \(B\) straddles.
3. Persistent part \(K(A,B)=Z(A)\cap B\) is spanned by unstraddled sectors of \(A\).

**Corollary 13.2.** A merge that straddles sector \(p_t\) removes \(p_t\) from the retained algebra however much pinching occurs in the same step. Pinching cannot discharge the commutative retention premise for a merging owner.

### Retention selection and record closure

Let \(A_0\supseteq A_1\supseteq\cdots\) be any decreasing chain. A **retention selection** σ chooses at step \(j\) a set \(\sigma_j\) of minimal central projections of \(\widetilde A_{j-1}\). Define
\[
\widetilde A_0=A_0,\qquad K_j=\operatorname{span}\{Z(\widetilde A_{j-1})\,p:p\in\sigma_j\},\qquad
\widetilde A_j=A_j\vee K_j.
\]

### Theorem 13.3 (record closure) — CORRECTED

For every chain and every selection σ:

1. **Decreasing.** \(\widetilde A_j\subseteq\widetilde A_{j-1}\) and \(A_j\subseteq\widetilde A_j\). Closed global distinguishability \(\widetilde G_j\) is nonincreasing, and \(G_j\le\widetilde G_j\le G_0\).

2. **Kept records are central (one-step only).** \(K_j\subseteq Z(\widetilde A_j)\). With the **unitalization** \(K_j^+=\operatorname{span}\{1\}\vee K_j\),
   \[
   \widetilde A_j=\operatorname{span}\{ak:a\in A_j,\,k\in K_j^+\}
   \]
   equivalently \(\widetilde A_j=A_j+\operatorname{span}(A_j K_j)\). (Draft-6 displayed form without unitalization is false; GPT Finding 1 counterexample: \(\widetilde A_{j-1}=\mathbb C^2\), \(A_j=\mathbb C\cdot1\), \(\sigma_j=\{p=(1,0)\}\) yields RHS \(=\mathbb C p\neq\mathbb C^2\).) Every selected old sector is an unstraddled central sector of the **next** closed algebra. Record compatibility holds on selected corners **at that step**.

   **Not claimed for arbitrary σ:** multi-step nondecreasing receiver information. GPT Finding 2 counterexample: \(A_0=A_1=\mathbb C^2\), \(A_2=\mathbb C\cdot1\), select \(p\) at step 1 and nothing at step 2; central distinguishability on \(\{p,1-p\}\) falls from maximal to zero.

3. **Three canonical selections recover the reading lattice.** For the commutative tree \(A_j=B_j\):
   - \(\sigma=\emptyset\) ⇒ \(\widetilde A_j=B_j\) = **\(R_{\min}\)**.
   - \(\sigma=\) sectors owned by survivors ⇒ shell algebra = **\(R_{\mathrm{inc}}^-\)** (Thm 11.1, Lemma 11.4).
   - \(\sigma=\) all sectors ⇒ \(\widetilde A_j=B_0\) = **\(R_{\mathrm{inc}}^+=R_{\mathrm{full}}\)**.

4. **Pinching collapses the lattice.** For nested pinching, \(\widetilde A_j=A_j\) for every σ (Lemma 12.1).

5. **Loss arrow = unselected straddle.** \(\widetilde A_j\) strictly coarser than \(\widetilde A_{j-1}\) on Z iff some unselected sector is straddled. For survivor σ this is Prop 11.6(a)/(b).

### Proposition 13.4 (hereditary survivor contract) — NEW

A selection σ is **hereditary on retained owners** if: whenever owner \(t\) is retained from step \(j\) through step \(k>j\), every sector of \(t\) that was selected at step \(j\) remains selected (or is refined into selected descendant sectors) at every intermediate step.

**Survivor selection** \(\sigma_j=\{\text{minimal central sectors of }\widetilde A_{j-1}\text{ owned by atoms in }X_j\}\) is hereditary on retained owners: survivors are defined by (E1) stable addresses; ownership transport by (E2); discarded sectors are exactly those of removed atoms (Lemma 11.4).

**Consequence.** Under survivor selection, for every retained owner \(t\), \(R_t^{(j-1)}\) is a deterministic function of \(R_t^{(j)}\), so \(I(S;R_t^{(j)})\) is nondecreasing while \(t\) is retained — recovering Theorem 11.5(2). Theorem 12.2 item 2 applied along the nested selected corners of a hereditary selection likewise gives multi-step nondecreasing central information on those corners. Arbitrary σ needs no such claim.
