## 12. Exact common-center theorem (corrected historical Lemma 13.1)

Let B subset A be a unital inclusion of finite-dimensional complex C*-algebras, with minimal central projections p_i of A and q_j of B. An edge i~j means p_i q_j!=0. Products commute because p_i is central in A. A retained sector q_j straddles if it meets at least two old p_i.

1. Z(A) intersection B = Z(A) intersection Z(B). Indeed an element central in A commutes with B, so if it belongs to B it is central there; the reverse inclusion is immediate.
2. An individual p_i belongs to B iff no q_j meeting it straddles. If p_i belongs to B, item 1 makes it central there, hence a sum of q_j, and each q_j either lies under it or is orthogonal. Conversely, if every q_j meeting p_i meets no other p, then q_j=p_i q_j for those j and p_i=sum_j p_i q_j is a sum of those q_j in B.
3. Full compatibility Z(A) subset B holds iff no q_j straddles, by applying item 2 to every p_i.
4. Form the bipartite incidence graph with vertices p_i and q_j. For each connected component C set e_C=sum_{i in C}p_i. Then e_C=sum_{j in C}q_j and

   K(A,B)=Z(A) intersection B=span{e_C: C a connected component}.

Proof of item 4: since both families sum to 1, and all products linking different components vanish,
sum_{i in C}p_i=sum_{i in C,j}p_i q_j=sum_{i,j in C}p_i q_j=sum_{j in C}q_j.
Thus e_C lies in both centers. If z=sum_i a_i p_i=sum_j b_j q_j lies in the common center, multiplication by each nonzero p_i q_j gives (a_i-b_j)p_i q_j=0, hence a_i=b_j on that edge. These scalar coefficients are constant on each connected component. This proves spanning. Distinct e_C are nonzero orthogonal projections summing to 1, so they are independent. They are the minimal central projections of the common commutative algebra K.

The individual-sector criterion does not describe a basis of all K. For A=C^2 and B=C1, neither old minimal projection lies in B, but K=C1. On X=(o,p,u,v) with u merged into p, A=C^X and B=span{e_o,e_p+e_u,e_v}; K=B, not span{e_o,e_v}. These examples correct the historical span assertion, without refuting items 2 or 3.

Old-unit corollary (historical 13.2): if a retained q straddles an old unit p_t in the FINAL inclusion, then p_t is not in the retained algebra. Any refinement that restores p_t must separate along that old unit, removing the straddle touching it. This statement does not require losing sums that contain p_t, and does not assert that an individual old unit is necessary for one new accessible effect.

  Atilde_j=span{a k:a in A_j,k in K_j^+}=A_j+span(A_j K_j).

Indeed K_j is spanned by orthogonal central projections of the preceding algebra, so K_j^+ is a commutative unital algebra commuting with A_j. ### 13.2 Hereditary retention and its limits

Arbitrary selection assures only the one-step survival in (b). For A_0=A_1=C^2, A_2=C1, retaining p=(1,0) at step 1 and nothing at step 2 loses its distinguishing record. Multi-step monotonicity is not claimed without hereditary retention.

A selection is hereditary on a continuing owner if the earlier selected projections belonging to that owner remain retained at every subsequent step (possibly as sums of selected refinements), for as long as that owner persists. Define that owner's measured record algebra at step j to be the commutative algebra generated by all of its projections selected up to j and preserved by this contract. These measured record algebras are nested: each old generator is still present and central, while new selected projections add generators. State restriction and data processing therefore make distinguishability on these specified records nondecreasing. This does not claim that the owner's entire center is nested. For example, take a fixed full corner with A_0=A_1=C^3, A_2=span{e_1,e_2+e_3}, and sigma_1=sigma_2={e_1}. The selected e_1 survives hereditarily, but the whole center loses the distinction between e_2 and e_3. Full owner-record inclusion must be separately established if the whole center is the intended record.

For growing corners, raw weights support deterministic exact-record recovery and the subnormalized trace-norm comparison; when invoking general contractive distinguishability measures on normalized states, unitalize the measured record algebra with the ambient identity and an outside outcome. Do not renormalize separately by the changing owner's mass and infer monotonicity of every contractive divergence. For example, two states can have weight 0.2 on disjoint old internal outcomes (conditional trace-norm contrast 2), then each acquire the same new weight 0.8; the old raw weights persist but the newly normalized internal contrast is only 0.4. To speak of different owners across steps requires the specified owner assignment and measured-record inclusion; an arbitrary collection of selected sectors alone supplies no owner dynamics.
