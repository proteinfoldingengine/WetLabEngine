## 2. Candidate bridge question

For a native labelled state E with original floors and a prescribed exact labelled destination, can an explicitly defined certificate determine a legal next move and support repeated protected repair to that destination in the selected A11 class?

Split this into two distinct obligations.

### B1 — one-step sufficiency and input accounting

Specify the proposed input map before claiming sufficiency: which labelled W/C coordinates, root-floor information, receiving-root support information, candidate incidence status, and destination information are available?

R1 Section 5 already proves immediate legality through candidate-specific data:

    Add(i,x) is legal iff A_E(i,x) >= 2;
    Del(i,x) is legal iff D_E(i,x) >= 1 and |E_i|-1 >= f_i.

Computing those data uses the candidate shadow or surviving-cover intersections with the receiving root. W/C update identities likewise depend on the edited root. It is not enough to refer to those identities and silently assume all required input is available from aggregate W/C values.

A full syntax table identifying presence or absence for EVERY labelled root-label incidence reconstructs the full incidence state E. Supplying that table cannot, by itself, establish information compression. Conversely, the syntax bit for just one candidate does not specify its complete root support or floor slack. B1 must distinguish these cases rather than hiding the state in the phrase 'candidate syntax'.

The B1 deliverable is an exact input definition and a proof or obstruction to one-step sufficiency on that definition. Re-expressing the full state is permitted as bookkeeping but must not be reported as a new compressed-state theorem. No B1 result is claimed here.

### B2 — iterated repair sufficiency

After B1 and source selection, ask whether an existing A11 repair construction factors through the proposed current certificate, prescribed destination, and any explicitly declared memory or stage variable.

Immediate legality, updating the certificate after a chosen move, and selecting moves that terminate at the exact destination are separate obligations. A policy must remain within the selected class and protected band, preserve original floors, and carry a termination argument. Destination feasibility assumptions must be explicit. A hidden dependence on the full state or repair history is not certificate-only scheduling.

A12.2's fixed-order truncation obstruction and A12.5's restricted overlap-input obstruction do not prove full W/C scheduling insufficiency. Conversely, the existence of a full-state constructive repair does not prove that its choices factor through a smaller certificate.

Possible outcomes are a factorization theorem for the selected construction, or an indistinguishability obstruction for the precisely stated policy model. A matched-input pair must contradict the same required output or show that no permitted policy can succeed on all states sharing those inputs. Merely showing that two successful schedules start differently is not an impossibility proof: a common successful choice may still exist. Any difference in reachability must respect the frozen domain and destination-feasibility assumptions.
