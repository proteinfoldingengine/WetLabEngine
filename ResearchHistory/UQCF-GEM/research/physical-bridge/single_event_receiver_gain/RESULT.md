## 1. Positive consequence first

One actual receiver need not obtain the entire distinction exclusively in every carrier for a useful gain to occur. For a fixed P1 leaf event, the pruning ledger relates that receiver's gain to two pre-event responses to a unit placed at the receiver itself. If those responses are nonnegative, either one being positive forces a nonzero formal receiver contrast afterward.

This yields a narrower construction target than Draft 28's all-context exclusive transfer: identify an existing receiver record whose event ledger and two local responses are justified, then show that one response is nonzero and that the record is actually accessible. The argument does not derive those premises. In particular, mathematical distinctness of native grades does not imply that a proposed read map distinguishes them.

The field construction also supplies an exact access test. A post-event own-grade effect has pre-event pullback `h(x_p+x_l)`. The missing native bridge must justify its availability after the event and the complete receiver's blindness before it. This is one concrete effect and one actual event; it does not require full C3 or all-context faithful columns.

## 2. Single-event setting and gain identity

Let W be a finite rooted carrier, l a leaf, p its parent, and Y=W minus {l}. Work over the reals (the identities also hold over the rationals). The P1 map P fixes every retained coordinate other than p and sets

    (Px)_p=x_p+x_l.

Occurrence is accepted as an empirical input. Identifying that actual event with P1 remains a separate typed premise; no occurrence law or fundamental clock is introduced. Compare the same event and source/reference identities in both preparations.

Let F_W:R^W→R^W and F_Y:R^Y→R^Y be linear formal holding maps, with h_v^W(x)=(F_Wx)_v. Impose only this event's ledger relation

    F_Y P = P F_W.                                      (PN_e)

No all-carrier naturality, sibling exchange, cone locality, normalization, or positivity away from the entries named below is required for the theorem. Those properties may be independently available in a candidate application.

Let the declared source pair be x and x+g e_l, g≠0. Specify blindness of the receiver's linear holding at this actual carrier:

    h_p^W(e_l)=0.                                       (B_e)

Linearity makes this independent of background x. This is not yet blindness of the receiver's complete accessible record, and is not Draft 28's chain-comparison condition Bl_D^a.

### Proposition 1: the event's erased direction determines the gain decomposition

Set v=e_l−e_p. Under PN_e there is a scalar beta with

    F_W v=beta v.

Under B_e define t=h_l^W(e_p). Then

    beta=h_p^W(e_p),
    G:=h_p^Y(e_p)=h_l^W(e_l)=beta+t.                     (1)

The receiver's post-event contrast for the declared pair is exactly gG.

**Proof.** P is onto and ker P=span{e_l−e_p}. PN_e gives P F_Wv=F_YPv=0, hence F_Wv=beta v. At coordinate p this reads h_p^W(e_l)−h_p^W(e_p)=−beta, so B_e gives the first identity. At l it reads h_l^W(e_l)−h_l^W(e_p)=beta. Finally P e_l=e_p and the p-coordinate of PN_e gives h_p^Y(e_p)=h_p^W(e_l)+h_l^W(e_l)=h_l^W(e_l). Linearity gives the contrast. QED.

### Corollary 1: two positive entries suffice for a one-event gain criterion

Assume, in addition, only

    h_p^W(e_p)≥0,           h_l^W(e_p)≥0.                (POS_e)

Then beta,t≥0, G≥beta, and

    G>0 iff beta>0 or t>0;
    G=0 iff beta=t=0.                                   (2)

In particular, if F_W(e_l−e_p)≠0, then beta>0 and G>0. Conversely, no gain forces F_W(e_l−e_p)=0 and t=0. Annihilating the erased direction alone does not force no gain.

This sufficient nonzero-kernel-response condition is exactly a nonzero own-unit response h_p^W(e_p)≠0 under PN_e and B_e. It must not be portrayed as an independently earned faithfulness principle. It is sufficient, not necessary: the second branch t>0 may also give gain. If an independently sourced empty-leaf/locality condition supplies t=0, then G=beta and the nonzero-kernel-response condition is equivalent to gain.

No numerical lower bound independent of beta is claimed; no physical units or normalization are derived. A nonzero formal scalar contrast still requires an available effect that distinguishes it. Coarse effects may fail to separate two different scalar values.

## 3. Exact analytical controls

### A. Positive normalized all-prefix family: actual blindness is not chain blindness

Use the three-vertex fork W={p=0,l,c}, with l and c children of p. On every nonempty rooted prefix subcarrier Z, put

    d(Z)=c if c belongs to Z, and p otherwise;
    F_Z=s I_Z+(1−s)e_d(Z) 1_Z^T,       0≤s≤1.           (3)

Here 1_Z^Tx is the total grade. Every coefficient is nonnegative and every column sums to one. For any leaf deletion Q:Z→Z', Qe_d(Z)=e_d(Z') and 1_Z'^TQ=1_Z^T. Consequently F_Z'Q=QF_Z, including both orders of the pruning diamond and their composites. This is a full PN family on the four subcarriers, not merely a pair of unrelated matrices.

On the full fork,

    F_W e_l=s e_l+(1−s)e_c,
    F_W e_p=s e_p+(1−s)e_c.

Thus B_e holds, beta=G=s, t=0, and pruning l gives receiver share s and surviving side share 1−s. At s=0 the receiver remains blind before and after despite positive normalized holdings and the actual nonidentity P1 event. At 0<s<1 there is positive receiver gain, although the unit is not held exclusively at the source or receiver and Draft 28's ET fails for kappa=1.

On the chain {p,l}, instead,

    F_{p,l} e_l=(1−s)e_p+s e_l.

Therefore Bl_1^a holds only at s=1. Blindness at the actual fork cannot be silently substituted for blindness on the comparison chain. The three-vertex no-gain example is minimal among such positive normalized examples: with only p,l, the post-event carrier is {p}, so total normalization forces G=1.

### B. Nonzero kernel response is not necessary

On W={p,l}, in order p,l, take

    F_W=[[0,0],[1,1]],       F_Y=[1],       P=[1,1].

Then PN_e, B_e, positivity and column-total normalization hold. Both units are initially held at l. Here beta=0, t=1, and G=1. The erased relocation contrast is already annihilated by F_W, yet the declared source-presence contrast transfers to p. Distinguish these two contrasts.

### C. Each local sign restriction matters for the stated sufficient implication

On the same two vertices, take

    F_W=[[a,0],[−a,0]],       F_Y=[0],       a∈{−1,1}.

PN_e and B_e hold, beta=a≠0, and G=0. For a=1 only h_l^W(e_p) violates POS_e; for a=−1 only h_p^W(e_p) violates it. Thus a nonzero kernel response without the two stated sign restrictions need not produce gain. These signed controls are not asserted to have positive or unit-normalized total holdings.

## 5. Actual access: the retained field gives an explicit test

The accepted field construction uses cone totals c_v=Σ_{u≥v}x_u and a comparison-compatible response C with incident-edge coordinates

    y_v=phi_v−phi_parent(v)=a_depth(v)c_v.

The same depth coefficients apply on retained carriers. P1 preserves every retained cone total and every retained depth. Hence it preserves each retained y_v and, with phi_0=0, every retained potential:

    phi_Y(Px)=phi_W(x)|_Y.                              (4)

The proof sums unchanged edge values along the unchanged root-to-vertex path. C and P1 suffice for (4); root shift RS, a constant response coefficient, and nonzero coefficients are unnecessary for this restriction identity. This follows directly from the accepted response form and is consistent with §24.2's C-only preservation argument.

At p, the post-event incident edges are a subset of the pre-event incident edges; only the l edge disappears. Any function of the complete post-event local field is consequently a function of the complete pre-event local field. If the two preparations agree on the probability laws of all actually available pre-event local effects and that available set contains these post-effect pullbacks, they agree on the post-effect laws as well: integrate each common pulled-back effect against the two initial laws. The same argument applies to deterministic preparations.

Complete local-field access before the event is one sufficient condition for this inclusion. Locality alone is not: §§23–24 constrain permitted reads to be local but do not grant every local function. Restricted available algebras can change across the event and remain eligible for acquisition. The generic pullback argument is already familiar from retained_reader_write and center_access_growth; the application below identifies the particular function to trace here.

When the relevant response coefficients are nonzero, the post-event own grade has the following local pre-event representative for nonroot p:

    G_{p,l}(x)=c_p−Σ_{u∈ch_W(p),u≠l}c_u
             =x_p+x_l=x_p(Px).                          (5)

At Genesis replace c_p by the conserved total, if its independent availability is justified. Adding that unchanged datum to both complete local interfaces still gives the inclusion above.

For a fixed existing post-event binary grade effect h, the relevant pullback is h(G_{p,l}), not necessarily the entire number G_{p,l}. A newly accessible contrast requires that this effect was not already available before; complete initial receiver blindness and nonzero post-effect contrast must also be proved. Failure of algebra inclusion or mere unavailability of G_{p,l} is not sufficient. One may already access h(G_{p,l}) without accessing G_{p,l} itself.

### D. A genuine pruning with a blind divergence and a pre-informed complete local field

Take W={0→p→l}, g>0, nonzero a_1,a_2, and compare nonnegative equal-total grades x^0=g e_0 and x^1=g e_l. For x^0 the Genesis-balanced field is zero; for x^1 it is

    (phi_0,phi_p,phi_l)=(0,a_1g,(a_1+a_2)g).

Both initial own grades at p are zero, since the divergence c_p−c_l is zero. After pruning l, the retained field is unchanged and the own grade at p is 0 versus g. However, the incoming gauge-invariant edge value phi_p−phi_0 is already 0 versus a_1g before pruning. Thus a receiver with complete local-field access was already informed. The same field contrast occurs for the original one-location pair 0 versus g e_l, whose totals differ. Equal-total preparation is not required by the theorem and neither pair's native preparability is claimed here.

This is not a contradiction to the selected blind-read theorem. It shows why blindness of its cancelling scalar read cannot establish blindness of a receiver whose other available effects have not been specified. A native restricted record could still implement a real access change; naming its divergence is not a derivation that it does so.
