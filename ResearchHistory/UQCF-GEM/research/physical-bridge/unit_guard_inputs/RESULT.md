**Scoped analytical counterexample and source reconciliation.** The preceding receiver-command assessment gave a conditional two-command readout if a global one-unit deficit guard is physically enforced and its consequence is accessible. This note tests a concrete proposed origin of that guard: retain the current deficit, use the inherited atomic increment law, and update the budget from the named events and local acting-view data.

The proposal does not work on the declared inputs. The same truthful current budget, complete hitting profile, complete acting view, common carrier, edited support and parent support can require different guard decisions for the SAME universally carrier-safe deletion. ## 1. What the inherited one-unit statements actually assert

Two existing statements must not be conflated.

- The v16.26 increment proof shows that one same-union own-view leaf deletion changes only its parent's local hitting number and increases it by zero or one. It also gives the exact trigger: an increase occurs precisely when every old minimum cover is destroyed.
- The v16.53 native admissibility document separately states a total excursion bound D_q<=1 for its claimed normalization paths, where D_q is the sum of |h_x-q_x| over all internal vertices relative to one FIXED target profile q. The bare native primitive itself preserves nonempty nested supports and a fixed root; the profile budget is an additional path condition.

Thus a path staying within the band is a controlled mathematical construction. The cited results do not prove that every native event obeys an actual universal budget guard. The preceding receiver control was explicitly conditional on adding such enforcement; it did not derive enforcement from the atomic theorem. Treating the path budget as an established physical law would change its status.

From an exact starting profile, one atomic deletion automatically stays within total excursion one. Subsequent increments can accumulate. This already-published fact is reused here, not presented as another theorem.

The retained-count sources preserve a different positive result. Dynamic residual miss counts update exactly once the old field and active support are supplied. The ordered spectator-event result reconstructs a cardinality from a legitimate seed and complete actual directions because each such event changes that cardinality by exactly plus or minus one. Neither result states that every incidence deletion has a known deficit increment.

## 3. Two valid states with identical candidate inputs

Use P={a,b,c,d,e}. All addresses and order are fixed in the two states F and G. The binary tree is:

- o has children p,z;
- p has children v,w;
- v has leaves r_v,s_v;
- w has leaves r_w,s_w;
- z has leaves r_z,s_z.

There are five internal vertices (o,p,v,w,z). Take q=1 at all five.

| --- | --- | --- |
| o,p | P | P |
| v | {a,b} | {a,b} |

Every support is nonempty and every child support lies in its parent. Both carriers are exactly the same T. The complete acting view is

    Y_a(F) = Y_a(G) = {o,p,v,w}.

The acting index sees no global leaf. The complete input carrier K=T is also identical. The requested vertex v is a tip of Y_a and has a strict descendant r_v in K. The inherited positive-extension theorem therefore certifies -a(v) as universally carrier-preserving from these own-view/carrier inputs, in BOTH states. This is not a leaf deletion smuggled outside that information contract.

At o, p has all of P and z has {d,e}, so h_o=1. At p, the two children initially share a, so h_p=1 in both. At v and w each pair of leaves has an identical nonempty support, so h_v=h_w=1. At z the singleton children {d},{e} are disjoint, so h_z=2. Hence

    h(F) = h(G) = (1,1,1,1,2),        D_q(F) = D_q(G) = 1.

The edited support S_v={a,b}, its parent S_p=P, its two child supports {b}, the root support and the complete a-view all match. What differs is another index's cover of the sibling branch: b covers w in F but not G.

### Both starts are reachable from exact profiles through the same preparation commands

## 4. The same certified deletion has different costs

Execute the candidate mathematical deletion -a(v). Its own-view output is the SAME in both states:

    Z_a = {o,p,w}.

The removed incidence is covered by b and all supports remain nonempty and nested. It is one proper atomic deletion in the inherited positive-extension safety class.

This is not an all-NOOP objection. Both histories contain genuine edits, both counters are correct, the same proposed deletion is natively safe in both, and only the additional fixed-q budget distinguishes its admissibility. The absence of information is specific to the named guard task.

## 5. The missing fact and the valid conditional update

The inherited minimum-cover trigger explains the difference exactly. At p before the request, F has two singleton minimum covers, {a} and {b}; G has only {a}. Removing a from v destroys {a} in both. The surviving {b} cover in F keeps h_p fixed; no old minimum cover survives in G.

For this fixture, access to the single predicate b in S_w would settle the decision. This is a sufficient discriminator for the two states, not a claim that this particular bit is the unique or universally minimal guard representation. It is precisely an additional other-view membership fact absent from the candidate package.

Generally, for a carrier-safe deletion at v with parent p, write the inherited increment as delta in {0,1}. Once delta is legitimately known,

    D_q' = D_q + |h_p + delta - q_p| - |h_p - q_p|.

The complete current profile and q determine how to use delta; they do not in general determine delta itself. In the displayed pair h_p=q_p=1, so the new deficit is simply D_q+delta, with delta=0 versus one. Counting named deletions or knowing that delta is at most one cannot replace knowing whether the old minimum covers survive.

The dynamic residual theorem already supplies an exact update-closed field of miss counts for its declared target-four support domain, given correct initialization and active supports. This counterexample does not refute that theorem or assert that all guards require a full-state read. Its data package does not contain that field, and the target-four theorem is not silently generalized to this arbitrary tree budget. A future retained certificate can use a different sufficient representation, provided its initialization, update inputs and native availability are justified.

Likewise a complete actual-incidence ledger together with the full initial incidence state would reconstruct the states and their costs. Those full seeds differ between F and G. The pair does not refute such a reconstruction; it rejects treating a known exact profile, a common event-direction ledger and a truthful current scalar budget as equivalent to that much richer input.

## 6. Reference-reset and receipt controls

**Resetting q changes the question.** If, just before the tested deletion, q is replaced by the full current h, the current excursion is zero. The atomic increment bound then admits both prospective outputs: their excursions relative to the RESET reference are zero and one. This respects the one-step theorem but abandons the previously accumulated fixed-reference budget. It cannot implement the original guard, which allows the F output and excludes the G output. No fundamental time or clock is involved; this concerns the reference held fixed across ordered updates.

**A semantic cost receipt would add the missing information.** A record that supplies the true delta after an actual event can update a legitimate old counter by the formula above. A transcript containing just the primitive label, address and direction need not supply that semantic cost. Using a promised cost or legality receipt to explain how the guard initially computes that cost moves the open input into the receipt producer. System-side dependence on hidden incidences is permitted if independently derived; calling a verifier's output a native receipt does not derive it.

**A rejected or absent response need not report the exact guard value.** The inherited core-probe clarification permits an unchanged outcome even for a legal command. Thus observing no edit is not automatically evidence that the budget was exhausted. A committed edit that is known to obey a guard can have a sound one-sided implication, but the guard's applicability and retained observation of that edit must already be justified. The current result imposes neither deterministic outcomes nor guaranteed completion as a physical requirement.
