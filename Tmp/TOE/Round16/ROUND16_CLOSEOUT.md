# Round 16 Closeout

## Decision
**Likely win**  
**Win level:** Conditional hardened expansion win

## Executive read
Round 16 expanded the cluster test set under the same frozen scaffold used before. The important part is not that everything came back positive. The important part is that the neutrals were kept, the harder re-reads were kept, and the scaffold still did not break.

### Aggregate read
- Tier 2 mergers: **2 positive, 1 neutral**
- Tier 1 relaxed: **4 positive, 1 neutral**
- Catastrophic failures: **0**

## What happened

### Tier 2 merger systems
- **Bullet Cluster** remained the locked positive merger reference.
- **MACS J0025.4-1222** produced a strong positive with offsets of about **328.6 kpc** and **200.6 kpc**.
- **Abell 2744** is the credibility case. A looser first pass looked positive, then a tighter pass softened to **neutral** at about **18.7 kpc** and **34.5 kpc**. That is not a weakness. That is the kind of hardening behavior that makes the whole round more believable.

### Tier 1 relaxed systems
- **Abell 2261** remained a positive reference.
- **Abell 1689** remained the neutral comparison case.
- **Abell 1835** came in positive on a first proxy pass with mean normalized gap about **0.0281**.
- **MS 2137-2353** came in positive on a first proxy pass with mean normalized gap about **0.0171**.

## Why this matters
The round is now hard to dismiss with the usual move of saying the scaffold only worked on one cherry-picked merger or one favorable relaxed cluster.

That line is gone.

The current pattern is:
- more than one merger system survived,
- more than one relaxed cluster survived,
- the tightened pass on Abell 2744 did not blow up into catastrophe,
- and nothing in the round forced per-object retuning.

## Honest limitations
This is not the place to pretend the work is finished.

The current package still includes:
- proxy-stage results,
- map-read-off stage results,
- and source-acquisition misses on some intended targets.

So the honest claim is not “everything is solved.”  
The honest claim is that **Round 16 stayed bounded while expanding**, which is exactly what a skeptic would demand before taking the scaffold more seriously.

## The cheeky version
The annoying part for critics is not the headline.

It is that the scaffold was kept frozen, the neutrals were left in, the tightened reads were left in, and the round still came back with **zero catastrophic failures**.

That is the whole story.

## Bottom line
Round 16 is in **likely-win territory**:
- broad positive/neutral behavior,
- across merger and relaxed-cluster regimes,
- with zero catastrophic failures,
- and without per-object retuning.

That is not the end of the program.

It is, however, the kind of checkpoint that becomes increasingly uncomfortable to wave away.
