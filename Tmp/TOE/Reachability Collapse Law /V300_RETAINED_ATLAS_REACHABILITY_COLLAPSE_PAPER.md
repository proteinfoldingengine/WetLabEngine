**Status:** exploratory toy-model law report  
**Scope:** retained-atlas resilience dynamics, adaptive reachability, branch selection, intervention timing

## Abstract

We investigate a retained-atlas resilience toy model composed of local healing, poisoning, memory accumulation, interface resealing, connectivity regulation, and detox propagation dynamics. The system was not built by directly prescribing final outcomes such as fragmentation, runaway drift, or recovery. Instead, those outcomes emerged from the interaction of lower-level update rules.

Across stochastic runs, poisoning sweeps, topology variants, memory variants, and intervention tests, the toy repeatedly produced:

1. coexistence bands,
2. early branch selection,
3. basin lock,
4. late intervention failure,
5. early intervention superiority,
6. resealing saturation,
7. adaptive reachability collapse.

The strongest compressed law discovered so far is the **Normalized Adaptive Reachability Law**:

\[
A_{\text{norm}}(t) = \frac{A(t)}{A_{\text{baseline}}}
\]

Failure becomes likely when \(A_{\text{norm}}\) falls below a regime-relative threshold:

\[
A_{\text{norm}} < A_c
\]

In current toy runs, \(A_c\) lies approximately in the range:

\[
A_c \approx 0.53 - 0.58
\]

This law is stronger than raw poisoning, raw mobility, or raw pocket size because it measures the deeper condition: whether adaptive future trajectories remain reachable.

## 1. Background and Motivation

The retained-atlas toy began as a repair and routing system. It contained local update dynamics for healing, poisoning, mobility, resealing, connectivity, and drift detox. The initial goal was not to recover a known physical theory. The goal was to observe whether retained-memory dynamics could produce stable, measurable structure.

Early tests produced geometry-like observables:

- effective transport distance,
- least-action rerouting,
- holonomy-like mismatch,
- scar-memory persistence,
- interface permeability,
- hysteresis,
- resealing propagation,
- regional recovery waves.

As the toy became more complex, single-variable explanations failed. Raw poisoning did not fully explain outcomes. Raw scar magnitude did not fully explain outcomes. Resealing helped, but saturated. Connectivity restoration alone did not reliably recover bad-branch cases.

The key shift came when the toy was reinterpreted as a **future-state accessibility system**.

The deeper question became:

> Does the toy fail when damage is high, or when the accessible set of adaptive futures collapses?

The answer from V294–V299 was that adaptive reachability is the stronger organizing variable.

## 4. Branch Selection and Basin Commitment

The toy exhibited early self-reinforcing loops.

Example:

\[
pocket \uparrow
\rightarrow mobility \downarrow
\rightarrow detox \downarrow
\rightarrow retained\ poison \uparrow
\rightarrow pocket \uparrow
\]

Another loop:

\[
K \downarrow
\rightarrow recovery\ propagation \downarrow
\rightarrow local\ strain \uparrow
\rightarrow K \downarrow
\]

These loops produced early branch selection. Once the run entered a bad branch, later intervention could reduce severity but often could not fully return the run to adaptive recovery.

This motivated the distinction between:

- **pre-commitment intervention**: changes branch selection,
- **post-commitment intervention**: reduces severity but often cannot change basin identity.

## 5. Adaptive Reachability

Adaptive reachability measures the remaining accessible adaptive future trajectories.

It is approximated as a geometric mean of five components:

\[
A(t) =
\left(
R_f \cdot C_w \cdot B_e \cdot D_r \cdot R_v
\right)^{1/5}
\]

where:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| \(R_f\) | recovery-front speed |
| \(C_w\) | corridor width |
| \(B_e\) | branching entropy |
| \(D_r\) | detox radius |
| \(R_v\) | reversible-state fraction |

Each component measures a different aspect of whether recovery futures remain accessible.

### 5.1 Recovery-Front Speed

Recovery-front speed rises when \(C_{\text{health}}, R_{\text{seal}}, D_{\text{health}}\) are strong and falls when poison and pockets dominate.

### 5.2 Corridor Width

Corridor width measures how much usable transport/recovery channel remains.

### 5.3 Branching Entropy

Branching entropy measures how many adaptive route options remain available.

### 5.4 Detox Radius

Detox radius measures the propagation reach of detox and resealing dynamics.

### 5.5 Reversible-State Fraction

Reversible-state fraction measures whether current deformation remains recoverable rather than locked.

## 6. Normalized Adaptive Reachability Law

Raw \(A(t)\) thresholds shifted by topology and memory regime. Dense regimes, sparse regimes, noisy regimes, and long-memory regimes had different absolute scales.

The invariant appeared after normalization:

\[
A_{\text{norm}}(t) =
\frac{
A(t)
}{
A_{\text{baseline, regime}}
}
\]

The candidate law is:

\[
A_{\text{norm}}(t) < A_c
\Rightarrow
bad\ basin\ likely
\]

where current toy calibration gives:

\[
A_c \approx 0.53 - 0.58
\]

This means the invariant is not absolute reachability. The invariant is **relative loss of reachability compared with the regime baseline**.

microdynamics

## 8. Intervention Law

Reachability-triggered intervention was more portable than earlier hand-built warning scores.

Operational rule:

\[
if\ A_{\text{norm}} < 0.53 - 0.58:
\quad trigger\ full\ staged\ repair
\]

Staged repair sequence:

1. suppress pocket growth,
2. reseal interfaces,
3. restore connectivity/mobility.

Weak partial repair failed or harmed some runs. Full staged repair was required.

## 11. Conclusion

The retained-atlas toy supports a strong internal law:

> Failure occurs when adaptive reachability collapses below a regime-relative threshold.

Normalized adaptive reachability explains why:

- early intervention works,
- late intervention fails,
- resealing saturates,
- topology shifts raw thresholds,
- the same poisoning level can produce different outcomes,
- bad outcomes are path-selected early.

The current best law stack is:

1. \(A_{\text{norm}}\): adaptive future accessibility,
2. branch selection: basin commitment,
3. \(C_{\text{health}}\): local healing,
4. \(H_{\text{health}}\): connectivity homeostasis,
5. \(R_{\text{seal}}\) and \(P_{\text{recover}}\): interface and regional recovery,
6. \(D_{\text{health}}\): long-memory drift detox.

The central insight is that the toy is not merely a poisoning/healing system. It is a **geometry-of-reachability system**.
