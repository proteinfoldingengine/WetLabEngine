# Formal Draft Abstract

We investigate whether recoverability under pruning in finite graph-state systems requires retained source-origin information. Earlier observable-based candidates—transport, lineage retention, continuity, H-like scalar summaries, and geometry-like summaries—failed closure under leakage, identity-neutral, or frozen target controls. We therefore reformulated the closure question as a theorem-level implication: if a reconstruction map recovers source-origin assignment after transformation and pruning with error below ε, then the retained compressed observation must contain source-origin information above a Fano-style minimum threshold. Using a certified reconstruction operator and frozen information measure, independent synthetic hardening across graph families, source alphabet sizes, pruning levels, and reconstruction operators produced 5,040 trials, 708 successful reconstructions, and zero falsifications. These results do not prove a universal law, nor do they imply physical spacetime or GR. They support the narrower claim that, in the tested finite-graph recoverability harness, successful reconstruction after pruning required retained source-origin information.
