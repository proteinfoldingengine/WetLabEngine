# Challenge 13 Conditional Win Post Draft

We are calling **Challenge 13 a conditional win**.

And because this is moon-shot work, we are not going to be timid about what the evidence says.

Challenge 13 was designed to pressure-test a frozen gas-first framework under harder conditions:
- first on the locked set
- then with a rare stellar exception overlay
- then on a fresh unseen holdout
- then with a direct sanity audit on the biggest gains

The result:

- **13A frozen locked set**
  - mean improvement: **6.557220**
  - positive rate: **0.70**

- **13Q.5 frozen working baseline**
  - mean improvement: **6.697876**
  - positive rate: **0.70**
  - one rare exception

- **13Q.10 unseen holdout**
  - mean improvement: **50.251108**
  - positive rate: **1.00**
  - exceptions triggered: **0**
  - catastrophic failures: **0**

Then we audited the top-gain galaxies directly.

That audit passed.

So the story at closeout is not:
“the stellar branch took over.”

The story is:
- the **gas-first baseline remained the dominant rule**
- the stellar overlay stayed bounded as a **rare exception mechanism**
- and on the new unseen holdout, the frozen baseline generalized **without needing the exception at all**

That matters.

A lot of work looks exciting while it is still being tuned.
Much less work still looks strong after you freeze the model and make it face unseen data.

This one did.

So our conclusion is straightforward:

> **Challenge 13 is closed as a conditional win.**

Conditional only because the broader external file available locally turned out to be a summary table rather than a true radial-point external corpus, so it was not structurally scoreable under the frozen framework.

That is a data-shape limitation, not a model failure.

For a moon-shot program, a frozen baseline that:
- generalizes to unseen data,
- improves every holdout galaxy,
- triggers zero catastrophic failures,
- and survives a top-gain audit

is a real success signal.

We are going to treat it that way.
