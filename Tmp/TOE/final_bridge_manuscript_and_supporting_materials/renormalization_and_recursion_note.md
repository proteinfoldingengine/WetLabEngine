## 3. Ladder-recursion result

The lower weak ladder was already supported by nested thresholding on a normalized retained-envelope variable.

The stronger question is whether the ladder thresholds themselves obey a compact recursion law across contexts.

To test this, lower-ladder thresholds were fit across multiple:

- scale settings
- noise settings
- local gain settings

Two candidate recursion families were compared:

### Affine recursion
\[
\Theta_2 \approx a \Theta_1 + b
\]

### Multiplicative recursion
\[
\Theta_2 \approx \mu \Theta_1
\]

### Current fit result
Both families remain plausible on current data, but the **affine recursion** fit currently performs better than the multiplicative fit.

This does not yet settle the recursion law decisively.
But it does shift the current evidence in favor of an affine threshold family.

### Current recursion conclusion
The strongest current recursion conclusion is:

> the lower weak ladder appears recursively organized, and on current contexts the threshold relation is better described by an affine recursion than by a pure multiplicative law.

That is the strongest current statement supported by the fit.
