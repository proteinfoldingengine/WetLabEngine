# Bridge Publication Posture — Performance First

## Core framing

This version presents the bridge in its strongest predictive and structural form.

The emphasis is on:
- highest-performing hierarchical closure
- strongest local frontier refiners
- strongest seam calibration
- strongest transfer and stress results

## Key message

The bridge should be presented as a **hierarchical retained-state law** whose strength comes from:
- a global hierarchical selector
- unified local frontier laws
- calibrated seam geometry
- strong transfer
- robust lower-ladder recursion
- and a sparse crossover transition pocket

## Why choose this posture

Choose this posture if the priority is:
- strongest metrics
- strongest closure story
- strongest empirical support
- least vulnerability to claims of underfitting or oversimplification

## Tradeoff

This posture is less elegant than the most compressed possible formulation.
It accepts some modular structure in exchange for stronger performance and stronger calibration.

## Best lead sentence

The retained-state bridge is best understood as a hierarchical regime law with compact local frontier models, strong transfer, thin calibrated seams, and affine recursive lower-ladder structure.

## Best publication use

- memorandum
- technical paper
- intermediate-results publication
- packet for reviewers who care most about evidence density and robustness
