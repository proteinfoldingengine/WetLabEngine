# Bridge Executive Summary — One Page

## What this work is

This project tests whether synthetic regime structure can be generated from a **retained-state bridge** built from normalized residual-envelope coordinates, rather than treated as a collection of unrelated classifier labels.

The central question is whether a compact retained-state law can explain:
- regime selection
- local transition seams
- transfer across contexts
- and weak-ladder organization

## Strongest current result

The bridge now supports a **strong intermediate closure result**.

The system is no longer best described as a flat classifier.
It is better described as a **hierarchical retained-state law** with:

1. a **global hierarchical selector**
2. a **unified weak-ladder frontier law**
3. a **unified boundary/crossover frontier law**
4. **thin calibrated seams**
5. **strong family and perturbation transfer**
6. **affine recursive organization of the lower weak ladder**

## What is now strongly supported

### Hierarchical selector closure
A flat selector was too weak.
A hierarchical selector produced the strongest current closure.

### Transfer
The bridge transfers strongly across:
- held-out families
- moderate perturbations

### Weak-ladder emergence
The lower weak ladder emerges from thresholding of a normalized retained-envelope variable.

### Recursion family
Expanded cross-context testing strongly favors:
- **affine threshold recursion**
over
- purely multiplicative recursion

### Unified frontier laws
The bridge now supports compact scale-aware laws for both major frontier systems:

- weak ladder frontier:
  - eta geometry + log-scale

- boundary/crossover frontier:
  - u/v/eta geometry + log-scale

### Crossover interpretation
The crossover is not a broad bulk regime.
It is best interpreted as a **sparse local transition pocket**.

### Seam calibration
The seams are now quantitatively supported as:
- real
- thin
- localized
- limited in perturbative persistence
