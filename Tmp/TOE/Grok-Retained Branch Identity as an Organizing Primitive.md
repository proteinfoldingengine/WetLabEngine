# Retained Branch Identity as an Organizing Primitive for Recoverable Causal Geometry in Discrete Branching Systems

**A bounded scientific memorandum** summarizing the progression from PathLock and retained-path dynamics to lineage-addressable causal geometry experiments in discrete branching systems.

## Abstract

This memorandum summarizes a sequence of exploratory computational experiments derived from prior UQCF-GEM, PathLock, Dynamic Elephant, retained-path, and branching-causal-structure investigations. The work no longer attempts direct GR closure or TOE claims. Instead, the focus has shifted toward identifying a possible first-principles organizational primitive: **retained branch identity**.

Across multiple toy-model experiments, the strongest and most reproducible pattern observed is that systems preserving lineage addressability and branch identity under irreversible branching exhibit more stable causal accessibility geometry than systems based on memoryless diffusion, random smoothing, or over-merged histories.

The experiments suggest that geometry-like structure may emerge not primarily from raw vector state, but from conserved lineage relations and retained branch accessibility. The work remains exploratory and toy-model based. No claim is made that the results reproduce General Relativity, Einstein equations, ADM closure, or external empirical physics.

## Key Terms

- **Branch identity**: The persistent label or reference that distinguishes one retained lineage from others across branching events.
- **Lineage addressability**: The ability to uniquely resolve and address a specific retained branch’s history and relations, even after raw vector damage or relabeling.
- **Retained coherence**: The continued preservation of internal consistency and relational structure within a branch through irreversible changes.
- **Causal accessibility geometry**: The emergent structure of “who can affect whom” that arises from conserved lineage relations (distinct from raw coordinate or vector geometry).

## Experimental Progression

The experiments progressed through several major stages.

1. **Branching Geometry Survival**  
   Branching-cascade geometries consistently retained stronger locality-sensitive structure than generic geometries during out-of-slice audits.
2. **Cone-Biased Propagation**  
   Retained branch identity generated propagation behavior more aligned with emergent causal cones than random diffusion baselines.
3. **Failure Atlas**  
   A systematic failure atlas was constructed, including over-merge failure, high-noise failure, mixed-damage failure, observer inconsistency, and geometry collapse.
4. **Over-Merge Reversal**  
   The strongest insight emerged when over-merged branch histories caused geometry collapse, but lineage protection restored recoverability.
5. **Noise Robustness**  
   Noise alone did not destroy geometry if branch identity remained redundantly protected.
6. **Identity-Map Conservation**  
   The strongest result occurred when geometry recovered after severe raw-state damage *only if* the lineage address map was preserved.

## Irreversibility and Temporal Ordering

Another major conceptual development involved irreversibility.

The experiments suggested that repair fails not because vectors become noisy, but because lineage identity becomes unrecoverable once addressability collapses.

This connects to a broader UQCF-GEM-inspired interpretation: temporal ordering may emerge from irreversible branch stabilization and entropy bookkeeping rather than existing as a primitive background coordinate.

The current toy systems cannot establish this physically. However, the failure boundaries support the narrower computational statement: irreversible lineage collapse creates path dependence and hysteresis in recoverable causal organization.
