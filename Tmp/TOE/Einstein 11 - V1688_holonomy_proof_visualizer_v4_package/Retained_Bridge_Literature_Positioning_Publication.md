## 3. Why Reversible Full-Matrix Holonomy Is Not the Primitive

In a standard differential-geometric setting, holonomy compares a vector after parallel transport around a loop. Such a framework assumes tangent spaces, local invertibility, and a geometry that supports reversible comparison.

The retained recombination model starts elsewhere:

```text
p → q is not neutral motion through a pre-existing space.
p → q is a retained recombination event.
q → p is not the inverse of p → q.
```

Therefore, a reversible condition such as:

```text
T_qp T_pq ≈ I
```

is not expected at the primitive layer. In fact, clean microscopic reversibility would undermine the model’s premise that pruning/recombination history is retained and directed.

The successful observable is therefore not:

```text
full reversible matrix holonomy
```

but:

```text
native directional holonomy of the retained correction mode
```

This is the core result the literature scaffold helps contextualize.

## 5. Geometry as the Exhaust of Information

### 5.1 Bekenstein: Entropy and Geometry

Bekenstein’s 1973 black-hole entropy paper established one of the foundational links between geometric area and entropy: black-hole entropy is proportional to horizon area in Planck units. This does not imply the Retained Bridge model, but it provides a canonical precedent for treating geometry and information as deeply coupled.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** The model’s weak-form residual and correction terms are not arbitrary numerical constructs; they belong to a long tradition in which geometric quantities can encode thermodynamic/informational structure.

Reference: Jacob D. Bekenstein, “Black Holes and Entropy,” *Physical Review D* 7, 2333 (1973).  
Link: https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevD.7.2333

### 5.2 Jacobson: Einstein Equation as Equation of State

Jacobson derived the Einstein equation from horizon entropy proportionality and the thermodynamic relation:

```text
δQ = T dS
```

He explicitly framed the Einstein equation as an equation of state, born in a thermodynamic limit.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This is one of the strongest references for the idea that gravitational field equations can be macroscopic thermodynamic constraints rather than microscopic primitives. The Retained Bridge’s weak-form law is not Jacobson’s derivation, but it is conceptually aligned with the idea that geometry-like constraint equations may emerge from underlying thermodynamic/informational structure.

Reference: Ted Jacobson, “Thermodynamics of Spacetime: The Einstein Equation of State,” *Physical Review Letters* 75, 1260 (1995).  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9504004

### 5.3 Verlinde: Gravity as Entropic Force

Verlinde argued that gravity can be understood as an entropic force associated with changes in information and holographic screens. His work does not contain a retained recombination ledger, but it supports the broader hypothesis that gravity-like behavior may arise from informational/thermodynamic principles.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** The Retained Bridge may be positioned as a concrete finite-harness mechanism in the broad family of entropic/emergent gravity approaches, while avoiding the overstatement that it is a derivation of Verlinde’s model.

Reference: Erik Verlinde, “On the Origin of Gravity and the Laws of Newton,” *Journal of High Energy Physics* 2011.  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0785

### 5.4 Analogue and Emergent Gravity

Liberati, Girelli, and Sindoni discuss analogue and emergent gravity scenarios where effective spacetime or gravitational dynamics arise from non-gravitational substrate systems.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This supports the general methodological stance that geometry-like behavior can emerge from substrate dynamics. The Retained Bridge substrate is not a Bose–Einstein condensate or condensed-matter analogue; it is a retained recombination ledger.

Reference: Stefano Liberati, Florian Girelli, Lorenzo Sindoni, “Analogue Models for Emergent Gravity.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.3834

## 6. Entanglement, Tensor Networks, and Geometry

### 6.1 Ryu–Takayanagi: Entanglement Entropy as Area

The Ryu–Takayanagi proposal relates boundary entanglement entropy in AdS/CFT to the area of minimal surfaces in the bulk geometry.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This is a foundational result showing that entanglement structure and geometry can be mathematically linked. The Retained Bridge does not use AdS/CFT minimal surfaces, but it similarly treats structured information as capable of producing geometry-like observables.

Reference: Shinsei Ryu and Tadashi Takayanagi, “Holographic Derivation of Entanglement Entropy from AdS/CFT.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0603001

### 6.2 Swingle: Entanglement Renormalization and Holography

Swingle connected MERA tensor networks and entanglement renormalization with emergent holographic geometry. The key idea is that organizing information across scales can define a higher-dimensional geometric structure.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** The L3/L4 retained topology can be positioned as a dynamic recombination analogue of the broader idea that information organization can carry geometric content. However, the Retained Bridge is not a MERA model and should not claim that H4_perp is identical to tensor-network entanglement curvature.

Reference: Brian Swingle, “Entanglement Renormalization and Holography.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.1317

### 6.3 Van Raamsdonk: Building Spacetime from Entanglement

Van Raamsdonk argued that connected classical spacetime is intimately related to quantum entanglement, and that disentangling degrees of freedom can cause spacetime regions to pull apart.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This supports the conceptual bridge from relational information to connectedness/geometry. The Retained Bridge offers a directed, recombination-ledger mechanism rather than a holographic entanglement construction.

Reference: Mark Van Raamsdonk, “Building up spacetime with quantum entanglement.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.3035

### 6.4 Maldacena–Susskind: ER=EPR

Maldacena and Susskind proposed that entangled black holes can be interpreted as connected by Einstein–Rosen bridges, motivating the ER=EPR conjecture.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This supports the idea that entanglement and connectivity/geometry may be deeply related. The Retained Bridge does not claim wormholes or ER=EPR; it uses this literature only as conceptual support for information-connectivity geometry links.

Reference: Juan Maldacena and Leonard Susskind, “Cool horizons for entangled black holes.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.0533

## 7. Directed Discrete Order and Pre-Geometric Histories

### 7.1 Sorkin: Causal Sets

Causal set theory proposes that spacetime may be fundamentally discrete and ordered, with continuum spacetime emerging as an approximation to a causal partial order.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** The retained provenance ledger is not a causal set in Sorkin’s technical sense, but it shares the idea that directed order may precede continuum geometry. The Retained Bridge’s directed cycle and non-invertible recombination transport are compatible with this general worldview.

Reference: Rafael D. Sorkin, “Causal Sets: Discrete Gravity.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0309009

### 7.2 Markopoulou: Quantum Causal Histories

Markopoulou defined quantum causal histories as causal sets with Hilbert spaces attached to events and evolution operators attached to causal relations.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This is one of the closest conceptual neighbors. The Retained Bridge similarly attaches local algebraic structure to nodes and directed transformations to edges, although its local algebras are retained recombination-generated rather than Hilbert-space event histories.

Reference: Fotini Markopoulou, “Quantum causal histories.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9904009

### 7.3 Hawkins–Markopoulou–Sahlmann: Evolution in Quantum Causal Histories

This work gives a precise definition of quantum causal histories as discrete, locally finite, causal pre-spacetimes with matrix algebras at events and completely positive maps between causally related algebras.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** The analogy to local generated algebras and edge maps is strong. The Retained Bridge’s native transport is not a completely positive map formalism, but both frameworks use local algebraic event structure and directed evolution/transition maps.

Reference: Eli Hawkins, Fotini Markopoulou, Hanno Sahlmann, “Evolution in Quantum Causal Histories.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0302111

## 8. Non-Associative Geometry and Obstruction Structure

### 8.1 Baez: The Octonions

Baez’s review explains the octonions, the largest normed division algebra, emphasizing their non-associativity and broad mathematical connections.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This supports the legitimacy of non-associativity as a serious mathematical structure. It does not support emergent gravity directly, but it helps frame O3/L3 associator obstruction as mathematically meaningful rather than pathological.

Reference: John C. Baez, “The Octonions.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0105155

### 8.2 Blumenhagen–Plauschinn: Nonassociative Gravity in String Theory?

Blumenhagen and Plauschinn found indications of a three-bracket structure for target-space coordinates in closed-string backgrounds with flux, suggesting a possible noncommutative/nonassociative gravity theory.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This is one of the closest analogues to the O3 obstruction. The Retained Bridge’s O3 is not a string-theory three-bracket, but both represent a failure of associative composition that can carry geometric significance.

Reference: Ralph Blumenhagen and Erik Plauschinn, “Nonassociative Gravity in String Theory?”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.1263

### 8.3 Mylonas–Schupp–Szabo: Nonassociative Geometry and Twist Deformations

Mylonas, Schupp, and Szabo developed nonassociative deformations of geometry in non-geometric string theory, connecting nonassociative star products, twist deformations, quasi-Hopf structures, and 3-cocycles.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This literature supports the idea that nonassociative obstructions can be connected to deformed geometry. The Retained Bridge measures such obstructions in a finite retained recombination ledger, not in non-geometric string compactifications.

Reference: Dionysios Mylonas, Peter Schupp, Richard J. Szabo, “Nonassociative geometry and twist deformations in non-geometric string theory.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.7306

Reference: Dionysios Mylonas, Peter Schupp, Richard J. Szabo, “Non-Geometric Fluxes, Quasi-Hopf Twist Deformations and Nonassociative Quantum Mechanics.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.1621

### 8.4 Szabo: Higher Quantum Geometry and Non-Geometric String Theory

Szabo’s overview discusses nonassociative deformations of geometry, triproducts, tribrackets, and higher geometry in non-geometric string theory.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This provides review-level context for why higher/nonassociative geometry is relevant to attempts to understand pre-geometric or deformed geometric structure.

## 9. Network Thermodynamics, Cycle Currents, and Irreversibility

### 9.1 Schnakenberg: Network Thermodynamics

Schnakenberg developed a general network theory for systems governed by master equations, including cycle currents and thermodynamic behavior on graphs.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This is highly relevant to retained-order incidence continuity. The Retained Bridge’s antisymmetric edge current,

```text
J_pq = 1/2 · (F_pq − F_qp)
```

and node residual,

```text
continuity(p) = incoming J − outgoing J
```

are naturally described in graph-current language.

Reference: J. Schnakenberg, “Network theory of microscopic and macroscopic behavior of master equation systems,” *Reviews of Modern Physics* 48, 571 (1976).  
Link: https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/RevModPhys.48.571

### 9.2 Seifert: Stochastic Thermodynamics Along Trajectories

Seifert reviewed stochastic thermodynamics, extending thermodynamic quantities like work, heat, and entropy production to individual nonequilibrium trajectories and discussing cycle decompositions of entropy production.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This supports the use of directed paths/cycles as thermodynamic objects. The Retained Bridge’s pruning-order/recombination ledger is not a standard stochastic thermodynamics trajectory ensemble, but the analogy is strong.

Reference: Udo Seifert, “Stochastic thermodynamics, fluctuation theorems, and molecular machines.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.4176

### 9.3 Crooks: Fluctuation Theorem and Forward/Reverse Processes

Crooks derived a generalized fluctuation theorem relating entropy production and nonequilibrium work for systems with stochastic, microscopically reversible dynamics.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** Crooks is a useful contrast point. The Retained Bridge does not assume microscopic reversibility at the primitive retained-recombination layer. However, Crooks supports the broader scientific importance of comparing forward and reverse trajectory structure in nonequilibrium systems.

Reference: Gavin E. Crooks, “The Entropy Production Fluctuation Theorem and the Nonequilibrium Work Relation for Free Energy Differences.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9901352

### 9.4 Esposito–Van den Broeck: Broken Detailed Balance

Esposito and Van den Broeck formulated stochastic thermodynamics for systems with nonequilibrium constraints and broken detailed balance at steady state.

**Support for Retained Bridge:** This supports the idea that directed cycle currents and continuity structures can remain lawful even when reversible detailed balance fails.

Reference: Massimiliano Esposito and Christian Van den Broeck, “The Three Faces of the Second Law: I. Master Equation Formulation.”  
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.1683
